There are both important similarities and differences between the conquest of Mexico and the landing of Europeans in North America . The Spanish and the English became the dominant powers in Mexico and North America respectively and would therefore determine the fate of the Indians in Mexico and North America in the coming years . There was an enormous difference in the speed in which the natives of North America were subdued compared to the Aztecs in Mexico . The Spanish first conquered the Aztecs -LRB- which took only two years -RRB- and then began to settle their people ; the Europeans who entered North America first settled part of the land and then instigated the conquest of the continent as they progressed westward over two hundred years . Consequently contemporaries would view the two processes in a different light ; Cortez 's expedition into Mexico is nearly always called by chroniclers and historians `` The Conquest of Mexico . '' This reflects the fact that the Spanish conquest of Mexico was a definitive defeat of the most influential native power in America at the time . In comparison historians describe the arrival of Europeans in North America as a settling or planting because it was a steady but ongoing process that did n't initially involve the subduing of any significant society comparable with the Aztecs . There are a number of factors which influenced the interaction between the Europeans and the Native Americans . The most important issue to consider is what the Europeans wanted and expected from America and the reasons they left their homelands for the `` New World . '' Their reaction to what they found in America would influence further European actions there . The attitudes and opinions of the Europeans towards the Native Americans and the attitudes and opinions of the Native Americans towards the Europeans were also significant in determining their interactions . A number of alliances were formed between the Europeans and the natives and these also influenced the course of events . Whilst the European powers in both North America and Mexico had distinct advantages over the native populations , such as superior weaponry , the natives themselves had different advantages such as knowledge of the landscape and initial superiority in numbers . Some investigation is required to determine the balance of these strengths and how they would influence the outcome of the conflicts between natives and Europeans . It was the aims of the Europeans , modified by what they found in America that would determine their actions in the New World . The desire for gold would be the primary drive for the Spanish conquest of Mexico , whereas other nations became interested primarily in trade and settling after they found no gold in North America . Hernan Cortez , the leader of the Spanish conquistadores in Mexico , intended to collect more gold than the first two Spanish voyages to the Yucatan made in the 1510s . Yet Cortez 's force was not equipped for a march into the centre of Mexico . Cortez drew much of his assessment of the Aztec 's defensive capabilities from the first two voyages to the Yucatan made by Juan de Grijalva and Francisco Hernandez de Cordoba . These expeditions found only traces of gold and merely succeeded in angering the native Maya population . However , when Cortez landed in the Yucatan , the Maya did not produce sufficient resistance to dishearten him . Some of Cortez 's men had been on one of the first two voyages and so had first hand experience . Cortez 's force of about six-hundred , several horses , cannon , guns and armour landed in the Yucatan in 1519 . When Cortez met the Aztec royal ambassador he exclaimed , ` We Spaniards have a disease of the heart that only gold can cure . ' The French became interested in the New World after French pirates intercepted Spanish treasure ships bringing gold back from the Aztec empire in Mexico . Additionally , the Frenchmen Verrazano and Cartier began to explore the North American coast and the St Lawrence during the late sixteenth and early seventeenth centuries . Although the French were initially enticed to America by the prospect of finding gold , they found few traces of gold in North America at that time . Instead they found Indian populations who would trade beaver furs -LRB- a luxury in Europe -RRB- for European goods . The very first English colonies established by Sir Walter Raleigh in 1585 and 1587 were made to challenge the domination Spain had in the Americas . However , these had disappeared when John White , an English cartographer returned to re-supply them . Many historians have speculated as to the fate of the colonists , but one of the most prominent theories is that they were wiped out by hostile Indians in the area . The more successful English settlement of Virginia -LRB- from 1607 -RRB- was threatened by local tribes . Crucially , the English settlers of Virginia were often given unrealistic expectations by those who encouraged them to go and believed the New World to be a paradise . The Dutch also visited America , following in the footsteps of other European nations . These expeditions , led first by Henry Hudson in 1609 -LRB- an Englishman in the service of the Dutch East Indian Company -RRB- , were smaller in size than those of the English and French . Hudson sailed up the river later named after him and met Indians who traded tobacco for knives and beads . Trade became the main reason the Dutch would now attempt to colonize America . Land became the most important English acquisition in America . The English came to colonize the coastal region to be used as farmland and the maintenance of livestock . When the Spanish found gold in America , they increased their efforts in the New World to find more ; when the English found ` free ' land in North America -LRB- as the natives there did n't claim ownership -RRB- , they expanded to find more . Land became for the English in the 1600s what gold was for the Spanish in the 1500s ; a primary drive for sending their people to America . ` Gold , trade and tillage represent the three stages in the history of colonization and the greatest of these , because fundamentally essential to permanence is tillage . ' The attitudes of the European invaders towards the Native Americans varied . When Europeans came into contact with the Native Americans , taking note of their tribal dances , their more primitive weapons and agriculture and religious beliefs and practices , they often classified them as savages ; strange and uncivilized people who were not as `` human '' as white Europeans . These opinions were reflected in literature at the time . Possibly Caliban -LRB- the `` Noble Savage '' in Shakespeare 's ` The Tempest ' -RRB- is based upon a Native American . Although `` uncivilized '' the savage could be improved by contact with European civilization . Contrastingly , Cortez was clearly awe inspired by Aztec society as when he arrived in Tenochtitlan , he exclaimed : `` what rational beings live here . '' When Henry Hudson sailed up the river later named after him , ` except for two tragic incidents -LRB- attacks by hostile Indians , -RRB- he found the Indians a `` loving people '' ' ` To the Indians the newcomers seemed like magical monsters . '' The natives in both Mexico and North America often viewed the Europeans as gods due to their light skin complexions . ' When Cortez first arrived in Mexico , Montezuma , the Aztec emperor , believed him to be the returning god Quetzalcoatl . Chasteen even claims that ` Montezuma , the Aztec Emperor , had no earthly idea who , or what the Spaniards might be . In fact , Montezuma apparently suspected they were gods . '' By a fifty-two-to-one coincidence , Cortez had landed in the Yucatan in 1519 , a year that coincided with `` one-reed , '' a date on the Aztec calendar on which Quetzalcoatl was expected to return to reclaim his kingdom . Cortez 's appearance also happened to match the description of Quetzalcoatl in Aztec myth and as Michael Woods points out , myth was a very important part of Aztec society . Cortez discovered Montezuma 's misunderstanding of the nature of the Spanish through the gifted translator , Malinche , whom the Maya had given to him after he had defeated them when he initially arrived in the Yucatan . Whilst the Aztecs greeted the Spaniards with lavish gifts of food and golden idols , they treated them with suspicion and asked them to move their camp ` seven leagues away , ' and asked the Spaniards not to come to Tenochtitlan . The Spanish could only scrape together a couple of chairs and some velvet items which the main Aztec emissary looked at in disgust . Possibly this started to undermine Montezuma 's belief that the Spanish were returning gods . Montezuma also welcomed Cortez into Tenochtitlan as a guest with this cryptic message : ` Oh my lord , your are fatigued , you have come to your city of Mexico which I have guarded for you for a little while . They said you would return and now you have done so , but first go to your palace and rest and go in peace . ` It seemed as if Montezuma still expected Cortez to be Quetzalcoatl . It is possible that , like the Aztecs , the Indians of North America at first believed the ` white men ' to be godly creatures as their ancestral history did n't mention men of European appearance . It is uncertain whether the Powhatan tribe wished the English to leave their territory because after receiving European goods they enabled the English settlers to subsist by giving them maize and corn and showed them how to grow it . Later , after experience , Indian chiefs began to express their worries about the presence of Europeans in their homeland : ` We were happy when he first came . We first thought he came from the light , but he comes from the dusk of the evening now , not like the dawn of the morning . He comes like a day that has passed , and night enters our future with him . ` After an increasing number of disagreements in the 1620s , relations between the English and the Powhatan tribe became hostile and both sides distrusted the other . A common phrase used in the years after the 1620s by the English was ` the only good Indian is a dead Indian . ' On occasions the natives saw the Europeans in North America as people who needed their help , or people they could trade with ; often only too late did they realize that they were in fact invaders coming to take their land and their earthly possessions and remain . Cortez took many natives as servants and porters on his march to Tenochtitlan , even being forced to take many as warriors . The Europeans in North America took few natives as slaves relying on indentured servants transplanted from Europe . This may demonstrate a different attitude between the Spanish and the other Europeans towards the worth of Indians as labourers ; however , Cortez was cut off from the European world so he was forced to use native porters instead of European ones . Yet the Spanish began to take Slaves in their early colonies in the Caribbean demonstrating more will than any other European powers to take the native peoples as servants or slaves . The clash of religions between the Natives of the `` New World '' and the Europeans of the `` Old World '' were different in North America and Mexico . Later English settlers moving to North America were escaping religious persecution . Generally in the early years of the settling of North America there were few attempts by the largely Protestant Europeans to convert the natives to Christianity . In contrast , Cortez and the Spanish conquistadors seemed like Catholic evangelists . Cortez brought along one priest and often they were so shocked when they found evidence of Aztec sacrifices that they proclaimed the Christian gospel to the natives . When Montezuma showed a Cortez a room in one of their pyramids that had figures of Aztec gods but that also contained bowls of still warm human hearts , supposedly Cortez remarked : '' ' I do n't understand how a prince as great as you and a man as intelligent as you can call these things gods , I would like your permission to place a cross in here . ' '' Montezuma was greatly offended by this remark . Yet the Aztec tradition of human sacrifice made some Spanish believe that they had entered `` the jaws of hell . '' This clearly shows the contradiction between Christianity and the Aztec religion . Many alliances were set up between the natives and the Europeans ; In Mexico they tended to be short term political and military alliances , whereas in North America , at least initially , alliances tended to be made for trading and support purposes . However , later in North America , tactical alliances formed to enable power in certain areas to be swung towards one particular European nation or another . The Dutch were met by Indians in mid 1609 , who offered them food and water on their voyage up the Hudson River . The English in 1607 were given food and showed how to grow maize by the Powhatans in a similar way to when Cortez 's expedition was greeted by unarmed Aztec people offering them food in 1519 . They were also all attacked sporadically by various tribes , the Spanish by the Maya and the Tlaxcaltecs ; the Dutch by the Iroquois and the English by Powhatan 's tribe . The only European peoples who were n't attacked when they initially arrived were the French . However , the Aztec intention was made clear as they asked Cortez to leave Mexico ; the gifts were merely a sign of good intent . This was probably true for the Indians of North America who believed that the strange new people would leave if they satisfied them with gifts . Cortez first made alliances with the Totonacs , small groups of Maya , the Quiahuiztlan and the Cemophaultecs . These alliances were absolutely necessary as ` Cortez could not hope to conquer the empire with so few men , regardless of the military superiority they had demonstrated in the past . '' His most powerful allies were the Tlaxcaltecs who hated the Aztecs . All these native tribes entered these alliances partially blind to the true nature and intent of the Spanish . The Tlaxcaltecs were in a dangerous situation when Cortez arrived as they were engaged in war which would have inevitably led to their defeat by the Aztecs . Without this alliance , Cortez 's would have had little chance of capturing Tenochtitlan . Just as the Europeans would need assistance from the Indians in order to survive in North America , Cortez and his men needed local populations -LRB- who were willing to defy the Aztecs -RRB- to provide porters , supply food and contribute soldiers to make a sustained march into Mexico to defeat Montezuma . Some of his Indian allies would also serve as scouts and intelligence agents , similar to those the English would use in North America . Through these scouts , ` Cortez learned of the political division within Montezuma 's empire , and of the extent to which the Aztecs were resented by the subject peoples and by other kingdoms . '' Many times Cortez was welcomed into villages under Aztec rule and even villages that were tributaries to the Aztecs gave them food and water . Despite the fact that the Tlaxcaltecs were involved in a ` flower war ' with the Aztecs they initially attacked the Spanish . After several battles and the ransacking of native towns , by the Spanish -LRB- attempting to demonstrate their military might without substantial loss -RRB- the Tlaxcaltecs agreed to the treaty that Cortez had offered to them . Although they could have defeated Cortez , ` the Spanish would also be powerful allies ' in the Tlaxcaltecs ' war against the Aztecs , just as the Tlaxcaltecs would provide experienced warriors to aid the Spanish attack on Montezuma . When the Europeans arrived in North America , they formed complex relationships with the local natives . The French and the Dutch built trading posts and set up alliances with local Indians , trading European goods for furs and food . The French allied themselves with the Huron and the Dutch with the Iroquois . Both sets of alliances proved to be very profitable for both parties as the Indians traded animal furs for European goods such as guns , cloth and steel weapons . When the English settlers landed in Virginia , a particularly harsh area of North America in 1607 , where mosquitoes spread various diseases ; they were initially ill-prepared to survive there . At first they relied on the Indian tribes to provide them with the means to live but many were confident of their own self-sufficiency as Angie Debo points out , ` the thought never occurred to even the most enlightened Englishmen that the Indians might have something to give and apparently they never suspected that the natives had lost `` a scheme of life that worked '' ' The local Indians largely consisted of a tribe of about 10,000 Powhatan . Despite sporadic fighting , ` initially the coastal Indians helped the white settlers develop a subsistence economy . They taught the Europeans how to plant corn and use fish for fertilizer . '' Soon , John Smith -LRB- the leader of the English colony -RRB- decided it would be more profitable to trade than fight . Powhatan 's tribe gained many European tools and commodities , such as rifles and hatchets . In return the Indians provided the English with maize , corn and hides and taught them how to cultivate these new foodstuffs . In both North America and Mexico intermarriage resulted in strengthening political ties between powers . Despite the story of John Smith being saved by Pocahontas from execution by Powhatan possibly being untrue , it is clear that her marriage to John Rolfe , an English colonist , strengthened the alliance between the English settlers and the Powhatans . However , after Pocahontas died , relations between the English and Powhatan deteriorated rapidly . This was similar to an event in the Spanish conquest of Mexico when Cortez 's men arrived to assist the Cemophualtecs in a conflict against Aztec warriors . The Cemophualtecs gave the Spanish eight women who were all daughters of nobles and kings . This strengthened the new alliance between Cemophaultecs and Spanish . Whilst the lucrative fur trade meant that the French and the Dutch were interested in maintaining friendly alliances with the Indians for a number of years ; gradually the initial trading alliances between the Europeans and the Native Americans of North America changed . Before arriving in the `` New World , '' rivalries already existed between the European powers who were effectively competing against each other for land and wealth . The English also engaged in trade with Indian tribes but later , when their greed for land increased , they began to use their alliances to destabilize other European powers in the area . The English , jealous of the Spanish gold and the Dutch and French trade , firmly established themselves in Virginia , gaining their own powerful allies , the five nations Iroquois . ` Indian allies were absolutely necessary , to act as auxiliaries and scouts . The Indian , it emerged , was the key to dominion in the wilderness . When North America was at what passed for peace , imperial success was measured in terms of influence with the tribes . ` Conflict was a significant factor in European-Amerindian encounters and warfare was an important tool in determining which side would dictate terms to the other . Within these conflicts Europeans used alliances with native tribes against each other and native tribes used Europeans to finish off old feuds against other tribes . With conflicting European powers now arriving in North America in larger numbers , Indian alliances would prove very important to the stability of European presence in North America : Often European and Indian -LRB- and European and European -RRB- were able to live side by side in relative harmony . The Dutch and the Iroquois and the French and the Hurons initially benefited from trade with each other . Yet this trade led to an Indian dependence on European goods and soon the Europeans began to take advantage of their Indian allies . The European demand for beaver fur meant that the North American Indian tribes would hunt the beaver almost to extinction . Trading relationships would now begin to break down . After the introduction of alcohol into native societies by the Europeans , the means of getting dead drunk was even more desired among many Indian tribes than the new needs of life the Europeans had come to supply them with . The Europeans gained the upper hand in their relationships with the natives in North America , just as Cortez was able to dominate his relationships with his native allies in Mexico . The tribes who allied themselves with Cortez effectively gave up their independence and now relied on his success in defeating the Aztecs . They did not realize that their efforts to assist the Spanish in defeating the Aztecs would not be reciprocated in any substantial manner . They believed the Spanish would bring them freedom from Aztec rule and give them a share of their success ; yet effectively all the Spanish conquistadores did after defeating the Aztecs was to supplant Aztec nobility with themselves . One of the most important issues to consider when studying the interactions between the natives and the Europeans in North America and in Mexico is the matter of how the Europeans were able to conquer the natives so absolutely . The native peoples of Mexico and North America had several advantages over the small and concentrated numbers of European settlers and conquerors : their superior knowledge of the terrain , understanding of the nature of the other peoples living in their homeland and their greater numbers . However , although the Europeans had fewer numbers and little knowledge of the terrain , they had modern weapons such as guns and crossbows . It is surprising that the Indians did n't kill the Europeans the moment they landed . This was probably due to their interest in these new and astounding people . In both North America and Mexico , European weapons proved decisive in battles against Native populations . The Conquistadores took cannon , guns , crossbows and armour that would protect them from the majority of Aztec projectile weapons . The Europeans in North America brought similar weapons to the Spanish in Mexico . Obsidian -LRB- a dark , semi-translucent volcanic glass of the same composition as rhyolite -RRB- was one of the most important materials in Aztec warfare . Whilst it produced weapons and tools of unprecedented strength in the native world , they did not match those produced with Spanish steel . The Indians in North America used copper and bronze but their only sources of iron were fragments found in meteors . Therefore , they could not use local materials to make weapons as durable as European steel swords or knifes . In nearly all weapon classes of the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries , the Europeans had the advantage over the Native Americans . This meant that the most battles between Indian and European resulted in more Indian casualties . A difference between the Spanish and the English is the fact that the English traded guns and ammunition for Indian commodities during later periods in the settlement . With their superior weapons the Europeans brought tactics that were alien to the native population . Whilst at least some of the Indians in North America would be armed equally against the English in battle , the Aztecs would not . However , the North American Indians did not develop experience of European technology quickly enough to use them effectively against the Europeans until later , and they could not adapt their existing tactics to incorporate the gun alongside their bow and arrow and tomahawk . The dominant power in North America , the English colonists , did not initially use horses against their Indian foes to the extent that the Spanish did in Mexico . The Spanish took several horses to Mexico , which gave them a great psychological advantage , despite the fact that they were unsuited to the terrain of Mexico . The Aztecs had never seen horses before and their only experience with quadrupeds was the Chihuahua . Consequently , they were terrified by Spaniards on horseback who looked like `` godlike beasts . '' The Aztecs first believed that the horse and rider were one and the Spanish allowed them to believe this for as long as possible . Similarly , the first English colonists in New England would n't let the natives see their wounded or sick so that their enemies would think they were invulnerable . One may say that the Aztecs had the best chance of any native culture of repelling any intrusions into their territory owing to the number of people they could call upon to defend their empire . In comparison , the natives of North America were spread out thinly over the land and few tribes had any permanent settlements which the Europeans could attack . However , the Aztec empire was not as powerful as might be thought as many tribes within their empire were disloyal enough to side with Cortez . Also , the determination of the Aztecs to conquer other people and obtain tribute from them stretched their forces to the point where they were unable to effectively resist even modest Spanish forces . Similarly , there existed divisions between the tribes of North America which existed even before the arrival of the Europeans . ` The various tribes of New England often fought among themselves , usually over disputed land . Had they been able to forge a solid alliance , they would have been better able to resist the encroachment of white settlers . ` It was part of their culture to go to war with each other , just as it was part of the Aztec 's culture to wage war and conquer other peoples . Although certain tribes were interested in trading with Europeans , instead of fighting them , such as the Iroquois who traded with the Dutch and the Hurons who traded with the French , a number of tribes remained hostile to the Europeans and engaged in many conflicts with them . Surprisingly in some areas tribes flourished with the European settlers for long periods of time . One of the reasons the Aztecs could not have wiped out the Spanish when they first arrived was the fact that they could not transport sufficient materials of war as ` there were no wheeled vehicles or draught animals in Mesoamerica . '' All supplies of food and water for a large force had to be carried by an army of porters . This inability to manoeuvre their army meant that they could not ensure that all conquered regions stayed loyal to Tenochtitlan . This enabled Cortez to drive a wedge through the Aztec empire as he was able to pick up allies who resented Aztec rule . The English in North America picked their allies well , choosing the Five Nations Iroquois who were possibly the most skilled of all native warriors in battle . The English used them to put pressure on the French and Dutch settlements In North America . Similarly , the Iroquois used the English to help them defeat their long term opponents , the Huron . ` It was not a war of subjugation ; they hated the Huron intensely , like brother against brother . '' Both The Spanish and the English used allies to defeat their foes in the area they were interested in for gold and land respectively . Both the Aztecs and the Powhatans were defeated by European powers because both made crucial diplomatic mistakes that both John Smith -LRB- leader of the colony in New England -RRB- and Hernan Cortez -LRB- of the Spanish conquistadors -RRB- would exploit . The royal ambassador of the Aztecs made their first mistake in allowing Cortez to discover there was more gold within their realm . The second was Montezuma 's belief that Cortez was Quetzlcoatl and also to allow Cortez safe passage into Tenochtitlan . From there , Cortez captured Montezuma and used him as a puppet ruler until his own people killed him and forced the Spaniards out of Tenochtitlan . Their last mistake was to believe that the Spaniards would not return . Cortez 's next move was one of the most astonishing feats in history as he built a fleet on land and transported it to lay an eighty day siege on Tenochtitlan . ` This was a new kind of warfare for the Aztecs , a siege of a city which lasted 80 days and which included women and children . It was total war , something which the people of the old world had not experienced before . '' This was what brought Tenochtitlan to its knees and even though the Spanish had to fight street by street , they still managed to overwhelm the Aztec warriors with their superior weapons and other Indian allies . John Smith , the strong leader of the English colony in New England may not have accomplished as great a feat as Cortez ; yet he organized his settlement so that it would function properly , setting up provisional laws and punishing those who refused to work . He also successfully negotiated with the Powhatans , which ensured that while the colony gained strength , they were kept relatively safe from the local Indians . Like Cortez he facilitated the survival of the men under his command . The Powhatans ' first mistake was to continue trading food with the English whilst their colony grew in strength . After the Powhatans killed 350 colonist in 1622 , John Smith 's successor proceeded to exterminate the Powhatans , even poisoning Powhatan chiefs at a peace settlement . Disease may have been the single most important European weapon deployed against the native peoples in America , despite it being unintentional . In both Mexico and North America , after the Europeans had come into contact with native tribes , the Europeans passed on diseases of the Old World . The Indians had been isolated from the rest of the world for centuries so European diseases such as smallpox , measles and scarlet fever produced devastating effects on native populations : ` According to Henry Dobyns , there were almost a hundred epidemics and pandemics of European diseases among Native Americans between the first contact and the beginning of the twentieth century . '' He continues , ` many Native American communities lost 75 per cent or more of their members within just a few weeks . ' In the short time that it took the Conquistadores to defeat the Aztecs , the Spanish also managed to infect the Indian populations in Mexico -LRB- and Tenochtitlan -RRB- with smallpox during the winter of 1520 . Although many people died , the most devastating effect was the deaths of the Aztec leaders . Those who emerged to take their place ` lacked both the experience of their predecessors and the time needed to consolidated their rule and reaffirm allegiances with tributaries . '' Notably , the Spanish also lost many supporting Indian allies and their leaders . However , it was far less devastating for the Spanish as the leaders who came to replace the old native leaders were more loyal to the Spaniards than their forerunners . In both Mexico in the sixteenth century and in North East America in the seventeenth century , European diseases severely weakened native populations of the ability to fight and reduced their ability to feed themselves . Some historians ' estimates put total population reductions due to European diseases at ninety per cent . Whilst the native 's superior numbers when the European settlers first arrived in the New World , it is clear that after the fruitful voyages of Christopher Columbus , there would be an endless supply of Europeans willing to risk everything in order to make a profit or even a new life for themselves in America . They could also bring with them , and increasingly manufacture locally , the weapons of subjugation ` The New World was ' a land to be desired , and , once seen , never to be left . '' It was the initial aim of all the European powers to find gold in America . However , only the Spanish were successful . Their greed for gold predetermined their relationships with the Aztecs and the local people ; the Aztecs had the gold and the Spanish would take it from them by force . The Dutch and the French , who landed in North America , found no gold . Instead they found populations of native peoples who were willing to trade furs that were expensive luxury items in Europe for European goods . The English also found people to trade with , but their primary interest was land and power . The Europeans in North America largely saw the natives as savages . The Spanish saw the Aztecs as `` rational beings '' and were amazed at how developed their culture was . However , they were shocked at their religious practices and where possible sought to convert them to Catholicism . In contrast the Europeans of North America initially left the natives to their traditional beliefs . The nature of the alliances that developed between native and European in Mexico and North America initially seem different . In Mexico , the alliances formed between the Spanish and native populations were more political and military in nature . The ones formed between the natives and the Europeans in North America appeared initially to be for trading and support purposes . However , although the time scales are different both the Spanish in Mexico and the Europeans in North America were able to manipulate their native allies so far as to dominate and subdue them . An additional factor determining the relationships in North America was the use by Europeans of native tribes to extend their rivalries to America , which did not happen in Mexico . Although the Europeans had superior weapons , the natives initially had greater numbers and a superior knowledge of the terrain and should have been able to repel them . However , the native tribes in North America were n't unified and in Mexico the Aztecs had not succeeded in crushing all tribal resistance within their empire . Both also made many tactical errors that contributed to their defeat and were greatly weakened by the diseases that the Europeans brought from the `` Old World . ''
From the few years preceding the American Revolution , the thirteen colonies of the United States were becoming more and more independent from their mother country , Britain . This would have several effects on the British attitude to her colonies both from the British leadership in America and her public and parliament back home . Yet the relationship between the colonies and Britain `` seemed very close , both culturally and politically - and it is remarkable how this rosy picture changed so quickly . '' Even though there was still a great demand for British exports from the American colonies , each colony was individually was becoming more self sufficient on its own agriculture and produce . The fact that America was such a great distance to travel in those times , taking between three to five months to travel by sailing ship , meant that contacting England in times of crisis would be delayed by a significant amount of time . This distance would also mean that during any conflict it would take time to reinforce British troops if heavy losses were to be suffered . In many respects , the British should have won the American revolutionary war . Some reasons for this include : the strength of Britain and her empire at the time , the seemingly superior combat experience of the British troops compared with the American forces , -LRB- much of which comprised of farmers and young inexperienced men looking for adventure -RRB- and the better-quality weapons that the British army carried . However , by the time the fighting actually started , there was great patriotic support for the Revolution from all leagues of American society . The British also made some rather unfortunate blunders , loosing support from well needed allies and further alienating remaining loyalist support among the colonists . One of the first factors one should consider when examining the causes of the British defeat in the American war of independence is that of the popular support of the colonists for the patriotic movement that was taking the thirteen colonies of North America by storm . `` On September 5 th 1774 the first Continental Congress assembled in Philadelphia . '' This was to later organize the Declaration of Independence based on the widely published pamphlet , `` common sense . '' The strength of feeling and confidence in their own ability to survive should not be underestimated among the colonists . One tends to forget that by 1750 , the older colonies such as New England and Virginia were into their seventh of eight generation of peoples , who now saw themselves not as subjects of the British Empire but as Americans . What agitated them even further was that the British issued taxes after suffering a doubling in their national debt after the Seven Years War : This huge debt lay at '' # 122,600,000 in 1763 '' and `` the carrying charge of approximately # 4,500,000 absorbed more than half of the annual budget . '' Reforms of taxes included the Stamp Act of February 13 th 1765 , the Revenue Act of 1764 which prevented the printing of the dollar in all colonies and the Quartering Act which forced colonists , particularly in New York to allow British soldiers to stay within their homes or inns . This lead to disasters for American-Anglo relations such as the Boston Massacre where four Americans were killed and turned into martyrs and the Boston tea party where around 350 chests of tea was destroyed by patriotic activists . These events lead the English to retaliate with force , the leading to the huge growth of the patriotic movement among the colonists . After examining the evidence , one has to wonder how the loosely organized American army beat the might of the `` greatest army in the world . '' The British army had the greater experience and at first the greater number of regulars than the `` poorly supplied , inexperienced army . '' However , the Americans still managed to win the war of independence . Many historians have considered the American Civil War to be the first ` modern ' war in which both sides fighting used new weapons and technology . It was also the first war in which both sides suffered exceptionally high casualty rates , the next war with casualty rates as high as this was the First World War . It has been said that more Americans have died in the American Civil War than in all of the subsequent wars America has fought in put together . From the very beginning of the war , one may say that the war was particularly one sided as the Union in the north a much greater population than the south . Due to the division between the economies of the north and the south , with the south maintaining a plantation economy based on slavery and the north developing an industrial economy , one may claim that the north had a distinct advantage over the south in the way that its economy was far more suited to maintain the production of war materials . The generals who led the armies of each side also had a decisive part in the outcome of the war , both north and south having a number of skilled and incompetent generals . However , the reasons that each side fought the war were occasionally cause for concern as the south always had a clear aim ; to protect her slave economy and her independence from the north . The north however was divided on the issue of the emancipation of slaves as a reason for fighting the war . Yet the idea of a united America gave a rather more patriotic reason for the northern soldiers fighting against the rebellious south . Lincoln cleverly played up his policy of reuniting north and south as his main war aim . The defeat of the confederacy was not due to just one or two main reasons , but due to a complex combination of events and policies . In another configuration of the dynamics of the war , the south may have won and the issue of slavery may still be a problem in America today . The politics that led to the war coming into existence played a greatly important part in the outcome of the war . The greatest use of politics in the war was to encourage soldiers to enlist for a cause that they would consider worth fighting for . However , Lincoln 's inauguration caused a great deal of irritation among the southerners as in ten southern states his name did not even appear on the ballot , therefore the vast majority of his support coming from northern states . Upon his inauguration as President , Lincoln set about to consummate the most prominent of his policies at the time , that of the reunification of the United States by reversing his predecessor 's claim that the secession of the southern states was legal . Despite slavery being the central issue of the war from the start , the Union was to be the greatest policies for the initial enlistment of soldiers . The mysterious reason for the great amount of support and enthusiasm that northern soldiers gave for the union has not been debated much by historians . To understand the defeat of the south fully , one has to examine a series of material factors . The difference between the northern economy and the southern economy was hugely important in explaining why the south lost the war . From the very beginning the north had the advantage of its industrial might which would outweigh the southern ability to produce weapons , clothing and food for the soldiers : ` Military striking power in a modern , all-out war is finally supported , conditioned and limited by the physical scope and vitality of the basic economy . ` Mass production techniques adopted by northern industries meant that the northern soldiers were fed , clothed and armed far more readily than their southern counterparts . When attempting to determine why the winner of a war did win that war , an obvious area of study would be the comparative strength of each side 's military , their commanders and the men who actually fought the war . The generals who lead the men in the epic battles that took place across American farms , fields and towns were also influential in determining the outcome of the American Civil War . Initially it seemed as if the south had the stronger leadership after the embarrassing defeat of Union forces in ` Bull Run ' with great generals like General Robert . E. Ultimately the union generals were of a higher quality at the end of the war than at the beginning ; but the union commanders had and always had the advantage of numbers and supplies that even great southern generals like Lee and Johnson could not overcome . Historians have explained the south 's defeat in a variety of ways . Even farmers , rising to meet the demand of the increased need for food , did n't suffer economic plight in the North as is common twentieth century economics during wartime . It was a combination of the many deficits of the south such as a small population , an archaic economy and a fraught and inexperienced political leadership , and the strengths of the north such as its mighty economy , large population and strong moral support for the war that worked together against the South .

In many aspects it is Marquez 's ability to weave fantasy into reality that makes him and his novel ` One Hundred Years of Solitude ' stand out so vividly in the minds of critics and readers from all over the world . His talent for seamlessly intertwining the magical events that take place in the fictional town of Macondo with realistic events , forces the reader to scrutinize everything he says in order to ascertain whether it contains any supernatural elements . However , as prominent as these magical events may be , one has to ask the question as to whether there is any meaning to them ; or if there is any point for them to be included , other than as artistic devices . On the one hand , Marquez may use them to allude to certain types of characters such as the strong , outgoing Jose Arcadios or the solitary and studious Aurelianos . On the other hand , he may use the magical elements in his novel to accentuate a certain theme he is exploring . There are many events in the novel that cause debates over whether magic is present or not . The reason for confusions such as this is that Marquez plays with the reader 's expectations of time , reality and history . Interestingly one can claim that this may be seen as an example of magic ; as Marquez has utilized the basic style of modernist literature and cultivated it to the extent that he even places himself in the novel as an active character . His novel has since been placed in the magical realism category and this itself is a ` narrative style which consistently blurs the traditional realist distinction between fantasy and reality . '' Marquez clearly uses magic to mark a point at certain times in the novel . By simply being completely extraordinary in nature , the magic he uses marks events he describes even more vividly than the simple narrative of more traditional novel . One of the most vivid , shocking and important magical images in the novel is that of the ` pig 's tail . ' It is mentioned early on in the novel by Ursula , the wife of the founder of the town , Jose Arcadio Buendia . This image of a Buendia child being born with a pig 's tail seems to haunt Ursula all the way through the novel , despite the fact that it is only mentioned about four or five times . It appears early in the novel when Ursula expresses her worries about having a child with a pig 's tail when she marries her cousin Jose Arcadio Buendia -LRB- Macondo 's founder . -RRB- She is worried about this because when her aunt married to an uncle of Jose Arcadio Buendia 's they had a son who was born with the tail of a pig : ` for he had been born and had grown up with a cartilaginous tail in the shape of s corkscrew and with a small tuft of hair on the tip . '' This image is particularly exceptional as it refers directly to another of the book 's main themes ; the theme of incest . Marquez makes this image particularly repulsive by the comparison of the tail to a corkscrew and the inclusion of hair on it . This clearly shows that incest is a taboo as it is in reality . In many societies , there still exists myths that claim that a child from sex between family members can cause physical defects in that child . An example is said to be that the child may be born with six toes or fingers . Marquez takes this one step further , making this claim seem rather ridiculous . However , this image appears again later in the novel when Ursula is ` convinced that the wild behavior of her children was something as fearful as a pig 's tail . '' Clearly Ursula does believe in the misfortunes of having a child through incest . This magical image , very alike to the punishment of a naughty child in a fairytale is used by Marquez possibly to show how ingrained the myth of incest is in the minds of the population of Latin America and even the rest of the world . It may also be used to punish the characters for their defiance of social traditions as the very last Buendia to be born through sex between brother and sister , Aureliano and Amarata Ursula , is also the one to be born with the tail of a pig . Due to its proximity to the end of the novel , this image suggests that the Buendia line is doomed and that it always has been . Another interesting magical image in the text is that of the butterflies that appear when Mauricio Babilonia is present . It is difficult to decipher what this image means , but it has been suggested that it represents the love between Renata Remedios and Mauricio Babilonia . These butterflies are only present during one chapter which demonstrates that this supernatural idea is not as important as that of the pig 's tail . However , they do serve a significant purpose and are a beautiful example of how creative Marquez 's writing can be : ' . . she had been bothered by a sudden flapping of wings . It was the butterflies . Meme saw them as if they had suddenly been born out of the light and her heart gave a turn . ` In these sentences , the appearance of the butterflies leads Meme to experience a sudden feeling of passion . This is because by this point she has already linked the emergence of ` yellow butterflies ' to the coming of Babilonia whom by that time she has fallen passionately in love with . Marquez 's use of the butterflies serves as a link between the couple , and a demonstration of their love . We are also given no indication that the butterflies were present around Babilonia before meeting Meme . When Meme 's mother , Fernanda breaks up their relationship by sending Meme away ; ` The yellow butterflies would invade the house at dusk , ' causing Fernanda great dismay . This may be interpreted as Fernanda 's passive punishment from Meme for ending their relationship as she says nothing after the incident . Also supporting the argument that the butterflies represent the love between the couple is the fact that the butterflies ` did not give him -LRB- Babilonia -RRB- a moment 's peace , ' when he was lying on his back paralyzed for the rest of his life . Clearly the butterflies represent the burden of his love for Meme that he carries until his death . One magical event that seems to have little meaning is the ascension of ` Remedios the beauty ' to the skies . Up until this point she was a very mysterious character , one of great beauty that called to men like a siren . The more she deviated from fashions and normal womanly behavior , the more she became attractive to men . The answer lies with the opinion of the vast majority of men that would probably conclude that a women shaving her hair off and wearing unusual clothing would n't appear as attractive as a similar woman complying with fashion . This is debatable . This event of her rising towards the heavens seems to be an appropriate way for her to leave the novel ; instead of expiring like all the other more earthly Buendia 's . ` One Hundred Years of Solitude ' also contains some very humorous passages where Marquez makes jokes to relieve tension , or to mock some of the more ridiculous characters . Within one passage , he mixes humor with magic to ridicule a Texan , Mr. Brown . Mr. Brown joins one of Aureliano Segundo and Petra Cotes 's wild Champaign parties and gets drunk forcing his dogs to dance to the music : ' ... several times he got dead drunk in Petra Cotes 's house and he even made the fierce German shepherd dogs that went everywhere with him dance to some Texas songs that he himself mumbled in one way or another to the accompaniment of the accordion . ` Marquez probably uses this image of fierce dancing dogs and the mumbling Texan to ridicule eccentric American foreigners who travel to South America in search of a profitable investment . This magical image of his fierce dancing dogs either works as a relief from the tension that is created by the fight between Fernanda and Aureliano in the previous couple of pages , or it reminds us that although Mr. Brown may be a ridiculous character , he is also one with sinister motives who later causes the massacre of all of his workers . It has been said that ` Modernism was built on a sense of lost community and civilization . '' This is very true for the community of Macondo , as they are cut off from the world for many years before a clear path out of the town is found . Yet by including magical events , people and places , Marquez develops his town and community to be expressive of a number of themes that he uses in the novel . The theme of incest is clearly represented and distinguished by the image of a child being born a deformity in the shape of a pig 's tail . Marquez clearly emphasizes the love between Babilonia and Meme with the emphatic image of the yellow butterflies . He includes a supernatural character whose life is filled with solitude due to her incompatibility with the rest of society and who leaves earth in a ` flurry of sheets . ' He also uses hand in conjunction with humor not only to mock , but to warn the reader of a dangerous character in the form of Mr. Brown . By using magic he effectively draws the reader 's attention to important events , themes and characters in the book . In addition , it is a fresh device that aids Marquez in his narrative , adding curiosity that causes the reader to reflect more on the significance of events and of the meaning of the novel as a whole .

To answer this question it is useful to study a confined area of the Caribbean . Furthermore it is more practical to study a time period of three or four decades . Therefore to demonstrate that sugar brought about a social revolution in addition to an agricultural revolution , this essay will focus on the islands of Jamaica and Barbados from around the years 1630 to 1690 . This is because by 1700 , both Jamaica and Barbados became significant producers of sugar . Moreover , the English had found few traces of precious metals in the parts of America which they initially colonized . This meant that the islands -LRB- or inland settlements -RRB- that they did set up tended to be far more focused around agricultural activities . By the mid 1600s England was in possession of both Jamaica and Barbados . As a result , sugar would become the defining feature of the islands ; shaping their future and acting as a precedent for the development of other Caribbean islands in later years . In order to show that society and agriculture in the Caribbean experienced change after the introduction of sugar into its economy , it is useful to make a brief summary of the condition of both areas before continuing . Jamaica was originally a Spanish colony before the English seized it in 1655 and gained formal possession in 1670 through the Treaty of Madrid . Barbados was initially settled in 1627 by the English so remained a British colony until its independence . Spain did n't truly realize the potential economic wealth that could be generated from producing and harvesting the sugar cane in large quantities until after silver and gold mines began to dry up in South America . By then the Spanish had lost some of their territories in the Caribbean to other powers vying for control in the area . In Cuba , sugar became the predominant force in the economy only by in the late 16 th century . The early forms of agriculture in these areas consisted of a number of small planters growing either crops to feed themselves along with a little surplus to sell on to others , a intermediate amount of people with a few indentured servants or slaves who grew their own crops in addition to other more economically productive crops such as tobacco , coffee and sugar , and a minority of larger plantation owners who owned larger numbers of indentured servants and/or slaves who grew large amounts of sugar , coffee or tobacco . When the Brazilian Civil War caused a crisis in sugar production in Brazil , Dutch traders and Barbadian planters saw a chance to open up the sugar market in the Caribbean . By the 1630s there was also a small crisis in tobacco and cotton production within some of the islands of the Caribbean and mainland America which created an even greater opportunity for the success of sugar . As sugar became more profitable for planters in the Caribbean , the demand for labour greatly increased . Before the introduction of large numbers of Africans into the Caribbean , a large amount of work on plantations was carried out by indentured servants . However , as V.T Harlow states `` The money which procured a white man 's service for ten years could buy a negro for life . '' As the prices of slaves decreased , planters found it more profitable to buy slaves rather than employ indentured servants who would have a limited term of service at the end of which they would leave to take land valuable to the cultivation of sugar cane . It was also becoming increasingly expensive to supply indentured servants to the Caribbean . The latter half of the 17 th century experienced great demographic changes which would result in a revolution in both the agriculture and society of the Caribbean . As the number of enslaved Africans soared in the Caribbean , the number of white settlers fell . `` The enterprising social class left voluntarily to the escape hunger and lower standard of living that was brought about by the lack of work . '' As small farmers and artisans left to other parts of the Caribbean and the American mainland they were replaced by great numbers of imported slaves . This would have great effects for the social structure that was in place . Before there was the elite class who were defined economically by owning large farms and plantations , the majority of whom would own at least fifteen slaves or servants . Next in rank came the merchants , officials and professionals who were just below the planters . Next came the `` poor whites '' who were described pejoratively in Barbados as `` red legs . '' The `` poor whites '' consisted of servants , servicemen such as policemen and militiamen and small independent farmers . In 1645 there were 11,200 landed proprietors in Barbados ; by 1667 there were only 745 . The independent whites working the land for themselves were now being displaced by large farms with large numbers of enslaved Africans . This would mean that the general trend of the population shifted towards a more concentrated , elite white population and a vastly increasing number of enslaved peoples . Instead of three basic social divisions existing in a largely white population , from the 1650s onwards three different basic social divisions could be made : `` free white persons , free non-white persons , and slaves . '' The basic three divisions within the white population still existed during the increasing demand for sugar in the mid 1600s , yet the elite class was becoming concentrated and the lowest white class was becoming smaller . As the number of whites decreased , they needed to impose laws to restrict the movement and activities of slaves in order to control them . One such law stipulated that any slave leaving their plantation for any period of time had to be issued with a slip of paper from their master allowing them to do so . Others issued concerning punishments for disobedient and rebellious slaves were released throughout the 1600s . Due to English attitudes , the white planters did not allow slaves to practise religion the Caribbean . The Christian faith was a great social rallying point for slaves in the southern States in North America and black communities built tight societies around their churches which were segregated from the white population . They were controlled by militia who were made up of the poorer whites and in turn were controlled by the plantation owners . One report of Cuban commissioners even claimed that : `` A country 's agriculture is not the result of caprice or prejudice . It rests on the society which produced it , and develops according to the institutions which govern that society . '' This shows that society -LRB- or control of the masses by the elites -RRB- is not merely loosely interlinked with agriculture , but that it was produced as a result of it . However , despite the black population rising and developing within the confines of slavery , distinctions could be made that at least approximated social distinctions . At the bottom of the black `` social class '' there were the basic labourers who worked long back breaking days on the sugar plantations . As you moved up to the next division one found the domestic servants who were often allowed to sleep within the houses of their master . On the top level of the black social class were the scouts , watchmen and rangers . Outside the enslaved population there existed a free coloured population , particularly in the urban areas . They could be considered to have been on the same social level as the lowest white populations . However , they were discriminated against by the more powerful white population , being barred from certain professions and given a lower wage . They also had to compete with slaves who would work zealously in an attempt to buy their freedom from their master . The majority of the free colours were women in urban areas . These women were the most likely to be able to start a family . There were also cases of complex inter-racial relations in the Caribbean . Free black women and those in domestic service often had relationships with their masters . It was the vast disparity between the number of whites and the number of Africans that made complex social relationships like this possible in a world that abhorred the thought of it . It may be suggested that society did not experience a revolution , but merely shifted in weight towards the elite during the intensification of the Caribbean 's sugar production and her slave intake . Planters have been used to having large numbers of indentured servants whom may well have been considered as slaves by the way they were treated . Slaves were often worth more to their masters -LRB- # 35 a head per slave compared to # 10-12 for an indentured servant -RRB- than indentured servants as servants would only serve a term of several years , a slave would serve a term of his whole life . One may say that the slave merely replaced the indentured servant in society leaving little change but an increase in the black population . However , this is an oversimplification of the problem and not only was there a social revolution ; there was also an agricultural revolution . The numbers of the incoming Africans were just too great to deny that any social revolution could not take place . All of these people had to be controlled and the land they worked on had to be taken from other sources -LRB- smaller farms . -RRB- This resulted in the condensing of white society in the Caribbean and the development of black society ; developed far enough that 1649 , 1675 and 1692 saw large scale slave revolts brewing . Plantation owners controlled the society but slaves were becoming organized enough to rebel against their authority . Agriculture saw great changes during the rise of sugar . Large sugar plantations often took in the lands of smaller farmers who were forced out of the islands by economic hardship . These smaller farmers would often grow several different crops and different farmers grew different crops . This did mean that the Caribbean economy had no real great driving force behind it , but it was far more agriculturally diverse and self-sufficient than the sugar grinding machine it became during the mid to late 1600s . Imports rose greatly as sugar cane replaced other more basic foodstuffs such as wheat . As agriculture centred on sugar and became far less varied , society became far more diverse and complex as a whole new race of people were introduced in to the Caribbean . This would have a profound and efficacious effect on the future years of the Caribbean .
1838 is a crucial date in the history of Guyana as on the 1 August , the emancipation of Africans and creoles who had been working in sugar plantations was finally carried through and protected by law . In order to preserve the status quo between 1834 and 1838 , creoles were still bound to plantations through apprenticeship , which in many ways was similar to the conditions they had worked and lived in as slaves . This created great tensions between creoles and the colonial elites and as soon as the blacks were freed from their bondage , most sought to escape the plantation system permanently . Some became peasant and tenant farmers , and those who were lucky were finally able to rise through the social ranks . For example , they moved into the retail sector , become artisans and merchants ; some even became white collar workers such as lawyers and doctors and a very few were able to gain considerable wealth and aspired to become as esteemed as the traditional white colonial elites . However , despite this fresh fiscal activity , Guyana remained profoundly reliant on sugar plantations to fuel the majority of her economy . This meant that even before emancipation , plantation owners began to search for other sources of labour . The solution presented itself in the form of immigration from India , China and Portugal . The reasons these people had for immigrating varied from economic hardship to religious or political persecution . A majority of these immigrants -LRB- particularly the darker skinned Chinese and the Indians -RRB- filled the roles of the ex-slaves as indentured servants on the plantations . Largely , this meant that they experienced similar living and working conditions to the ex-slaves . Yet , generally , the Portuguese remained outside the plantation economy , preferring to focus their efforts on retail trade . Crucially , the colonial elites allowed the Portuguese to work outside the plantations due to their European background and their white skin . Elites placed them nearer the top of the ` racial and cultural stratification ' whereas non-white immigrants , were more desirable for plantation work due to the prejudices of white colonial elites in Guyana putting non-white immigrants as the lower end of the social spectrum . Essentially the cultural elites in Guyana sought to compel other cultures to conform to their cultural traditions and accept their view of racial superiority according to skin colour . When in Guyana , immigrants realized that their traditional religious practices , living arrangements , their language and nearly every part of their normal day lives would be challenged by a variety of influences and pressures from other cultures present within Guyana . Importantly , the majority of immigrants envisaged their stay in Guyana as a temporary affair which would last only as long as it would take to amass a fortune with which to return to their home country . Immigrant cultures , therefore , generally sought to preserve their traditional cultures as opposed to the creoles who had already accepted their place in Guyana as permanent . Consequently , Creoles often sought to mimic the cultural elites in order to increase their social mobility , whereas the immigrants generally attempted to resist the pressures of the elites in order to maintain their cultural heritage . The degree to which different racial groups would be affected would vary from person to person and group to group . For example , the Portuguese attempted to live as a closed social group and cut off as much outside contact as possible . However , as their primary trade was retail , they did experience cultural pressures from those they conducted business with . Both the Indians and the Chinese were forced to live in cramped and unhygienic plantation accommodation once reserved for the slaves , under the constant cultural restrictions and pressures emanating from elites and local creoles . It was in these conditions that immigrants attempted to preserve their cultural identity . In order to ascertain how far immigrant cultures were affected by other racial groups , it most helpful to examine how far their traditions changed -LRB- or if they were able to preserve them intact -RRB- under different pressures in several areas of life in Guyana . One of the most concerning challenges that immigrants had to face when they arrived in Guyana was the maintenance of traditional family structures . This is due to an acute shortage of females who boarded the labour ships bound for Guyana . ` From the outset it proved extremely difficult to get Indian women to emigrate . '' For example , the number of Indian females who arrived from 1880 to 1889 was 7,494 compared with the arrival of 16,408 males in the same period . However , the shortage of women was most evident among the Chinese as Brian L. Moore demonstrates : ` At the height of their immigration in 1863 , the proportion of females per hundred males was only 13.64 . Although this figure improved to 25.7 by 1880 , it was hardly an adequate ratio . ` This shortage meant that the Chinese population would remain largely insignificant in Guyana , and might even have been threatened with extinction . This had the effect of increasing the freedom of choice of partner for female immigrants . It also encouraged them to accept partners from other cultures . This represented a simple but significant departure from Chinese tradition , as in contemporary China , women rarely chose their partner . K. O. Lawrence claims that the shortage of female Indian immigrants was due to the fact that many were locked in pre-arranged marriages as part of an old Indian tradition . However , Brian L. Interestingly , the altered situation of the Indian immigrants in Guyana meant that traditional Indian marriages and even traditional patriarchal relationships were considerably adjusted . However , Brian Moore states that ` the Indian immigrants strongly resisted elite pressures to conform to the Anglo-Creole model of the male-female relationship . '' Instead , restrictions imposed on women through religious ideology , social institutions and ideas that were strongly rooted in Indian society itself were eroded in Guyana , allowing women freedoms that are commonplace in modern western society . Thus , arranged marriage , a staunch tradition that was central in Indian culture was reduced in Guyana . Crucially , ` heathen marriages ' were discouraged by colonial authorities who ruled that any children born under traditional Indian marriages were considered to be illegitimate and could not legally inherit property . This may be seen as a fundamental restriction on an Indian cultural activity that was an important part of their cultural identity ; Indian marriages are a proud and joyous event with great amounts of money spent on them . Thereafter , legal or traditionally binding marriages were seen as practical in Guyana as wives increasingly left their husbands for , more prosperous men . In addition , women in Guyana tended to regard cross-race marriage far less controversially . Significantly , the Portuguese population did not experience such severe shortages of female immigrants . The existence of interactions between other cultures in Guyana joined with the shortage of women in both the Chinese and the Indian populations caused significant cultural changes to take place . On the other hand , the Portuguese community contained almost equal numbers of men and women which meant they were able to preserve a more closed Portuguese society , effectively living outside the effects of other communities . Resultantly , from within the retail sector , several Portuguese immigrants were able to build up quite a significant amount of wealth . With this wealth , many developed from renting buildings for use as shops and homes or living in housing that was similar to what contemporary creoles lived in , to becoming part of the middle-class , to building or buying stately properties : ` By the end of the nineteenth century affluent Portuguese families owned and occupied some of the finest mansions in upper-class white areas of the city . ` The Portuguese immigrants filled these mansions with furniture and possessions that was usually imported from Portugal or Madeira reflecting both their cultural roots and their newly acquired wealth . Conversely , the Indians and the Chinese were forced to live in the old slave houses ; ` some might move from `` bound yards '' to adjacent `` free yards ; '' however , they were still within the confines of the old `` nigger yard '' where paternalism and quasi-feudalism died hard . '' Although plantation accommodation was of poor quality , which was frequently damp , hot and usually overcrowded , -LRB- a far cry from the thatched mud huts they were used to at home -RRB- by the 1860s the Indians added a little of their own home ingenuity and coated the floors with hardened mud and smeared them with cow-dung which destroyed vermin . This demonstrated that while in some accommodation , which was so cramped it made it almost impossible for the Indians to maintain their caste rituals , the immigrants altered their housing to recreate a more traditional Indian or Chinese home . Both furnished their housing with items such as rugs , drapes , and curtains that resembled a home away from home far from being in accordance of European tastes . The Indians even placed charms around the house to ward off evil spirits . Chinese immigrants were fond of candles and lanterns which they would usually make themselves ; both were important statements of traditional cultural identity . Housing became a very personal space for the immigrants in Guyana and all adapted their conditions to suit their own cultural tastes . However , it was clear that traditional housing would not be realized truly until the Chinese and Indians moved out of the plantations . Within them they were confined to living in cramped conditions , sometimes sharing with different cultures and often prevented them from carrying out their normal daily rituals that would allow them to maintain their customary cultural-religious traditions . Nevertheless , despite the limitations of accommodation for cooking purposes , both the Indians and the Chinese were fairly innovative at adapting the materials they had to reconstruct conditions suitable for cooking their traditional foods . ` The Indians built clay hearths for cooking , ' and the Chinese still used chopsticks for their meals instead of European cutlery . The Indians and Chinese tended to use rice in the majority of their meals and the Indians usually ate ` fish and vegetable curry , or curried rice , ' as testified by Alexander Alexander , a Scottish colonist . However , he claimed that of all the dishes the Indians ate , ` the most popular is coffee with bread or biscuits , at the outset the coolies fought shy of it , but now they prefer it to their much-loved rice curry . '' Yet , this opinion may be questioned as it is unlikely that Alexander shared meals with a large enough consensus to make his claims . Regardless of maintaining their traditional meal structure , the Chinese sought to supplement their meals with exotic fruits , vegetables and meats that were found in Guyana . One may argue eating new exotic foods would alter Chinese eating habits and therefore influence cultural changes among their community in Guyana . Yet , in essence , the Chinese still retained the same staple diet of noodles or rice with meat and vegetables . Not surprisingly , the Portuguese were the most successful in maintaining their traditional diets . On the whole they were able to afford to pay to import foodstuffs from Portugal preserving their own culinary treats , largely on account of the regular traffic that was maintained between Madeira and the colony . Diet was an important statement of cultural resistance made by the immigrant populations . They all refused -LRB- with a few exceptions -RRB- to adopt European or creole eating habits , striving to sustain foods that suited their own cultural individuality , thus supporting the notion of cultural pluralism . Perhaps the general expectation of returning to their mother country was the greatest motivation to maintain a culinary connection with home . Another important aspect of cultural adjustment that should be examined is how social organization was altered when immigrants moved to Guyana . For the Indian community , the fact that the idea and observance of a caste system ` lost its rigidity in Guyana , ' was a crucial change for Indian culture as it was almost unbendingly adhered to in India . However , the constant supply of new Indian immigrants meant that the basic structure upon which the caste system was based was maintained . Many arguments were used by planters when recruiting Indians from different castes such as ` to regard merely the condition of their hands and muscles , ' or to accept a majority of lower caste immigrants as they were more suited to agricultural labour . However , Brian Moore suggests that castes were adhered to in an ` inverse and perverse manner as higher castes were feared for their leadership qualities ' and lower castes favoured for plantation work . This claim is supported by Walter Rodney states that ` high caste status and the job of driver -LRB- a position of authority on plantations -RRB- each separately provided advantages which were transformed into class mobility . Possibly planters sought to reduce the power of high castes such as Brahmans as they feared worker uprising and would take every precaution to avoid it . On this point , the Chinese posed a worrying problem for the planters because they began to organize themselves into clan organizations and secret societies such as was traditional when establishing themselves in other countries : ` Many clan organizations were composed of persons who were bonded by a common surname and were from the same region or shared the same dialect . ` These secret societies attempted to maintain links with China . In addition , they followed a three point code : secrecy , to grant assistance in times of trouble and a respect for one another 's wife . This was a greatly important feature of the Chinese socio-cultural organization , a feature which soon became a problem for plantation managers ; these clan organizations very quickly began to take on criminal functions and at times , resembled the Chinese mafia . They conducted armed night raids on other plantations and creoles settlements , hoping to gain justice for the injustices they suffered as indentured labourers being forced to adopt a different culture . The gangs were therefore a symbol of Chinese resistance to cultural , social and economic pressures emanating from the host society . Again , the Portuguese were largely able to maintain their traditional social structure of classes that was common throughout the European community . However , the colonial elites used their cultural power to ridicule rich Portuguese families through mediums such as newspapers and local gossip in elite institutions . In doing this they hoped to persuade the Portuguese to conform to ` ideals of Victorian morality ; ' however , Portuguese pride allowed them to withstand these attempted attacks on their cultural identity . However , local conditions and special laws introduced in 1862 and 1865 prevented the continuation of the Chinese clan organizations , an important defeat of their traditional socio-cultural organization . Similarly , although the Indians were able to transfer the basic ` varna ' pattern of the caste system to Guyana , they were unable to transmit it with its traditional authenticity . Yet , one may claim that as with the Chinese clans , their caste organization meant that they had a basic structural framework for resisting the cultural influences of the elites and creoles present in Guyana . In Guyana , leisure pursuits sometimes proved to be a unifying force for all cultures . All cultures enjoyed theatrical and musical performances , and all practised various forms of musical instruments in their spare time -LRB- of which there was little for plantation workers . -RRB- In addition , Indians enjoyed tales or sacred books read by a Pandit and the Chinese worried plantation owners by opening opium and gambling dens , -LRB- which were often connected to the criminal sections of secret societies -RRB- where they spent a great deal of their spare time -LRB- and wages -RRB- . Opium smoking and gambling were considered to be twin evils by plantation owners who abhorred the practise taking place within their plantations . Yet as these were within in worker accommodation , there was little the elites could do to stop them . This presented quite a blatant disregard for the cultural superiority of white plantation owners and an interesting assertion of Chinese cultural identity . Quite antithetically , the Portuguese set up social clubs that were centred on the Church . The Portuguese strove to maintain the cultural exclusivity of these clubs and closed them to Portuguese members only . Normally , the clubs , societies and organizations of each culture remained closed institutions to external cultural influences , thus maintaining cultural identity idiosyncrasy . However , and importantly , creoles and Indians were occasionally found in Chinese gambling and opium dens demonstrating that examples may be found of intimate cultural interaction and adaptation . The example that is largely ratifying of leisure pursuits existing as a unifying force in Guyana is cricket . Cricket became popular amongst all cultures that ventured to Guyana , whether they were the colonial elites or the Chinese immigrants . In the nineteenth century , far from producing a homogenizing effect on the cultures , teams were organized by race , not plantation or region . Interestingly , voluntary participation in cricket posed the best chance for the Chinese and the Indian assimilation the dominant elite cultural values . However , unlike the Creoles who often attempted to adopt these elite traits to improve their social mobility , the Indians and the Chinese did not seek ` acceptance , social mobility or respectability among whites ; they merely took part to show the whites they could perform just as well , if not better than them . '' This also meant that both the Chinese and Indians did n't always play by rules such as ` Leg before Wicket ' as they demanded to only lose through distinct rules that they could understand and adhere to . Although cricket appeared to unite the different cultures in Guyana in a homogenizing sense , the fact that teams were organized by race seems to support the idea that in Guyana in the mid to late nineteenth century , there existed multiple cultures running parallel to one another in a more pluralized society . In addition , the rejection of elite rules is a lucid assertion of cultural resilience to elites who sought to enforce their ideal design of society through the medium of cricket . Another form of cultural resistance to the influences of elites and other cultures in society was the practise of religion . For the Indian immigrants , ` most of the religious duties and festivals were maintained , except for the namaz -LRB- prayers five times a day -RRB- and the full observance of the fast of Ramazan , ' that were both impractical within the rigours of the plantation work schedule . However , there was a distinct need for homogenization of the various forms of Hindu and Muslim religions as it was almost impossible to preserve every distinct variation . Bhaktism was therefore most suitable for overseas Indians as its ideals favoured egalitarianism , inter-caste fraternization and collective activity which would be important during the breakdown of traditional caste and family structures . This homogenization is even clear in the Hindu and Muslim temples and mosques built in Guyana that were unlike their counterparts in India due to the existence of multiple deities ` in a pantheon of Sanskritic gods . '' On the other hand , the Chinese found it increasingly difficult to sustain their traditional religions : ` One of the factors which affected the ability of the Chinese immigrants to preserve their traditional culture in Guyana was the influence of Christianity . ` Some Chinese who arrived in Guyana had already been converted to Christianity by missionaries who had reached China . In their small numbers , they were once again preyed upon by Christian missionaries . Crucial to the missionary effort was Tai Kam , a Chinese Anglican who arrived in the colony in 1864 and in 1866 was officially appointed missionary to the Chinese immigrants with a grant of # 300 annually . Due to the efforts of Tai Kam and other prolific missionaries , ` by the 1880s , there were very few Chinese who were not Christians . '' The elites also used their cultural power to reward obedient Chinese Christian communities such as increased wages or paid holiday . In this way , Christianity was used as a method of social control . As the numbers of Chinese Christians rose , the number of night attacks by gangs fell , thus reducing a part of Chinese cultural resistance . Yet , despite the success of Christianity as a mechanism of control , the Chinese did not follow Christianity in its pure form . Instead they chose to have traditional Chinese weddings and funerals , in occasion paid respect to Taoist Gods in Chinese temples and continued to celebrate Chinese New Year , gamble and smoke opium , clear forms of resistance to the principles of Christianity . This selective nature of Christian worship represented limited cultural assimilation . Look Lai claims that such assimilation never amounted to ' a process of absorption by the larger culture , but a more subtle process of hybridization . '' Thus the adoption of Christianity by the Chinese did not signal the Chinese community as admitting the cultural superiority of the colonial elites . As mentioned before , planters sought to avoid worker unrest at all costs . On the whole they recognized the how important religion was to the Indian immigrants and did not seek to displease their workers by preventing them from practising it : ` Religious activities among the indentured Indians were quite tolerated - even facilitated - by plantation managers , as long as these activities did not conflict with economic production . ` Nevertheless , missionaries still attempted to infiltrate Indian communities . Planter policy was haphazard and as stated before , they generally disliked allowing their workers access to a Christian education for fear it may foster disturbances . Even so , Indian priests were becoming more adept at resisting Christian missionaries physically and philosophically . Generally the Indian population was successful in opposing the imposition of Christianity on their culture , in the hope their return to India in better fortune . Those few who were given a Christian education merely drifted away from the norms of Indian cultural pursuits and became more rooted in elite or creole culture . Traditional religious expression , such as public festivals , such as the Indian 's Diwali and displays like Chinese New Year , may be viewed an additional form of cultural resistance . However , it is evident that both cultures ' religious celebrations experienced a process of homogenization to handle the problem of the sheer number of distinct , regional religious celebrations . For example , some large Indian festivals such as Daserah and Holi were collapsed into one , and many Chinese religious celebrations were dropped altogether due to the prominence of Christianity within their religious character . In addition , festivals and rituals that were unpopular with the elites were curbed with the use of draconian measures such as the introduction new laws and heavier policing around festival time . For example , the Tadjas , Muslim religious festivals , were being attended by creoles who were once Muslims themselves , often descended into acts of violence or at least loud shouting and arguments late into the night . The attendance of creoles was greatly despised by Indians Muslims who wanted the Tadjas to remain exclusively Indian . In reaction to the disturbances , colonial authorities introduced laws which outlawed the Tadja festivals or ` intolerable nuisances as they liked to call them . This led to the extinction of the Tadja from the Muslim religious calendar in Guyana . Similarly , Chinese New Year was a festival of great importance to the Chinese ; yet where they would normally prepare for weeks in China , they were only given a few days in Guyana . They still made lanterns for the lantern parade which were a crucial symbol of eminence in Chinese society ; the more ingenious the lantern , the higher the social status of that family . The Chinese also set off a great number of fireworks at New Year , which was a great irritation to the elites . Not surprisingly , in reaction they introduced laws to curb the use of fireworks during Chinese New Year . Surprisingly , Portuguese religious festivals often tended to upset ` certain respectable neighbours . '' One such noisy example includes the use of guns and fireworks during Christmas celebrations to which the cultural elites retorted with ` the use of the sanction of law to curb these practises leading on one occasion to the arrest of several Portuguese revellers . '' Indian , Portuguese and Chinese festivals continued to be celebrated publicly , albeit to a reduced extent . Curiously the Portuguese also maintained quaint Madeiran religious customs in the face of criticism from the Anglo-Creole clergy such as the taking off of hats upon the sound of the Angelus at 0600 , 1200 and 1800 and upon passing a church , thus signifying a proud resistance to elite cultural power . Religious festivals continued to be a starkly apparent challenge to the cultural dominance of the colonial elites and thus were a symbol of cultural resistance and of independent Chinese , Portuguese and Indian identity . It was inevitable that all immigrant cultures would experience some form of cultural change by moving to another country , no matter how capable equipped they were to resist transitions . For example , the Portuguese became successful enough not only to one of the first cultures to step into the gold and diamond industries but also ` Portuguese prospectors and merchants were able to unite with middle-class blacks and coloureds in a campaign for constitutional reform . '' As the Portuguese became more prominent members of Guyanese society , they were forced to interact more with different cultures . Initially they were begrudgingly compelled to conduct business with creole customers in their language to make money thus progressing homogenization between their two cultures . The Portuguese were probably the culture that came closest to creating a society that ran independently from the rest of Guyanese society . They maintained a diet very similar to that found in Madeira , they kept their education within their own ranks and preserved the same family and social structure . Their success was built around separate ethnic institutions -LRB- which other cultures had but to a lesser degree -RRB- which served to separate them from the rest of society . But , ` by the end of the century , Creolese was beginning to break down the barriers which had for so long protected the bastion of Portuguese exclusivity . '' For instance , they took part in cricket with other cultures increasingly had to work with other cultures to further themselves in Guyanese society . They also had to adhere to laws passed against use of guns at religious celebrations and make various other concessions to the colonial elites . The Indians were also fairly successful at protecting their cultural heritage : ` Wherever possible the Indians tried to fall back on their traditional cultural practices . There was very little effort to adopt those of the host society . '' They retained the same diet , refused to allow many of their children to receive an English education , adapted their accommodation to suit match their cultural background as far as possible , -LRB- as did the Chinese -RRB- and maintained the same basic family structure . However , limits on the number of female immigrants had considerable affects on the traditional power of males over their choice of partner ; it allowed rolls of choosing a mate often to be reversed . In addition , although importantly they refused to speak Creolese or English , preventing complete cultural infiltration of their language , they were forced to fuse several different variations of the Indian language to form a practical language that could be spoken by all of the Indian community . This process of homogenization was also necessary to unify religious practise and celebration in Guyana . This signified a loss of a great amount of cultural diversity within their community . Crucially , the Indian caste system , although transported across the Atlantic basically intact , was irreversibly undermined by the practicalities of the plantation . In a slightly more egalitarian sense , those who were good at plantation work were often afforded a greater degree of social mobility . Due to the weakness in numbers of the Chinese community , they experienced the greatest amount of cultural modification . The acute shortage of women caused a crisis in the sustainability of their community and also meant that their women were prepared to accept men from other races and take a greater power in choice of partner . The Chinese often willingly accepted a Christian education that altered not only their religious practises but their language too . Influentially , they managed to set up secret societies and gambling and opium dens that resisted the cultural advances of the elites and creoles . However , by the end of the nineteenth century with the adoption of Christianity and elite intervention , the secret society became extinct within the Chinese community in Guyana . They also maintained the same basic diet but with several exotic additions from creole diets and new fruits and vegetables found in Guyana . Essentially , all three immigrant cultures were affected in some way by the presence of other cultures in Guyana . The Portuguese managed to develop a community that basically was running parallel to the rest of society but was gradually converging culturally with other communities . The Indians were able to put up fairly stiff resistance to the advances of both the cultural elites and the creoles but essentially were forced to adapt to survive the practicalities of living as a large and diverse community in a plantation environment . The Chinese were even less successful at preventing their culture from being influenced by the social conditions in Guyana . It is clear that by the nineteenth century in Guyana , immigrant communities had not changed enough for there to be a completely homogenized society as there were still sharp distinctions between each community . However , immigrant communities were affected significantly enough to claim that Guyana did not experience a pluralized society by the end of the nineteenth century . Instead one may claim that only in the next century when immigrants and creole began forcing their way into government in ample numbers that cultural assimilation and a more unified value consensus would develop on a larger scale . One may point to the 1905 riots which were carried out by and a number of workers -LRB- and creoles -RRB- across the working class cultural spectrum as an indication of the changing cultural situation in Guyana .
