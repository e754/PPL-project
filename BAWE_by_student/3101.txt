There are many businesses that would probably consider themselves to be part of the hospitality industry . The word hospitality conjures up a plethora of different venues that could be included in this category , from a five star , exclusive hotel to a greasy spoon café on the motorway . A definition of hospitality needs to be given in order to consider the argument of Slattery , who believes only money motivates the businesses -LRB- 2002 -RRB- , and Lashley who believes that the guest experience is influenced by their previous experiences in the private domain -LRB- 1999 -RRB- . These sub sectors that many authors have previously split the industry into are the social domain , the private domain and the commercial domain . As one often needs to exchange money in order to receive hospitality now , the main consideration of this essay will be to decide whether this constitutes as hospitality at all , as the exchange of money requires no obligation to return the hospitality . The other form of money exchange to be considered is that between the employer and the employee . This can affect how hospitable the service appears to be . In order to decide on the impact that money exchange has on the hospitality given , the domains of the industry need to be explained , and the ways these domains influence the way the hospitality businesses are run , in order to be seen to be hospitable . A definition of hospitality can be found in many books on the subject of hospitality . Jones -LRB- 2000 , page 1 -RRB- has a basic definition that says hospitality is `` provision of overnight accommodation for people staying away from home , and provision of sustenance for people eating away from home . '' This definition is fairly simple , but captures the essence of the industry . However , it needs to make an implication as to the motive behind the offering of hospitality . This can then distinguish between the commercial and private sections of the industry . Many businesses can be included in this definition , and the Hotel and Catering Training Company includes hotels , restaurants , clubs , pubs , bars , cafes and the private sector to name but a few of the businesses in the industry . According to King -LRB- 1995 -RRB- , hospitality can take place in both the private and commercial domains . The three domains of hospitality , used by many authors to extend their definitions of the industry are the private , social and commercial domains . The domains clarify the reasons that hospitality takes place , and how it takes place . Lashley -LRB- 1999 -RRB- defines the private domain as hospitality that begins at home . Everyone learns to be polite and hospitable themselves in the private domain . Guests are often received into this domestic setting -LRB- Lashley , 1999 -RRB- , and the hospitality that takes place is seen as an act of friendship . There is often a mutual obligation to return the hospitality -LRB- Lashley , 1999 -RRB- , and this is where the social and private domains overlap . According to Lashley -LRB- 1999 -RRB- , this can increase the social standing of the person providing and receiving the hospitality . Lockwood and Jones -LRB- in Lashley & Morrison , eds . 2000 -RRB- state that the private domain is supply led . This is because the hospitality only takes place when the host decides to entertain , and not when the guest decides they want to receive hospitality . The commercial domain deals with all hospitality that is paid for in money exchange , and is demand led -LRB- Lockwood and Jones , 2000 -RRB- , as the hospitality takes place when the customer desires it . Due to this , Telfer -LRB- in Lashley & Morrison , eds . 2000 -RRB- has argued that the term ` commercial hospitality ' is a contradiction in terms , as the guests are n't chosen , and the hospitality does n't take place in the home . She has also written that there is a lack of hospitality in the commercial domain . This is due to her definition of a hospitable person , who is someone who is genuinely concerned for the welfare of the guest , whereas a host is someone who acts in a way to please the guest , but not necessarily for appropriate motives . One considers the idea that a host is more associated with the commercial domain , due to the less appropriate motives of the hospitality given . More often than not the guest is seen as a valued customer , and no particular effort is made to make them feel at home . However , there are exceptions . In the private domain , the host may be entertaining for self-interest , for example to secure a business deal , and this would n't be seen as hospitable -LRB- Telfer , 2000 -RRB- , and in the commercial domain , the host may be genuinely interested in pleasing the guest , and this could be seen as being hospitable . This means that both hospitable and non-hospitable people may be operating in the industry , and so the argument of whether the industry is truly hospitable , depends on the staff involved . The obligation to return the hospitality received is removed in the commercial domain due to the money exchange that takes place . This is the main difference between the commercial and private domains . In the commercial hospitality industry , two types of money exchange take place . Firstly , there is the exchange of money between the host and the guest . This is to pay for the services they have received , and therefore reduces the obligation to return the hospitality -LRB- Lashley , 1999 -RRB- . Returning the hospitality would be seen as the form of ` payment ' in the private domain . According to Lashley this money exchange limits the hospitality that a guest will receive , as the business dictates the style of service , and the choice given to a guest . These would still be chosen by the host in the private domain , but specifically targeted towards the guest in question , and could be changed for other guests . Lashley -LRB- eds 2000 , cited in Slattery 2002 -RRB- recognises that the commercial sector is driven by the `` need to extract surplus value from the exchange '' , but they also consider the fact that there is the opportunity for the host to be hospitable and have the desire to please the guest . This is because the commercial domain has developed from the private and social domains . Historically , it is the private and social domains that came first , when travellers were offered beds for the night , and nobility entertained the villagers . Going back to Telfer 's definition of being hospitable , if the guest has n't been pleased , then hospitality is n't being offered . Slattery -LRB- 2002 -RRB- argues with Lashley that the only reason people are in the hospitality business is to make a profit , just as in any other industry . He therefore believes that the three domain approach is useless in understanding the industry . This suggests that the private and social domains have made no contribution to the way that the commercial hospitality sector is managed . The main motivator of the person in charge of the business is profit , and as this is not the case in the private domain , it makes the links between them expendable . Fearn cited in Mullins -LRB- 1998 , page 34 -RRB- says that `` things that are done to obtain profit '' are the reasons for a business ' existence . This implies that the motive of making a profit is important , but so is the way the profit is made . In the hospitality industry , the profit is made by making the guest feel at home , and being hospitable towards them as this is what they pay for . The argument on this subject between Slattery and Lashley is ongoing . Slattery has been forthright in his disagreement with Lashley , in order to convey the point that business is a cut throat world , where many are out to earn as much profit as possible , often not minding who they hurt on their way . Slattery sees Lashley 's point of view as being very idealistic in the harsh world of business . The second form of money exchange is that between the employer and the employee -LRB- Telfer , 2000 -RRB- . Telfer suggests that the need to make a living is not the only reason for working in the hospitality industry . She puts forward the idea that if the person is a hospitable person , then they may wish to use this trait in order to please the guests , but also earn money from one of their personality traits . Herzberg 's two factor theory , proposes that there are hygiene factors that do n't motivate a person to work , but their absence would demotivate , and there are motivators -LRB- Accel-Team . com , 2004 -RRB- . These motivators include achievement , recognition , responsibility , and interest in the job . This last one links to the suggestion by Telfer that the hospitable person is putting their trait to good use . The others can be obtained by working to please the guest in a genuine way . The recognition can come from guests and employer when a job has been done well , and can lead to achievement and responsibility . These theories consider the prospect that an employee is more that just money minded . Lashley -LRB- 1999 -RRB- states that this trait of hospitableness needs to be developed into the psych of all employees to remove the impression of hosting as opposed to genuine hospitality that is often present in the hospitality industry . This suggests that Lashley recognises the potential for firms to solely concentrate on the profits . An author who disagreed with the idea of Herzberg 's two factor theory was Frederick Taylor . One of the elements of his theory of scientific management included the system of piece rate pay -LRB- Accel-Team . com , 2004 -RRB- . Although this is n't strictly applicable in the hospitality industry , it suggests that the only motivation for workers is the money that they go home with at the end of the day . This would be an ulterior motive for the person working in the hospitality industry , and the employee would be seen as a host , as per the definition of one according to Telfer -LRB- 2000 -RRB- . The three domains of the concept described by Lashley -LRB- 1999 -RRB- are very much linked together . The private and social domains go hand in hand , as when someone offers hospitality in the private context , they are also socialising . These domains rely on each other existing in the way that the commercial domain relies on them both . The roots of the commercial domain are in the private domains , from the days when travellers were offered beds for the night , and then people started to charge them -LRB- Lashley , 1999 -RRB- . Slattery considers the three domain concept to be redundant when thinking of the commercial industry -LRB- 2002 -RRB- . He sees the commercial and private domains to be completely separate entities . This is due to money exchange taking place , and his belief that profit is the main reason for being in business . However , if the private domain did n't exist , then neither would the commercial domain . Slattery is right in the fact that many businesses exist for the purpose of making a profit , but the need to treat the customer well , and carry out the actions that make the profit are as important -LRB- Fearn , 1971 -RRB- . If this was not done well , then the profit would n't be made . Of course the main differences between the commercial and private domains is that money exchange takes place , in two contexts , and so the obligation to return the hospitality is removed . Although the money exchange between the employer and the employee exists , the employee still has to have an interest in the job , for them to be motivated to do it -LRB- Herzberg -RRB- and so although they are being paid , they should still be a good host , and if they possess the trait , then hospitable as well . The money exchange between the guest and business is that that generates the profit . Although this would be seen as an ulterior motive to be providing the hospitality -LRB- Telfer , 2000 -RRB- , it removes the obligation to return the hospitality , which in the private domain , would act as a form of payment . Lashley -LRB- 1999 -RRB- still recognises that the need to make a profit is a motivating factor in the commercial hospitality industry , but he balances this by adding that a certain amount of hospitality has to be given in order to keep the guests that generate the profit .

Yield management is the process of maximising revenue by selling the allocating the right amount of capacity to the right customer at the right time -LRB- Kimes , 1989 -RRB- . This means setting prices according to the demand for a given period . In periods of low demand , customers can take advantage of lower prices , where as when demand is high , higher prices are charged -LRB- Ingold , McMahon-Beattie and Yeoman , 2000 -RRB- . However , it has been well documented that the process of yield management can be perceived to be unfair by the consumer , as they may be paying a different price to another consumer . Yield management is a practice that is fairly widely accepted in the airline industry -LRB- Kimes , 1994 -RRB- . Kimes believes that this is because the practice has been happening for longer in the airline industry . Customers of the hotel industry find it hard to accept the fairness of yield management , as they often can not differentiate the products received by themselves and others who may be paying a different price -LRB- Kimes , 1994 -RRB- . When consumers pay for a product , they have a reference price for that product , which is the price that they perceive to be reasonable for the product they are receiving . This reference price is either gained from previous experience or what other people have paid for the same product -LRB- Wirtz and Kimes , 2003 -RRB- . In a hotel , a guest may compare their room rate with someone else 's . When yield management is practiced , the room rates that people pay may be different , and so the reference price may be lowered for the person who paid more , and they hence perceive the rate they pay to be unfair -LRB- Wirtz and Kimes , 2003 -RRB- . Consumers consider a price to be fair when they feel that the price is reasonable , and that the firm is making a reasonable profit -LRB- Kahnenan , 1986 -RRB- . The price becomes unfair to the consumer when this balance shifts in favour of the firm . However , price increases can be acceptable . Consumers consider that price rises are fair when the costs increase for the firm or the market changes -LRB- Wirtz and Kimes , 2002 -RRB- . Much can be done by firms to increase the perceived fairness of a yield management system . The main way to make the system seem fair is to differentiate the products in the mind of the consumer -LRB- Kimes , 1994 and Wirtz and Kimes , 2003 -RRB- . Restrictions can be applied to the hotel rates in order to reward those that can be more flexible with their dates , by giving them more favourable rates . A study by Kimes -LRB- 1994 -RRB- discovered that consumers felt that some restrictions were fair , as long as sufficient reward was available for the restriction being in place . An example of such restriction would be an advance payment with no or limited refund for cancellations . However , Kimes -LRB- 1994 -RRB- discovered that if too many restrictions were put in place , then they would be considered to be unfair . Another way to differentiate the product is to introduce rate fences -LRB- Wirtz and Kimes , 2003 -RRB- . These fences could include the length of stay in a hotel , or how far in advance the room is booked . The Marriott tests discovered that 87 % of business and leisure travellers felt that their advance booking and purchasing system was fair -LRB- Hanks , Cross and Noland , 1992 -RRB- . Another way to make the price differences seem fair is to frame the lower price as a discount of the full price -LRB- Wirtz and Kimes , 2002 -RRB- . During the study by Wirtz and Kimes , it was discovered that consumers perceived the discount to be fairer than a surcharge that would be charged in times of higher demand . An example of this type of pricing is an ` early bird ' discount in a restaurant , where a lower price is paid if customers eat before a certain time -LRB- Wirtz and Kimes , 2002 -RRB- . Choi and Mattila -LRB- 2004 -RRB- studied the effect that providing information on pricing policies would have on the perceived fairness of yield management . It was discovered that consumers were far more likely to perceive the practice of yield management to be fair if a full explanation was given for the different rates , as it allowed the consumer to help themselves to get a lower rate . If the customer was only given the information that the rates varied , the practice was not deemed as fair , as there was no way of being able to ` work the system ' to get a lower rate -LRB- Choi and Mattila , 2005 -RRB- . By making all their pricing information available , hotels are showing the customer that they are willing to be open about their policies , and are also demonstrating that there is a method behind the rates they charge and that they are not jus randomly generated -LRB- Choi and Mattila , 2005 -RRB- . One disadvantage to the hotel is the marginally increased cost of making the booking when the policy is explained to each customer , as the booking phone call could take longer -LRB- Choi and Mattila , 2005 -RRB- . However , Choi and Mattila -LRB- 2004 -RRB- believe that the benefits outweigh the costs as customers may be lost if they perceive the pricing policy to be unfair . Wirtz and Kimes -LRB- 2003 -RRB- also suggested that the prices of rooms could be `` bundled '' so that the room price itself would be indeterminable . This removes the issue of fairness for the customer , as they are unable to compare the room rate with someone else who may have paid the room only rate . However , this method does n't seem to address the issue of how fair the practice is . Due to the extent of literature on this subject , the fairness of yield management clearly worries those that practice it . However , in the hotel industry , yield management is becoming more acceptable -LRB- Choi and Mattila , 2005 -RRB- . This is backed up by an updated study by Kimes -LRB- 2002 -RRB- . Yield management can only help achieve long term profit if the consumer perceives the process to be fair -LRB- Wirtz and Kimes , 2003 -RRB- . Fair practices also have a positive effect on the service encounter , which will result in better customer satisfaction -LRB- Namasivayam and Hinkin , 2003 -RRB- . There are several ways that managers can make yield management appear to be fair . The products can be differentiated to justify their different prices , and it is important to make the pricing policy very clear to customers so they can make an informed choice . These methods will educate the customer , and hopefully make them more accepting of yield management as a practice in the hospitality industry .
