The analysis of recent banking crises is one of the most interesting fields in international financial macroeconomics due to their negative effects and recurrence , and the difficulties involved in their resolution . Diaz Alejandro -LRB- 1985 -RRB- , when referring to the financial liberalisation processes started in the 1970s , anticipated many of these events with the catchy title `` Good-bye financial repression , hello financial crash . '' Furthermore , during a new wave of financial liberalisation initiated in the 1990s , banking crises have shown more adverse effects when compared with the 1980s 1 . In this context of endemic banking crises a number of interesting questions have been raised among policymakers and academics : What are the causes of such crises ? Which policies should be pursued when a crisis arises ? How can another collapse be prevented once the crisis vanishes ? The Argentine 2001-2 financial turmoil revitalised these questions , exposing new issues due to the severity and extension of the distress , as the Argentine real GDP plummeted around 11 % in 2002 while credit to the private sector suffered a 39.2 % reduction in real terms -LRB- Dec-2002 / Dec-2001 -RRB- . Now the GDP has recovered at an unexpectedly high rate of 8 % in 2003 while the stock of credit to the private sector fell 18.3 % -LRB- Dec-2003 / Dec-2002 -RRB- without clear signals of recovery in early 2004 . The objective of this paper is to deal with the aforementioned questions to shed light to the analysis of modern banking crisis , focusing on the Argentine experience . With this purpose I will first summarise a number of stylised facts on the causes and resolutions of recent banking crises . With the ground set for the analysis of the Argentine case , I will address in detail the characteristics of the Argentine banking system -LRB- henceforth BS -RRB- before and after the threshold generated by the devaluation in January 2002 , including a brief comparison with the experiences of Mexico and Indonesia . The final step is to outline a number of policy recommendations to improve the situation of the Argentine BS . There are two main conclusions in this paper . First , that in the Argentine case credit to the private sector has shown a negative trend even after a pronounced upward trend in the real GDP , yielding what I have called financial hysteresis . Second , that for Argentina and Mexico there is evidence that the GDP Granger causes credit to the private sector , albeit the relative importance of the latter does not return to the pre-crisis level . The remainder of the paper is organised as follows . Section I addresses some stylized facts on the causes and resolution of recent banking crises . Section II introduces the Argentine experience . Section III compares the cases of Argentina , Indonesia and Mexico . Section IV briefly assesses policy recommendations for the Argentine case . Section V presents concluding remarks . There is evidence that countries affected by banking crises have shown an appreciated real exchange rate relative to its average level -LRB- Kaminsky and Reinhart 1999 , p. 485 -RRB- . This appreciation is often due to the upward trend in consumption and investment when agents face optimistic expectations about the economy , for instance after a process of economic liberalisation 2 . In such a situation , the profits of the tradable sector decrease , negatively affecting the quality of banking assets since some borrowers may face strong difficulties in performing their loans -LRB- Goldstein and Turner 1996 , p. 12 -RRB- . In almost all the recent experiences a credit lending boom has precluded the banking crises -LRB- Kaminsky and Reinhart 1999 , pp. 483-4 -RRB- . There are different ways in which this mechanism affects BS vulnerability : -LRB- i -RRB- in the phase of expansion of the economic activity and the lending boom it may be difficult to differentiate between good and bad borrowers -LRB- Goldstein and Turner 1996 , p. 14 -RRB- ; -LRB- ii -RRB- banks may be more lax in measuring risk in a prosperous economic situation -LRB- op cit. , p. 14 -RRB- and -LRB- iii -RRB- there may exist a ` bubble ' in the value of firms ' collaterals . In this context when a shock arises -LRB- e.g. , a sudden stop in external funding -RRB- negatively affecting the economy , the value of the collaterals in the BS may suffer a breakdown , thus affecting the system as a whole . The absence of a solid political and institutional structure has also influenced the likelihood of a banking distress -LRB- Goldstein and Turner 1996 , pp. 21-24 -RRB- . When agents perceive a fragile institutional environment or a weak government they tend to withdraw deposits in the domestic BS 3 . The withdrawal of deposits usually affects the vulnerability of the BS while increasing pressure on the exchange rate . On the other hand , a weak institutional framework reduces the possibility of implementing adequate economic policies when the banking crisis appears . Such vulnerabilities have been extensively discussed in the literature as one of the main factors behind banking woes during the 1980s and 1990s 4,5 . The liberalisation of the BS tends to generate an expansion in the number of banks and the overall availability of credit in the economy . When the process is not correctly monitored , banks may excessively accumulate risk assets thus increasing the system 's overall vulnerability to negative shocks -LRB- Kaminsky and Reinhart 1999 , p. 480 -RRB- . As Mishkin -LRB- 1996 , p. 32 -RRB- points out , the existence of foreign currency-denominated assets and liabilities in the BS may lead to a banking collapse . Under a currency depreciation borrowers may no longer be able to pay loans in foreign currency since most of their assets are denominated in domestic money . It is worth noting that longlasting macroeconomic instability in many developing countries has affected the credibility of the private sector in both , the domestic currency and the BS . In this context , and in order to speed up the development of the financial system in certain countries , monetary authorities allowed taking deposits and credits in foreign currency . Nevertheless , this fact increases overall vulnerability to a banking crisis , since the capacity of the Central Bank to function as a lender of last resort is limited in this context -LRB- Chang and Velasco 1998 -RRB- . As far as agents perceive this BS vulnerability and expect a currency depreciation , a bank run as stated in Diamond and Dybvig -LRB- 1983 -RRB- may arise 6 . As Goldstein and Turner -LRB- 1996 , pp. 18-21 -RRB- highlight , the excessive accumulation of public debt in banks ' assets also affects BS vulnerability . When banks accumulate a high share of public debt , the solvency of the system depends indirectly on the solvency of the public sector . So , when confidence in the sustainability of the public debt decreases the price of public bonds diminishes , thus negatively affecting the BS balance-sheet . In an extreme case , when the government defaults on the public debt the value of the banking assets may collapse 7 . The lack of transparent information in the BS has also been highlighted as an important element in explaining BS vulnerability -LRB- see Goldstein and Turner 1996 , pp. 21-24 -RRB- . Many experiences have shown that prudential measures in terms of liquidity requirements were not in accordance with the best international standards . The lack of transparency in banks ' information affects the capacity of customers and monetary authorities to accurately determine each particular bank 's situation , thus enlarging the asymmetric information problems in the system and the likelihood of a crisis . During the 1980s a typical external shock which ended in banking crises in developing countries was the sudden reduction in the terms of trade -LRB- see Kaminsky and Reinhart , p. 485 -RRB- . This shock negatively affects the profitability of the tradable sector thus diminishing its capability to perform its loans . The evidence also suggests reductions in the terms of trade have been more severe in those countries with a relative concentrated exports structure -LRB- Goldstein and Turner , p. 9 -RRB- . As the international financial markets have become more interconnected , especially during the 1990s , it has been relatively easy and cheap for international investors to mobilize financial resources among national States . This mechanism has had an important role in the genesis of banking crises , being more important in those countries with not well-developed capital markets 8 . The contagion mechanism usually works as follows : when a large international investor suffers a direct loss in a particular country he tends to reduce overall exposure in countries with similar characteristics , thus precipitating the likelihood of a banking crisis . It has been also argued that crises may be driven by sudden changes in expectations , under what is known as the multiple-equilibria scenario , thus yielding a capital outflow and in turn triggering the crisis 9 . Either the intervention or the liquidation of banks presents controversies in the economic literature . Scott -LRB- 2002 , p. 14 -RRB- highlights that this policy helps in reducing the BS uncertainty , enhancing the natural adjustment process of the system . On the other hand , Hawkins and Turner -LRB- 1996 , p. 37 -RRB- argued that this policy might have a negative effect since it may undermine the public 's confidence in banks , provoking a generalised bank run . As a rule of thumb , and considering the Argentine case , I sustain that avoiding banks ' liquidation during a crisis seems to be an adequate policy when the distress has systemic characteristics . In the economic literature exists an interesting discussion around the moral hazard problems involved with the deposit insurances -LRB- DI -RRB- -LRB- see Scott 2002 -RRB- . However , Hawkins and Turner -LRB- 1996 , p. 45 -RRB- sustain that in most banking experiences , monetary authorities explicitly or implicitly implement a DI to avoid a negative perception of the BS by depositors . They also argue that the DIs have shown important differences in the sources of funding and the amounts covered . Furthermore , in this regard Hawkins and Turner -LRB- op cit . -RRB- sustain that it is possible to design a DI which tends to reduce the moral hazard problem by limiting the amount granted and government participation in their capitalisation . As Del Villaral . -LRB- 1997 -RRB- point out it is often necessary to provide fresh funds to banks during the crisis . It is worth highlighting that the capitalisation process might be broadly characterized as public - or market-biased , the latter being recommended since involves lower public intervention . During and after banking crises monetary authorities tend to update their prudential regulations taking as a benchmark the norms of the Basel Committee -LRB- Del Villaral . 1997 , p. 22 -RRB- . However , as will be addressed when discussing the Argentine case , a proper regulatory setup may not be a sufficient condition to avoid banking crises . Since banking distresses are often associated with financial problems by the borrowers of the system , monetary authorities often apply different measures to alleviate their situation -LRB- Huerta and Urriza González 2000 , p. 25 -RRB- . Such measures often involve : -LRB- i -RRB- technical and financial support , -LRB- ii -RRB- reductions in interest rates , -LRB- iii -RRB- extension of loan maturities , -LRB- iv -RRB- differential exchange rate for those debtors with loans denominated in foreign currency or -LRB- v -RRB- a swap of debt denominated in foreign currency to domestic currency at a preferential exchange rate 10 . However , worth noting is that such policies often involve important fiscal costs and distorting incentives -LRB- e.g. , moral hazard problems -RRB- , the general criteria being to avoid public intervention as much as possible -LRB- Huerta and Urriza González op cit . -RRB- . Following the classification of financial systems stated in Demirguc-Kunt and Levine -LRB- 1999 -RRB- the Argentine BS could be characterized as bank-based , in line with the evidence of most mid-income countries . It is worth highlighting that the importance of the Argentine BS increased markedly after the implementation of the Currency Board -LRB- CB -RRB- regime in April 1991 11 . This CB regime positively reverted agents ' expectations about the future pattern of the economy , leading to a very fast growth of the BS -LRB- Heymann 2000 , pp. 34-42 -RRB- . Argentine monetary authorities also promoted important transformations in terms of the infrastructure and regulatory frameworks of the system during the 1990s -LRB- Fanelli and Pradelli 2002 , p. 22 -RRB- . From a regulatory viewpoint , the Central Bank of Argentina designed a strict regulatory framework to situate the BS near the best international standards . Furthermore , in order to improve confidence in the BS the Central Bank added a plus over the Basel Committee 's recommendations from 8 % to 11.5 % with respect to the system 's liquidity requirements -LRB- Fanelli and Pradelli 2002 , p. 30 -RRB- . By the same token , to strengthen credibility in the banks , portfolio risk requisites were imposed far above the international standards . All these elements generated a steady upward trend in the credits and deposits of the private sector during the 1990s -LRB- see Figure 1 -RRB- . This process also increased the financial deepening of the economy which , in turn , enhanced GDP growth rates . Figure 2 shows the evolution of credit to the private sector as a percentage of GDP -LRB- i.e. , an index of the financial deepening -RRB- which rose from 17.9 % in 1993:1 to 26.3 % in 1999:1 . However , this positive trend was reverted in 1999 as a consequence of the economic recession initiated by 1998:4 . It is worth highlighting that the macroeconomic environment during the 1990s showed an important correlation with the dynamics of the BS . In fact , between 1990 and 1997 the Argentine economy grew at an average rate of 5.1 % , positively affecting BS expansion -LRB- see figure 2 -RRB- . Nevertheless , when contrasted with international evidence , the Argentine BS never reached the levels of those countries with a similar GDP per capita -LRB- see Table 1 -RRB- , despite growing markedly during the 1990s . This point has been raised by Heymann -LRB- 2000 -RRB- , explaining that the Argentine economy 's instability before the 1990s encouraged the private sector to use informal sources of funding . The evidence discussed previously suggests that reverting this phenomenon -LRB- i.e. , to swap from informal to formal financial sources -RRB- is a process which takes long periods even when the economy shows a stable macroeconomic environment . The 1990s were also characterised by the existence of important external shocks upon the Argentine economy -LRB- Calvoal . 2003 -RRB- . These shocks , even though significant in the short-run , founded a solid BS which helped in reducing the magnitude of their effects . For instance , the 1994-5 crisis in Mexico markedly affected the Argentine economy with a reduction of about 20 % in total deposits in only a few months -LRB- Figure 1 -RRB- . Nevertheless , the strong regulatory framework of the BS and the adequate policies implemented by the Central Bank relaxing short-run liquidity requirements while reallocating banks in financial distress , helped in recovering the deposits much above the pre-crisis level -LRB- see Figure 1 -RRB- . The other important shocks faced by the economy , the 1997 and 1998 crises in Asia and Russia , respectively , and the devaluation of Brazil 's currency in early 1999 did not have important negative short-run effects on the BS . However , they raised an adverse perception on the soundness of the BS mainly due to the existence of two vulnerabilities : -LRB- i -RRB- From 1993 on , the Argentine government allowed the dollarization 12 of credits and deposits in order to expand the size of the BS . The basic idea at that time was to show , on the one hand , the government 's commitment to the exchange rate regime , perhaps ` doubling the bet ' as Heymannal . -LRB- 2002 -RRB- highlighted 13 . On the other hand , it was probably thought that an increase in the size of the BS would have been necessary to support additional expansions in the activity level . Figure 3 shows the marked dollarization of credits and deposits in the economy during the 1990s . Worth noting is that this upward trend was more pronounced by the end of 2001 , as economic agents perceived the inevitability of the currency devaluation . In such a situation , banks biased their portfolios towards those assets denominated in US$ dollars while the public swapped their deposits from Arg $ to US$ dollars to preserve the purchasing power of their respective assets under a devaluation scenario . -LRB- ii -RRB- The second vulnerability was the upward trend in the share of loans to the public sector in the total by the end of 1998 . As a consequence of the economic recession initiated by the third quarter of 1998 government revenues plummeted , forcing the public sector to borrow from the domestic BS 14 . This phenomenon was particularly important by late 2001 when the credit to the public sector rose to almost 50 % of the total -LRB- see Figure 4 -RRB- . Mainly due to the aforementioned vulnerabilities , the Argentine BS collapsed with the declaration of the public debt default in late December 2001 and the subsequent devaluation of the peso in January 2002 . From early 2001 , when economic agents realized the important constraints faced by the economy in defending the currency board regime , the BS suffered a constant withdrawal of deposits -LRB- Frenkel and Damill 2003 -RRB- . In this context the flows of new credits were contracted while agents indebted in US$ dollars cancelled their liabilities to protect themselves from a currency depreciation . Figure 5 shows the negative trend in credits and deposits to the private sector from the beginning of 2001 and the threshold generated by the currency depreciation in January 2002 . It is worth highlighting that the government decided in early 2002 to convert all the deposits denominated in US$ dollars to pesos at the exchange rate Arg $ 1.4 per one US$ dollar , whereas credits were converted one-to-one in what it is known as the asymmetric pesification , generating a different path in the evolution of credits and deposits -LRB- see Figure 5 -RRB- . This asymmetric pesification has been extensively criticised from an academic and a policy-making viewpoint as a consequence of the important distortions generated upon the banks ' balance sheets 15 . However , the dilemma faced by the government at that time was not simple . On the one hand , BS depositors highly intensified their criticism against the banks and government when it was officially decided to freeze deposits . Furthermore , this economic policy was fundamental in the resignation of the former President De La Rua in December 2001 . In this critical situation the new transitory government headed by Mr Duhalde had to deal with the inevitability of the currency depreciation and the discontent of depositors . In such a context the new government decided to pesify deposits at the same exchange rate after the devaluation in January 2002 -LRB- i.e. , Arg $ 1.4 per US$ dollar -RRB- , not to worsen the relationship with the depositors . On the other hand , the high dollarization of credits raised another dilemma between the BS and the private sector , since currency devaluation could imply the collapse of foreign currency-indebted firms 16 . In this context , and in order to avoid a further economic activity level deterioration , the government swapped US$ dollar loans to pesos at one US$ dollar equal to one Arg $ 17 . The overall effects of the policies implemented in Argentina between 2001 and 2002 in a context of a massive capital outflows from the banking system , led to one of the most important credit crunches suffered by any modern economy and , certainly , the most painful banking crisis experienced in Argentina . Figure 6 shows the shrinking of the credit to the private sector / GDP from early 2001 to late 2003 , with the ratio falling from 24.1 % to 10.1 % . Furthermore , the reduction in credit to the private sector when measured in real terms was about 50.4 % -LRB- Dec-2003 / Dec-2001 -RRB- . Another important issue arises when considering the evolution of deposits and credits separately -LRB- see Figure 5 -RRB- . Noteworthy is that while deposits start growing after July 2002 , credits show a negative trend only slightly reverted by the beginning of 2004 . This fact adds an interesting insight to the banking crisis , as the economy started to recover by the second quarter of 2002 . Furthermore , we can ask why the credits did not respond to the upward trend in the economic activity . To further illustrate this point in Figure 7 I show the evolution of credits to the private sector and GDP separately . Figure 7 clearly shows the important correlation until the first quarter of 2002 when this relation seems to no longer hold . To explain , I suggest as a hypothesis that when the BS collapsed in 2001-2 , the private sector swapped the way in which it funds current transactions from formal to informal sources . Such a swap seems to generate a financial hysteresis phenomenon 18 since the contraction in the credit to the private sector continues even though the economy recovers a steady growth path . Moreover , this fact suggest that the recovery of credit to the private sector is time-demanding , since agents must learn about the new economic environment , while regaining confidence in the banking system before credit channels return to a sound position . At this point , it seems interesting to ask if other recent financial crises showed the same pattern in the relationship between the evolution of GDP and credit to the private sector in the aftermath of economic distress , the objective of the next section being to compare the cases of Argentina , Indonesia and Mexico . Considering Table 2 , it is worth highlighting the strong impact that these financial crises have had in each country . In all these cases credit to the private sector tends to diminish while credit to the public sector moves in opposite direction . Particularly notorious is the case of Indonesia , since credit to the public sector rose from less than 1 % of GDP before the crisis up to well over 20 % after 1998 . A similar pattern was shown by the Mexican economy , although credit to the public sector never reached above 10 % of GDP -LRB- see Table 2 -RRB- . This upward trend in the proportion of credit to the public sector / GDP seems to be associated with each government 's active intervention to avoid a further collapse of the system . In fact , as Del Villaral . -LRB- 1997 , p. 74 and 1998 , p. 33 -RRB- point out , both the governments of Indonesia and Mexico pursued active public policies to bailout the private sector and the BS . In the case of Argentina , the government issued different bonds which now figure as assets in banks ' balance sheets to compensate banks for the costs due to the asymmetric pesification . As mentioned previously , in all the cases credit to the private sector as a share of GDP shrunk markedly . In fact , in Mexico the ratio was almost 35 % in 1994 , and even after many years of economic recovery this ratio is located around 10 % . The case of Indonesia shows the same pattern , since this ratio shrunk by more than half -LRB- from 53.6 % to 24.9 % -RRB- between 1998 and 2002 . In Argentina this ratio was also cut by half when comparing the pre-and post-crisis situations . In all these cases credit to the private sector measured as a share of GDP does not show evidence of returning to the pre-crisis level even though the economy shows strong signs of recovery . To further address this issue I will develop an interesting insight to the problem , asking if GDP impulses the evolution of credit to the private sector , a question which , as far as I know , is not addressed in the literature about banking crises . Considering Table 3 , a number of issues are worth emphasising . In the cases of Argentina and Mexico when the full sample is considered , the evidence suggests that the GDP Granger causes credit to the private sector . When sub-samples are considered the evidence also indicates causality in this direction but , since the number of observations is limited , the results are taken only as indicatives . The case of Indonesia shows a slightly different result . In fact , the causality seems to be in both ways , particularly after the financial crisis in late 1997 . Up to now I have addressed the vulnerabilities and factors that triggered recent banking crises as well as the main characteristics of the Argentine bank distress . In the remainder of the paper I will stress a set of policy recommendations based on the previous sections ' discussions and the analysis stated in Acosta Ormaechea and Todesca -LRB- 2002 -RRB- and Fanelli -LRB- 2002 -RRB- to address two main issues : -LRB- i -RRB- how to improve the current situation of the Argentine BS and -LRB- ii -RRB- how to avoid the vulnerabilities observed before the BS collapse . The role of financial institutions is , essentially , of arbitrage between investors and savers which is carried efficiently only if economic agents find a stable economic framework . So , the consolidation of a stable macroeconomic and institutional setup seems to be the first step towards the consolidation of the Argentine BS . In this sense the efforts of the Argentine government should be concentrated on restoring the economic and institutional basis of the economy , markedly affected after the collapse in 2001-2 . From a macroeconomic perspective the economic authorities should promote : -LRB- i -RRB- a stable exchange rate , -LRB- ii -RRB- a conservative monetary policy to avoid inflationary pressures , -LRB- iii -RRB- a controlled fiscal budget and -LRB- iv -RRB- the renegotiation of the defaulted public debt 19 . One of the characteristics of the Argentine crisis is that it not only involved the collapse of the economy but also important political and institutional distress . In this area , the challenges seem to be : -LRB- i -RRB- improving the quality of Argentine institutions -LRB- ii -RRB- redesigning the bases for a new contractual arrangement in the banking system not based on foreign currency-denominated assets and liabilities . One of the most important tasks for the Central Bank is to accommodate its role and the BS to the new economic environment generated after 2001 . In particular , it seems necessary to deal with the following issues : -LRB- i -RRB- Determining the norms that regulate the role of the Central Bank as a lender of last resort , a function that it did not have under the currency board regime 20 . -LRB- ii -RRB- Reviewing the regulatory and supervisory norms implemented during the CB regime to determine which modifications are needed . -LRB- iii -RRB- Following Hawkins and Turner -LRB- 1996 , p. 45 -RRB- , it seems interesting to consider a deposit insurance that is mutually funded by the public and private sector -LRB- i.e. , to avoid moral hazard problems -RRB- up to a limit which should be defined to enhance the confidence of depositors in the Argentine banking system . -LRB- iv -RRB- Establishing the situation of each bank in order to identify those institutions with financial problems , with the aim of designing individual rather than general solutions . -LRB- v -RRB- In those cases where the banks are suffering liquidity problems the Central Bank should encourage shareholders to capitalise their institutions . -LRB- vi -RRB- In Argentina , public banks have played an important role in attending the financial requirements of small - and medium-sized firms often exposed to the credit rationing phenomena 21 . In the future , the management of the public banks ' portfolios should follow strict operational rules to avoid the negative results experienced by public banks during the 1990s 22 . Another key step in recovering the Argentine BS is strengthening the confidence of depositors and borrowers . Improving the transparency in the BS will permit reducing the system 's information problems thus enhancing public confidence , which allows for a better diversification of risks . In particular , even though during the 1990s a number of improvements have been developed regarding transparency , the Argentine BS is still far away from the best international standards -LRB- Fanelli 2002 -RRB- . In the short run , the Central Bank may help in improving the situation by imposing the submission of a set of periodical information about the situation of each bank to their respective customers . As discussed previously the problem of dollarization was , perhaps , the main factor in explaining the collapse of the Argentine BS after January 2002 's devaluation . For this reason , it seems necessary to impose restrictions on foreign currency-denominated assets and liabilities . In this regard , Krugman -LRB- 1999 , p. 42 -RRB- directly suggests discouraging firms from taking foreign currency-denominated debt 23 . However , it seems to me that such a kind of ` radical ' policy may not be optimal , considering that certain firms have a high proportion of assets denominated in foreign currency -LRB- e.g. , those firms linked to foreign trade -RRB- . A proper measure would be to impose restrictions mainly to firms with a high proportion of non-tradable activities . Furthermore , as a rule of thumb , the restrictions may be set when a particular firm shows a ratio of sales abroad -LRB- i.e. , exports -RRB- to total sales below some threshold . The benefit of this mechanism is that the firms with a high share of tradable production will be able to find funding sources under better conditions -LRB- e.g. , lower interest rates -RRB- , reducing the costs of capital and positively affecting domestic production while the currency risk is properly hedged . The last two decades have been characterised by more integrated financial markets but also by the recurrence of deep banking crises , particularly among developing countries . In such a context the existence of macroeconomic , institutional and banking vulnerabilities has enhanced the likelihood of a banking collapse , an external factor often being the element that triggered the crisis . Which policies must be implemented once the crisis arises ? In this paper I addressed some insights as a rule of thumb to answering the question : -LRB- i -RRB- avoiding the liquidation of banks during the crisis since it may worsen public perception on the general health of the banking system , -LRB- ii -RRB- avoiding public intervention as much as possible and -LRB- iii -RRB- enhancing the regulatory and supervisory frameworks . Albeit painful , the Argentine experience raised new issues about the likelihood and effects of banking collapses . On the one hand , an adequate regulatory framework being a necessary condition for a sound banking system seems not to be sufficient for avoiding a crisis . On the other , a proper banking system depends greatly on the soundness of the economy as a whole . I also addressed an interesting insight to the problem of the Argentine banking crisis which , to my knowledge , has not been addressed in the literature on the topic . As a consequence of the BS collapse the private sector swapped its sources of funding from formal to informal , generating what I called financial hysteresis : once the economy recovers the GDP growth path , credit to the private sector still decreases . I argued that such a situation might be driven by a private sector lack of confidence towards the banking system . Interestingly , I also found evidence in Indonesia and Mexico of the reduction of the ratio of credit to the private sector / GDP even after many periods of sustained economic growth . However , considering the Granger causality test I found that the GDP Granger causes credit to the private sector in the cases of Argentina and Mexico , which may suggest further increments in this aggregate . A set of policies which may help in both restoring the current situation of the Argentine banking system and avoiding a future crisis was discussed , summarised thus : -LRB- i -RRB- encouraging the stability of the macroeconomic and institutional frameworks -LRB- ii -RRB- promoting an adequate regulatory and supervisory system according to the new economic environment , -LRB- iii -RRB- enhancing banking system transparency , providing all relevant information to their customers , -LRB- iii -RRB- avoiding the dollarization of the banking system and -LRB- iv -RRB- reducing loans to the public sector as much as possible . Valuable extensions of the paper are postponed for further research . It seems interesting to explore in detail the aforementioned hypothesis of financial hysteresis in the aftermath of a banking crisis . A first step in this direction should be to extend the international evidence as well as the samples considered for the analysis , attempting to shed light on the factors that would imply such behaviour . A considerable effort has been made in economics in the last thirty years with the aim of answering important issues regarding monetary policy : why an economy ends up with positive inflation rates without any remarkable positive effect on output , and why it seems difficult to achieve a final outcome with zero inflation and , at least , the same output level . To answer these questions the seminal papers by Kydland and Prescott -LRB- 1977 -RRB- and Barro and Gordon -LRB- 1983a -RRB- -LRB- henceforth KP and BG , respectively -RRB- first developed an analytical framework based on the so-called time consistency problem -LRB- TCP -RRB- . The policymaker faces a trade-off between unexpected inflation and output but , as the public incorporates that , the expected inflation is set at a sufficiently high level so that the government 's best response is to validate this inflation and the output is not affected . Many suggestions have been assessed about how to improve this suboptimal equilibrium . For instance , KP and BG highlight that by imposing strict rules on the policymaker the TCP might be eliminated . Rogoff -LRB- 1985 -RRB- , on the other hand , points out that by delegating monetary policy to a conservative central banker the TCP is reduced . Furthermore , this framework gives analytical support to the explanation about why modern Central Banks -LRB- CB -RRB- tends to be more independent 1 . The paper has two main objectives : firstly , address critically the TCP in monetary policy ; secondly , answer why by delegating monetary policy to an independent CB it is possible to reduce the TCP . Some evidence is also introduced to overview to what extent independent CBs have reduced the TCP from an empirical perspective with emphasis on the UK experience after 1997 . One of the main conclusions of the paper is that the TCP seems to arise only under institutional setups where the policymaker faces some degree of discretion . In this case , the policymaker faces a marginal benefit of generating inflation which exceeds the marginal cost when the inflation rate is announced below the equilibrium level , which may induce him to not validate such announcement . Furthermore , developing this particular insight to the problem I show that delegating monetary policy to an independent CB helps to reduce the TCP since , for a given marginal benefit , the marginal cost of generating inflation rises compared with the pure discretionary case . The remaining of the paper is organized as follows . Section I critically addresses the TCP in monetary policy . Section II assesses how an independent CB helps to reduce the TCP . Section III analyses some empirical evidence emphasising the UK case after 1997 . Section IV presents concluding remarks . The TCP 2 in monetary policy arises when certain decisions -LRB- forming part of an optimal plan -RRB- at an initial date are no longer optimal at a later date , even though the information set between periods has not changed -LRB- Blanchard and Fisher 1989 , p. 592 -RRB- . Kydland and Prescott -LRB- 1977 -RRB- -LRB- KP -RRB- first explained the TCP the monetary authority may suffer when conducting monetary policy . In particular , KP points out this problem considering a situation where the policymaker optimises a social objective function in each period , in a world where agents do not have mechanical ad hoc expectations upon past prices -LRB- op cit. , p. 478 -RRB- . A further refinement of this original idea was introduced by Barro and Gordon -LRB- 1983a -RRB- -LRB- BG -RRB- in an attempt to include a theory of expectations formation -LRB- op cit. , p. 592 -RRB- . In the remainder of the section , a detailed and formal explanation of the TCP in monetary policy mainly based on the BG contribution will be critically addressed . The notation used for the explanation is borrowed from Walsh -LRB- 2003 -RRB- which , in turn , slightly modifies the BG notation . As suggested previously , the TCP involves the incentives the policymaker faces when deciding economic policies , the way in which the public forms expectations and their respective interactions within a particular institutional arrangement . Worthwhile emphasising is the importance of the institutional framework in which the monetary authority determines economic policies -LRB- e.g. , growth rate of money supply -RRB- . As will be clear later in this section , the existence of the TCP implicitly requires that the policymaker have a certain degree of discretion when conducting monetary policy . Or , put another way , an institutional arrangement which does not commit the policymaker to strictly follow particular monetary policy rules . This paper critically addressed the TCP that a policymaker may suffer when conducting monetary policy , defined as a situation where his optimal decision is not the same before and after the public set the expected inflation , even though the information set between periods remains equal . I explained that behind the TCP remains the assumption that an institutional setup exists which allows the policymaker to behave with discretion . In other words , the policymaker is able to optimise his objective function in each period . Since he faces a trade-off between unexpected inflation and output and the public knows such incentive , the latter sets the expected inflation at a level at which the marginal benefit equals the marginal cost that the government faces for generating inflation . Thus , the economy ends up in a suboptimal equilibrium with positive average inflation without any effect on output beyond the natural rate . How this suboptimal outcome could be improved was extensively addressed . First , the expected social loss under discretion was compared with that given by the ` optimal rule ' : a fully credible institutional framework which commits the government not to depart from the announced inflation rate while still allows it to manage monetary policy to reduce the effects of supply shocks . However , it was pointed out that such a framework could not be very useful in practice since it is difficult to anticipate the contingencies that an economy may suffer . Nowadays , delegating monetary policy to an independent CB tends to be one of the most popular ways in which modern States try to overcome the TCP , analytically based on the Rogoff -LRB- 1985 -RRB- contribution . With a particular insight to the problem I argued that in this case the TCP is reduced since the policymaker faces a higher marginal cost of generating unexpected inflation when comparing with the pure discretionary situation . Furthermore , I explained that the TCP is not fully solved unless the inflation aversion of the central banker tends to infinite , an undesirable fact since it increases the stabilisation bias . A brief insight to the empirical evidence was made , showing a negative correlation between the degree of CB independence and either the average or variance inflation , without negative effects on economic activity . An overview on the effects of the change in UK monetary arrangement after May 1997 is also in line with these results . However , I sustained that this evidence should be taken as indicative , since other factors not directly related to CB independence may affect the final outcome . Put another way , empirical evidence suggests that the TCP tends to be partially reduced with more independent CBs but the difficulties in measuring CB independence and the existence of other factors which affect the likelihood of inflation suggest that that this evidence is only indicative . The concept of Credit Rationing -LRB- CR -RRB- has a long-lasting tradition in economic theory 1 . The importance of the CR phenomenon arises because it assumes that prices may not adjust in credit markets in the way assumed by the conventional auctioneer mechanism . When an excess demand for a good prevails in any market , prices will increase until such level as demand equals supply . On the other hand , if an excess demand for credit exists but the interest rate charged on loans does not increase to achieve equilibrium , a CR situation may occur . The negative effects of such a situation on the efficient allocation of resources can be interpreted from both a micro and a macro perspective . Projects with high social return could not be undertaken and agents may not be able to smooth intertemporal consumption properly when credit is rationed -LRB- Jaffee and Stiglitz 1990 -RRB- . On the other hand , fluctuations in the availability of credit could induce macroeconomic cycles as well as distortions in the monetary policy transmission affecting the real output of the economy -LRB- McCallum 1991 -RRB- . The existence of CR has created an active debate as well as the construction of different theoretical frameworks to explain it . In the early 1950s , Paul Samuelson affirmed that '' -LRB- ... -RRB- non-price credit rationing was entirely inconsistent with profit-maximization bank behaviour -LRB- ... -RRB- '' -LRB- Baltensperger 1978 , p. 171 -RRB- . Starting with Hodgman -LRB- 1960 -RRB- , a number of interpretations have been addressed to demonstrate that CR is consistent with competitive markets and rational behaviour . In particular , Hodgman -LRB- op cit. , p. 259 -RRB- indicated that the primary issue in his work was to address the question of CR avoiding non-competitive market structures or exogenous limits on interest rates 2 . By the same token , Jaffee and Modigliani -LRB- 1969 -RRB- asked if it is rational for a bank to limit the credit below the level demanded by borrowers when the interest rates and the amount of loanable funds are chosen according to profit maximization rules . However , although these frameworks properly addressed credit markets ' particular characteristics , they failed in constructing a consistent framework -LRB- Jaffee and Stiglitz 1990 , p. 853 -RRB- . Hodgman -LRB- 1960 -RRB- was unable to derive a non-monotonic loan supply function dependent on the interest rate , and CR was finally an ad hoc consideration . Jaffee and Modigliani -LRB- 1969 -RRB- `` implicity '' assumed the banks ' capacity to differentiate among borrowers and a positive and monotonic relationship between banks ' expected profits and the interest rate charged on loans 3 . Furthermore , they finally associated CR with institutional restrictions -LRB- Jaffee and Modigliani op cit. , p. 860 -RRB- . After Akerlof 's -LRB- 1970 -RRB- seminal paper , the imperfect information assumption was incorporated as a key element to explain CR . The first paper to incorporate this assumption was Jaffee and Russell -LRB- 1976 -RRB- with Moral Hazard considerations . Furthermore , Stiglitz and Weiss -LRB- 1981 -RRB- emphasized Adverse Selection problems . Nevertheless , there still remains an important debate about the empirical importance of CR as a consequence of Imperfect Information problems 4 . In this paper I will critically address the principal features of the Jaffee and Russell -LRB- 1976 -RRB- and Stiglitz and Weiss -LRB- 1981 -RRB- models to explain how the Imperfect Information assumption helps answer the question about CR . Following these frameworks , an empirical analysis will be provided to determine the CR 's possible impact on the Argentine economy . The principal conclusion of this paper is that the Imperfect Information assumption helps explain CR via the presence of a non-monotonic relationship between bank 's expected profits and the loan interest rate . The empirical evidence on the Argentine case suggests that CR is a feature on those less-collateralized borrowers between 1996 and 2001 . The remainder of the paper is organized as follows . Section I introduces the definition of CR and Imperfect Information . In section II Jaffee and Russell -LRB- 1976 -RRB- and Stiglitz and Weiss -LRB- 1981 -RRB- are developed and discussed . Section III addresses a framework proposed by Berger and Udell -LRB- 1992 -RRB- to test the empirical evidence on Argentina , and Section IV concludes . Following Jaffee and Stiglitz -LRB- 1990 -RRB- I will refer to Imperfect Information as opposed to Symmetric Information , to a situation in which '' -LRB- ... -RRB- borrowers and lenders do not have equal access to all available information -LRB- ... -RRB- '' -LRB- op cit. , p. 840 -RRB- . Asymmetric information is considered a particular case of Imperfect Information , with two different cases to be discussed in the paper : Moral Hazard -LRB- Hidden Actions -RRB- and Adverse Selection -LRB- Hidden Information -RRB- . Moral Hazard refers to a situation whereby one part of an economic contract is unable to observe the actions carried out by the other part , and which may negatively affect the interest of one of the agents involved . Adverse Selection arises when trading decisions of one individual depend on privately-held information which adversely affects uniformed agents -LRB- Mas-Colellal . 1995 -RRB- . The definition of CR includes a broad range of alternatives -LRB- See , Keeton 1979 , Jaffee and Stiglitz 1990 -RRB- . Following Keeton -LRB- op cit. , p. 9 -RRB- , there are two types of Credit Rationing : Type -LRB- 1 -RRB- refers to a situation where an individual receives a smaller-than-desired loan at the market interest rate ; Type -LRB- 2 -RRB- occurs when some firms or agents receive loans while others , identical from the lender 's viewpoint , do not . Hereafter , CR will refer to the type -LRB- 2 -RRB- stated above , i.e. an equilibrium situation since both , lenders and borrowers , are maximizing profits -LRB- that is , there is no economic rational incentive to depart from this situation -RRB- . The first theoretical model which includes the Imperfect Information assumption to explain CR was Jaffee and Russell -LRB- 1976 -RRB- . The presence of Moral Hazard behaviour by a group of borrowers who are indistinguishable ex ante by banks , in the form of an incentive to default on the loan , explains why banks may decide not to increase the interest rate above a certain level . In this situation it is possible to get an interest rate maximising bank 's profits below the level at which the loanable funds market clears . On the other hand , Stiglitz and Weiss -LRB- 1981 -RRB- emphasised the Adverse Selection problem . In such a case , banks know that a positive relationship exists between the interest rate they charge on loans and the risk of each project . As the risk of the borrower 's pool has a negative effect on the banks ' expected profits , and banks are not able to distinguish among the risks of an individual project , they may decide not to increase the interest rate above certain level . Again , it is possible to get an interest rate which maximizes banks ' expected profits below the clear-market level . Nevertheless , in the credit markets the interest rate is not the only contract dimension under consideration -LRB- see Bester 1987b , p. 888 -RRB- . In particular , banks intend to reduce information problems through collateral requirements as well as borrowers ' credit histories or minimum income certificates . In this situation , the explanation of CR via Asymmetric Information problems may not hold anymore 18 . In particular , Bester -LRB- op cit. , pp.887-888 -RRB- argued that in the credit markets there is a self-selection process whereby only low-risk borrowers accept high collateralized loans , thus avoiding asymmetric information problems . The feasibility of eliminating all the information problems through these `` screening devices '' could be criticised because it rests on the assumption that borrowers differ only in a limited dimension of characteristics -LRB- e.g. risk -RRB- 19 . Following Stiglitz and Weiss -LRB- 1992 , pp.162-163 -RRB- , I sustain that some degree of residual uncertainty after collateral is a typical feature of debt contracts , which implies that collateral is not able to eliminate all the credit markets ' information problems . It has also been addressed that in some developing countries the sources of CR may not be directly related to Imperfect Information problems since borrowers may have a '' -LRB- ... -RRB- great deal of information about relevant borrower characteristics -LRB- ... -RRB- '' -LRB- Gosh et al. , 2000 -RRB- . There is extensive literature on the empirical evidence on CR . On the one hand , Sofianosal . -LRB- 1990 -RRB- found evidence of CR in those loans not agreed under commitment in the US between 1973 and 1987 , while Ghoshal . -LRB- 2000 -RRB- agreed with the CR hypothesis in less-developed countries . In this paper I have critically addressed the two principal frameworks which incorporate the Imperfect Information assumption to explain CR . While Jaffee and Russell -LRB- 1976 -RRB- , the first model to introduce this hypothesis , emphasised Moral Hazard problems , Stiglitz and Weiss -LRB- 1981 -RRB- developed a model which relies on Adverse Selection considerations . Once these models were introduced , I answered the question as to how the imperfect information assumption explains CR in a way which synthesised both frameworks . As lenders are unable to distinguish from an ex ante basis between the risk and behaviour of an individual borrower , a non-monotonic relationship exists between expected lender profits and the interest rates charged on loans . In this situation , the interest rate which maximises the expected lender profits may differ from the market-clear rate . If there is an excess demand for loans at the former interest rate , a situation of CR may exist . Nevertheless , the CR hypothesis as a consequence of imperfect information was criticised by different researchers , particularly from an empirical point of view . It has also been emphasised that the existence of contractual mechanisms such as collateralised loans may help to differentiate borrower characteristics in a way which avoids asymmetric information problems . I have argued that differences among borrowers are not unidimensional , and a limited set of contracts may not generate a symmetric information scenario , since some degree of uncertainty after collaterals is a probable outcome in credit market contracts . I also indicated that empirical CR measurements rely on important difficulties , since they involve ex ante data , implying that supporting the hypothesis of CR or not might be sensitive to the way in which the data and the empirical tests are constructed . To conclude the analysis , two empirical tests on CR were introduced , adapting the Berger and Udell -LRB- 1992 -RRB- framework to the Argentine case . The evidence , although not conclusive , suggests the existence of CR among those less-collateralised borrowers in Argentina between 1996 and 2001 . The phenomenon of Currency Substitution -LRB- CS -RRB- has a long tradition in economic literature 1 . The term refers to a situation where a foreign currency is utilized as a medium of exchange by agents in a particular economy -LRB- Calvo and Végh 1996 , p. 153 -RRB- . This phenomenon is particularly important when the domestic currency is not fully credible , probably associated with a lack of confidence in the monetary policies applied in the country . If CS holds , agents will swap the domestic for foreign currency as the domestic exchange rate tends to depreciate . This approach has been utilized in the empirical literature on CS , where a demand for money function is augmented with the introduction of the exchange rate -LRB- Arango and Nadiri , 1981 -RRB- . The econometric testing of CS with a vector error correction -LRB- VEC -RRB- model has been applied in different developing countries with diverse results . For instance , Pozo and Wheeler -LRB- 2000 -RRB- did not find evidence of CS in Singapore against the US dollar . On the other hand , Bahmani-Oskooee and Techaratanachai -LRB- 2001 -RRB- and Prockal . -LRB- 2003 -RRB- found evidence of CS in Thailand and Latin-America ,2 respectively , also against the US dollar . The objective of this paper is to test the CS phenomenon in Argentina with a VEC model utilizing a simple open-economy money demand function . As usual in the literature on VEC models , the technique developed in Johansen -LRB- 1988 -RRB- and Johansen and Juselius -LRB- 1990 -RRB- to determine the cointegration relations is applied . As non-normalities in the residuals of the first vector autoregressive -LRB- VAR -RRB- formulation were found , the nonparametric test proposed by Bierens -LRB- 1997 -RRB- is also introduced . Both approaches confirmed the existence of one long-run relation in the demand for money . Finally , the results obtained through the estimation of the cointegration vector suggest the absence of the CS phenomenon in Argentina from 1991:04 to 2003:12 . The paper is organized as follows . Section I introduces the data . Section II discusses the demand for money equation . Section III explains the VEC formulation and the cointegration analysis . Section IV briefly describes an extension to analyse the model 's short-run dynamics . Section V concludes . The CS phenomenon may have important implications in terms of monetary policy since it implies a swap from domestic to foreign currency as the exchange rate tends to depreciate . A number of empirical studies of CS in developing countries have been carried out through VEC models , obtaining results which support or not the CS hypothesis . To analyse the CS phenomenon in Argentina between 1991:04 and 2003:12 , I followed the VEC approach addressed in Prock et al -LRB- 2003 -RRB- . In order to test for cointegration the well-known Johansen technique was followed , but several issues arose when this method was implemented . In particular , as a possible consequence of the Argentine data 's high fluctuations , the residuals of the first VAR formulation showed strong evidence of non-normalities . Since these non-normalities affect the inference in the Johansen technique , I incorporated 10 dummies in the VAR specification to obtain normal multivariate residuals . Nevertheless , inspection of the residuals of each variable indicated the possibility of further improvements in the specification . Concerned about this problem , the non-parametric test of cointegration proposed by Bierens -LRB- 1997 -RRB- was introduced . Furthermore , since the results of both approaches indicated the existence of one cointegration relation and the multivariate residuals behaved approximately normally , I continue with the Johansen technique to estimate the cointegration vector . Curiously , the normalised cointegration vector obtained with the Johansen technique does not show evidence of CS in Argentina for the period 1991:04 to 2003:12 . Several improvements in the monetary policies implemented during the 1990s may explain the differences with the results obtained by Prock et al -LRB- 2003 -RRB- with data from mid-1980s . Interesting extensions of this paper are postponed for further research . Firstly , the estimation of the cointegrating vector with the nonparametric approach of Bierens -LRB- 1997 -RRB- and the comparison of the results with those obtained by the Johansen technique . Furthermore , as a consequence of the high instability of the Argentine data , nonparametric methods for analysis cointegration seem to be an interesting avenue for future research .
