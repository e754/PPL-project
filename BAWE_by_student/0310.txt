First of all divisions had always been present in Black politics and they did not just suddenly emerge in the 1960 's , they did however became more pronounced and much wider during this decade . This essay shall examine some of the main causes of divisions within black politics and shall look at the consequences that these divisions had on black politics in the 1960 's . Since the 1950 's black politics had begun to gain more momentum and strength in the face of white supremacists and despite segregation . With the emergence of Reverend Dr. Martin Luther King as the leader of the Southern Christian Leadership Conference , black politics started progressing in leaps and bounds . By the beginning of the 1960 's King had become the personification of the Civil Rights Movement for many blacks , and whites . However it was also at the beginning of the 1960 's that another black man had begun his rise to prominence within the black ghettos of the North ; Malcolm X was soon to be recognized as King 's biggest political opponent . King and Malcolm have often been marked out as the biggest division in black politics , but this essay shall study the other divisions that emerged in the 1960 's as well . Nevertheless the divide between King and Malcolm can not be ignored . The divide stemmed mainly from a disparity in their ideologies . King was from a black middle class family in the south ; his father was a prominent force in campaigning for black rights and the Reverend at the Ebenezer Baptist Church . Martin received a full education , including university , and at nineteen was ordained . This middle class background was common to many black political leaders , though not all of them made it public knowledge . In contrast Malcolm had been raised in absolute poverty in the north . He had lost both parents by age twelve , and lived in foster care before moving to Boston to live with a half-sister . By this time Malcolm 's resentment of the white man had begun to grow and he loved the black ghettoes of Roxbury and Harlem . Malcolm was a hustler , drug pusher and user , as well as the leader of a burglary gang before ending up in prison at the age of 21 . It was during this time in prison that Malcolm converted to Islam , which he saw as ` the natural religion for the black man ' . It was this conversion to the Nation of Islam that was to set Malcolm on the path towards a place in black politics . First and utmost King was a dedicated Christian , a fact that some think King placed after his commitment to Civil Rights , though in reality they were wholly joined together . King believed whole heartedly in integration and in the ` American Dream ' . King believed in justice and love , with , as James H. Cone identifies , justice as his starting point , King wanted to tell the Negro to have `` the moral courage to stand up for their rights '' . The Montgomery bus boycott opened King 's eyes to the national problem of racism and eventually the global problem , so his ideas on love and justice began to evolve . Love symbolized integration and justice desegregation . Love was also the foundation of King 's philosophy of nonviolence . Eventually King and the SCLC were set ` to redeem the soul of America ' . King 's ideals developed continuously throughout his life but he had some basic principles to which he adhered to and which became groundwork of his future . His most publicized belief was nonviolence and nothing was more important to King , politically or religiously , than nonviolence , but he also held other fundamental values . He believed that Negroes must `` maintain a continuing sense of dignity and self respect '' , King wanted Negroes not to see in black and white but in the essence of equality , in that everyone is a human being , and an American citizen , despite color . King also placed great emphasize on hard work and the philosophy that `` God helps those who help themselves '' , he also connected this idea with the need for strong leadership in the Negro community . Finally this was entwined with King 's desire that Negroes uphold and improve their moral standards . Malcolm X came to represent the utter opposite of Martin Luther King , but in some ways the two men were similar . Like King , Malcolm was deeply religious ; he was just Muslim not Christian . After his release from prison Malcolm 's reputation within the Nation of Islam grew and the Prophet Elijah Muhammad became like a father figure to him . The two founding principles on which the Nation of Islam was based were a reaffirmation of black history and culture , and the rejection of the white man and his values . Before his break with Elijah Muhammad in 1964 , Malcolm became the main spokesman for Elijah and he was often described as the ` number two man ' within the group . In complete contrast to King 's theory on integration , Malcolm wanted segregation . Malcolm believed that for blacks to gain a total feeling of self respect they must separate themselves from the white man . Like King , Malcolm believed that blacks needed to gain more respect for themselves but he thought this was possible without whites . The black population of America needed to get back in touch with it 's roots and embrace their ` blackness ' , hence the symbolic name X , which stood for Malcolm 's African name that he would never know . After his break with Muhammad , Malcolm 's ideas evolved and his focus was more political than religious , but he always held very strong views , most of which clashed with King 's . So by 1964 there was one major division in the Civil Rights Movement , between those that followed King and those who followed Malcolm X . It is important to remember that while the Civil Rights Movement had never been entirely united before , this divide between two of the most well known black men in America , was of huge significance . The media were able to portray Malcolm X as a ` racist demagogue ' and King as ` the white man 's choice for the black man ' . Both men held a great influence over blacks in America but were unable to find a common ground to hold them together and fuse two very different ideologies into one , united philosophy . Other organizations like SNCC and CORE were also divided over the best route to follow and after the assassination of Malcolm X more divisions within the Civil Rights Movement began to emerge . One of the main threats to the Civil Rights Movement came from the idea and rise of ` Black Power ' from 1966 onwards . The phrase was originally coined by Stockley Carmichael at a rally in Mississippi , but it spread rapidly and had many connotations . King and other Civil Rights leaders realized that as the new Chairman of SNCC , Carmichael had made an unfortunate choice of words , and Black Power could only mean trouble . For most whites , Black Power simply meant black supremacy , even though Carmichael and Rick tried to say this was n't the case , ` Black Power is not Black supremacy ; it is a united Black voice reflecting racial pride in the tradition of our heterogeneous nation ' . King liked the idea of blacks gaining more self respect and pride but the phrase ` Black Power ' seemed to promote militant nationalists as it was too ambiguous in its meaning . The Old Guard integrationists immediately rallied together to denounce Black Power , Wilkins of the NAACP said that ` No matter how endlessly they try to explain it , the term `` black power '' means anti-white power ' . Black Power led to the final separation of the left and right wings of the Civil Rights movement . Indeed SNCC and CORE had never been further from King and the Old Guard . The distance between the different groups of the desegregation movement had been growing larger for a while and the rise of Black Power really wedged them apart . CORE had moved from its early integrationist policies to black nationalistic ideologies , where it no longer believed in integration or allowing white supporters in the organization . The withdrawal of white liberal support also occurred in SNCC and the public image of both organizations changed rapidly . The media portrayed the leaders and members of SNCC and CORE as racist demons inciting violence and riots among the black youth . SNCC was becoming increasingly radical as it made a sharp turn towards nationalism , which was epitomized by the creation of a separate political party . Carmichael led the newly formed Lowndes County Freedom Organization , more commonly known as the Black Panthers . The Black Panthers came to symbolize dangerous and militant black nationalists , not just in the eyes of whites but also in the eyes of many blacks . The Old Guard also disapproved of CORE and SNCC new resolutions on self defense , as the Old Guard had always remained convicted to a policy of nonviolence . During the late 1960 's Black Power seemed to mean whatever was convenient for the person promoting it , even Nixon rode on the popularity of Black Power . Over time Black Power became increasingly connected to socialism , leading to the emergence of a new ideology for young blacks and a development in King 's own theology , which led to more splits within the Civil Rights Movement . King refused to condemn the phrase ` Black Power ' and instead tried to make it more positive instead of the nationalistic rhetoric that many people saw it as . King 's stance on this issue caused tension between himself and other leaders in the Old Guard , like Wilkins , Young and Randolph . James H. Cone believes that it was around this time that King had become disenchanted with whites and more receptive to separatism , and as King 's speeches indicate his philosophy was evolving . The war in Vietnam really highlighted and confirmed King 's new ideas . King and the SCLC had originally tried to avoid the issuing of commenting directly about the war ; even though King disapproved he was encouraged not to say anything either way . Support for the Johnson Administration was high and SCLC did not wish to alienate its white , liberal allies . King decide to take a firm stand on Vietnam early in 1967 after completing his book -LRB- Where Do We Go From Here : Chaos or Community ? -RRB- , `` never again will I be silent on an issue that is destroying the soul of our nation '' . The war in Vietnam became the focus for many of Martin 's speeches in his last year , and he linked war with the poverty and racism in America . The war in Vietnam really marked the final change in King 's ideology and highlighted divisions among King 's supporters . SCLC and NAACP were both worried that King 's criticisms of the war would alienate the federal government and white financial supporters . The NAACP and the Urban League viewed the war as an opportunity to get in with the Johnson Administration , whereas SNCC denounced the war as early as January 1966 . Eventually , due to mounting pressure , SCLC came out officially against the war . King had decided that in order for America to progress internally the war in Vietnam had to end and this spurred him into action . King began to make anti-war speeches and take part in the anti-war marches that were being organized . This led to Civil Rights leaders arguing more between themselves , those like Rustin and the NAACP took a pro-Johnson stance whereas Bevel and Wachtel sided with King . Black Power and the Vietnam War really exposed the disunity not just within the Civil Rights Movement but also within the Civil Rights groups themselves . By the end of 1966 SCLC 's leaders and staff were tired and demoralized . Bevel left SCLC to devote himself to the anti-war movement and , after the departure of several other executive staff , SCLC was beginning to flounder . Other organizations like SNCC and CORE were also beginning to die . Black Nationalism and rioting and increased white repression coupled with the failure of the Johnson Administration and the Vietnam War was taking its toll on all factions in the Civil Rights Movement . By the spring of 1957 SCLC was immobilized by organizational and financial problems . SCLC 's influence in the north was still weak and many leaders felt that King was too preoccupied with the war and not focusing enough on the Civil Rights Movement . The 1960 's had seen the Civil Rights Movement divide quicker and stronger than ever before . King and Malcolm X marked the first big division that would lead to many other divisions in the following years up until the death of King and the eventual disbandment of the Civil Rights Movement .
Many historians and Che Guevara himself have recognized that there were two stages to the Cuban revolution ; the insurrectionary stage and the post insurrectionary stage . The first phase was armed action lasting to 1 st January 1959 ; and after that the political , economic and social transformation of Cuba comprised the second stage . This essay shall assess and evaluate the first stage . There are two ways to begin an assessment of Che Guevara 's contribution to the insurrectionary phase of the Cuban Revolution ; the first is by studying what Che actually did during the insurrectionary phase of revolution and what he accomplished , in other words was he a success ? The second approach is to examine what Che meant to Cuba and the revolutionary forces during the insurrectionary phase . Through these two approaches an evaluation shall be made of Che Guevara 's contribution to the insurrectionary phase of the Cuban revolution . The main area of concern when discussing Che Guevara is problem of objectivity and impartiality , and a fair conclusion has tried to be drawn from the evidence collected . The historian Richard Harris once wrote ` revolutionaries are not born , they are made ' , this statement has never been truer than with the revolutionary Che Guevara . As a child and teenager Ernesto Guevara never appeared to be the man he would later become . He was plagued by asthma his whole life , though as many historians and biographers agree this could account for Ernesto 's strong desire to prove himself as capable as his peers . He competed in football and rugby and has always been described as an ` adventurer ' . Still despite this daring and competitive character Ernesto never had any strong political beliefs till much later in his life , though his sense of the injustices of the world began to develop much earlier . Ernesto became fascinated by injustice when he took his now infamous trip around Latin America on a motorbike with Alberto Granados , in 1952 . This trip opened his eyes to the fate of many Latin Americans and marked his first step into the revolutionary world . Ernesto traveled extensively round Latin America after completing his medical degree in 1953 and his political ideas developed rapidly during this time , especially after he reached Guatemala . He supported the new President Jacobo Arbenz when the government expropriated land belonging to feudal estates and the United Fruit Company to give to the Guatemalan peasants ; he also supported the government treatment of the indigenous peoples , though at this time he was still not Communist . It was the CIA sponsored invasion of Guatemala that pushed Che into his belief in armed resistance . As the invasion force pushed into the capital Che tried to persuade arms to be given to the masses to support ` their revolution ' but this failed and Che got his first glimpse of ` Yankee Imperialism ' and its power over Latin America . Due to his support of the government Che was now a wanted man whose convictions and beliefs were growing stronger by the day . He was soon forced to flee and he arrived in Mexico in 1954 . Che 's arrival in Mexico would be were his real career as a revolutionary began . Che 's meeting with Castro in Mexico launched Che into the world of a revolutionary and a guerilla . His political beliefs were now firmly entrenched in his mind and he would often espouse them with a complete and utter honesty that horrified Fidel . During their time in Mexican jail , Fidel would passionately deny his Communist affiliations , while Che could n't compromise his beliefs by denying them . Che 's ability as a guerilla soon became apparent ; at the completion of training his instructor Colonel Bayo singled Che out as having ` excellent discipline , excellent leadership abilities , and physical endurance excellent ' . Che was given responsibility of training recruits at Fidel 's ranch , which was purchased specifically for purpose of guerilla training . It was during this training period in Mexico that Ernesto was given the nickname ` Che ' as an affectionate reference to the fact he was Argentinean . Over time Che was given more and more responsibility by Fidel as his abilities were recognized ` Che had that quality of personal magnetism that attracts and inspires the loyalty and devotion of others ' . It can be argued even now that Che 's strict ideas on guerilla training and warfare had already helped Fidel 's revolutionary force , but Che 's real potential was seen after the landing in Cuba . Fidel 's planned invasion was a disaster in which Che was actually wounded twice , though this has only added to the myth surrounding him . Fidel and his remaining revolutionary force retreated to the mountains and Che 's real contribution to the Cuban revolution began . Che was promoted through the guerillas till he reached the highest position available , that of comandante , he possessed boldness , determination and a strict sense of discipline that was crucial in the continuation of Fidel 's guerilla warfare . When in 1957 Che was given command of his own unit he soon proved he was worthy of the task , his forces soon controlled much of the Sierra Maestra , and the guerrillas had managed to defeat Batista 's best efforts to dislodge and destroy them . It was during this period of guerilla warfare that Che wrote his most well known book ` Guerrilla Warfare ' which was published in 1959 . This book is now required reading for any up and coming revolutionary . It has to be argued that if were not for Che would Fidel have succeeded in his guerrilla war ? Che 's forces were involved in the most crucial part of the insurrectionary phase of the revolution , the battle fought in Santa Clara in December 1958 . Che arrived in the city as the head of his guerrillas plus the students and peasants that had joined his convoy along the way . Che and his rebels managed to capture a government train filled with 400 reinforcements for the army . The train was surrounded and derailed and the troops forced to surrender , causing the troops already defending the city to dissolve into panic . Without this decisive action on the part of Che Guevara the battle in Santa Clara could have been lost and the guerrilla forces under Che could have been destroyed . On the 1 st January 1959 it was confirmed that Batista had fled and Fidel and his guerillas had won their battle to liberate Cuba , the insurrectionary phase of the Cuban revolution was over . So the first part of the Che 's contribution to the Cuban revolution can be drawn to a close and the question of whether he was a success can now be answered . Che Guevara 's participation in the Cuban revolution contributed directly to its success . The Che shown above is the ` insurrectionary theorist , strategist and tactician ' , who went on to utilize his skills in other revolutions . Is it possible that without Che , Fidel would have simply been the leader of another failed rebellion in a struggling Latin American country ? It is possible . Without Che who would Fidel had appointed as a comandante ? Would Fidel have been able to find another man as completely loyal and devoted to him as Che ? When the triumphant Fidel rode into Havana on January 8th his brother Raul was on his right and Che Guevara was on his left , thus symbolizing the high regard that Fidel had for his compañero . Che contributed an enormous amount to the revolution , but it was n't all about what he did , there was also another side to Che that contributed to the insurrectionary phase of the revolution . There is ` Che the guerrilla hero , the martyr and myth ' . Even if it can be argued that Che 's legend did n't really become global till after his death , during the insurrectionary stage of revolution the myth surrounding Che had already begun to grow . Che lived in the true revolutionary way ` todo o nada ' , ` all or nothing ' . The fact that Che has become such a martyr his that he was n't afraid to die for his beliefs ` In the arduous profession of the revolutionary death is a frequent occurrence ' . Che 's personal ideas on what a revolutionary was continue to add fuel to the legendry fire engulfing his name . Che was a prolific writer and his diaries and essays have provided future revolutionaries with guidelines , instructions and beliefs on how to become the ` twenty first century man ' . Che 's idea of this new man was a theory quite closely related to his ideas on guerilla warfare ; he believed there would be a new generation of men who would be willing to sacrifice themselves for the good of Latin America ; through armed struggle . Che 's essay on ` Socialism and Man in Cuba ' truly expresses Che 's deep belief in his ideas , ` There is no life outside the revolution . In these conditions the revolutionary leaders must have a large dose of humanity , a large dose of a sense of justice and truth ' . Ernesto ` Che ' Guevara quickly moved from being just a revolutionary to becoming a symbol for revolutionaries , a process that accelerated after his sudden and suspicious death . So what did he mean for the Cuban Revolution ? His constant literary and oratorical attacks against US imperialism showed how important the future of Cuba was to him and how important the future of Latin America was . Che firmly believed that `` Yankee Imperialism ' was responsible for many of the problems faced by Latin America and other third world countries ; he also believed that armed struggle was the only way to combat this growing threat . Che 's personal characteristics are also crucial to his myth and why he meant so much to Cubans . When Che was proclaimed by Fidel to be ' a Cuban by birth ' the people were able to see for themselves how much Che meant to their leader and how much he had contributed to a revolution in a country that was n't his own . Che 's capacity for personal sacrifice and the way he stayed true to his all his beliefs endeared him to the people as a real hero ; someone who would fight against the injustices in the world no matter where these injustices were present . ` Che 's exceptional devotion to the realization of his ideals was truly heroic ' . Even if Che 's political beliefs , morals and ideals are not in accordance with your own it is hard to deny the impact that he had as a revolutionary and a symbol . Ernesto ` Che ' Guevara made a huge and heroic contribution to the Cuban Revolution , both through his actions and his meanings . He was a true revolutionary . He never compromised his ideals ; he was loyal to the end ; he committed himself wholly and truly to his cause . Che was also distinct from other revolutionaries by his lack of desire to advance politically ; he was loyal to Fidel even if they disagreed later in their lives . Che Guevara emerged as a hero of the insurrectionary phase of the Cuban revolution and it he will now remain a hero for many years to come . ` His life was the personification of revolutionary idealism and practice , and his historic struggle continues to be an inspiration to those who fight against social injustice and oppression throughout the Third World ' .
In 1963 Martin Luther King declared his dream of a racially integrated , non-discriminatory , American society . Some historians may argue that three centuries before , this ideal had been a reality ; others may argue that this dream never was and never will be . This essay will discuss and examine the origins of racism and slavery in colonial America and shall attempt to determine which came first in this imperative historical debate . ` Perhaps no single institution has shaped the character of American history as much as slavery and no single feature of thought has been as important as white racism . ' It is important to remember that Africans were not the first peoples in America to be enslaved ; the Native Americans became the first servants or slaves used by the colonists . Many problems occurred with the use of Native American slaves or servants . The colonists found them poor workers and unsuited to the lifestyle , also diseases brought over by Europeans made the Natives , weak and many died . For this reason white indentured servants were sent over from Europe to replace the Native workers . In the early 17 th century the number of white indentured servants immigrating to the colonies had begun to decrease . Political and civil disturbances in England between 1640 and 1740 augmented the supply of white servants . The colonies had been trying to encourage immigration for some time before Africans began to arrive . However many problems arose with the indentured servants . Indentured servants could only be kept in employment for a limited amount of time , which was normally about four to five years . After this period of employment was over , the workers were meant to receive a small plot of land to farm . The main difference between the white and black servants was that the white servants had rights and were accepted by society , these were the two key reasons as to why slavery was to become a more popular option than white labor . The first Africans arrived in Virginia in 1619 , but did they come as slaves or indentured servants ? It is commonly believed by historians that the first 20 Africans in America came as indentured servants , like the white servants . An important aspect of this essay shall be to determine how or if the word Negroe became synonymous with the word slave ? On one side of the argument are historians of the Progressive and Marxist strains . They believe that slavery in colonial America developed because of important economic factors , and that racism was a result of slavery . However , in opposition to this argument are the historians who believe ` Blacks had been the subjects of discrimination and demeaning treatment from the outset and slavery then became another manifestation of race prejudice . '' I shall scrutinize both points of view and establish my own opinion in answer to the question . The former of these two points stresses how slavery developed because of important economic factors and not because of racist attitudes . When the colonies began in America , there were two main objectives that needed to be attained for success to be assured . The first of these was how to secure an adequate supply of labor , and the second was how to establish and maintain a degree of social control necessary to assure the rapid and continuous expansion of their capital by the exploitation of that labor . So as the colonies grew , and the number of immigrants decreased the easiest solution to the labor problem was to use Africans . Historians that consider economic factors for slavery developing as true , cite the fact that if slavery developed because of racist attitudes why did n't it become a legal institution till after 1660 ? When Africans first arrived they were indentured servants , like the whites and some historians argue that they were treated identically to the white servants . Life was tough for all servants and the Africans lack of rights and freedom was not unusual , but to begin with Africans did not have to spend their lives in servitude like slaves did . The case of the African , Anthony Johnson is often used by historians to illustrate how Africans could succeed in colonial America alongside the whites . Anthony Johnson came to the colonies as a black indentured servant , when he had completed his work period he was given land and ultimately ended up owning is own servants , some of which were mixed race . Eventually though the Africans ceased being servants and became slaves in the colonies , so how did this come about ? '' The colonists soon began to believe that African workers were much more economically viable than their white counterparts . Certain ideas , like the suitability of Africans to work the land began to develop within the colonies , which was often used as a justification of African labor . The colonists realized that they could keep the Africans from having rights or land , as the Africans did n't speak the language and did n't even understand what was happening to them . Africans were easier to get hold of than white servants , as the Africans were kidnapped and forced to sail to the colonies , whereas most white Europeans were given the choice to go . As J. Those first few lines of the novel contain some of the most critical clues needed to decipher The House of the Spirits . The beginning suggests that the writings of a female called Clara are important to the narrator but it is only by carrying on with the story that the reader can truly understand how significant the female word is . Clara first began to write when she ceased to speak after her sister 's death , from this point so early on in the novel writing becomes a momentous symbol for many things . It symbolizes female creativity , female resistance to patriarchal domination , female solidarity and love . The idea of writing is present and continuous throughout the novel , what is of added importance is that in The House of the Spirits the focus is on female writing . Allende has always tried to break down gender stereotypes through her novels , and The House of the Spirits really set the precedent for what Allende would produce later in life . Allende broke the traditional barrier of having a dominant male narrator and a passive female voice in the background . Women and the women 's word are the focus in this novel and Allende seems set to prove that the women 's word is a strong as a man 's . Alba 's dominant narrative controls and guides the story from the start , though other female characters aid her in this . Clara 's notebooks form the basis for the telling of the story and the importance of the notebooks reflects the value of Clara in the story . Clara occupies a magical place within the story , a space between the real and the spirit world , but also a place where she can never be ignored or forgotten . Even after her death Clara 's spirit manages to exist through time and transcend the human and spirit world boundaries to aid Alba and forgive Esteban Trueba . The importance that Allende allocates to Clara in the novel is mirrored by Clara 's own dedication to writing . Clara 's writing is constant in the story even though numerous events occur that could stop it . The constant moving between houses ; the births of her children ; an earthquake and even the rage of her husband do not stop Clara 's desire to write . Clara 's determination and dedication to writing contribute to the idea that writing brings coherence . Clara 's notebooks are Alba 's primary witness to the remarkable lives of her family and are perfectly ordered and understandable , indeed Clara refuses to let names be used more than once as ` repeating the same name just caused confusion in her notebooks that bore witness to life ' . As the reader begins to realize the importance of writing for coherence and understanding another female writer emerges . Clara 's daughter Blanca begins her own form of ` private writing ' through letters to her mother . Blanca is lonely , isolated and miserable and her correspondence with her mother , every day , is a source of happiness and love . These letters are evidence of how writing can be an act of love . At the end of the novel Alba uses these letters to write her own story and they help Alba acquire an understanding of her past that had been missing . The idea that looking at the past can help comprehend events in the present and preserve the future is an idea that progresses through the narration , both Alba 's and Esteban 's . It is important to remember that Alba is not the only narrator in the novel . Though Alba 's female narrative is dominant throughout the story , Esteban Trueba 's narrative contribution plays a significant role in helping the reader to understand that writing is an act of love . Esteban begins his narration in a strong but rather defensive tone , and he seems to feel the need to justify his actions ` I 've been a good patron ; there 's no doubt about it ' . As the story progresses the reader can sense a change in the tone of Esteban Trueba 's narrative as Esteban is forced to confront himself and he can finally see himself in a true light . ` Writing , in fact , rather than speaking , gives him the opportunity to confront his violent behavior ' . By the end of the novel Esteban Trueba can clearly see his appalling behavior towards the tenants of Tres Marías ; Clara ; Blanca ; Pedro Tercia and any one else whom he dislikes has eventually brought about his own downfall . By the end of the story Trueba needs forgiveness , he is a lonely , shriveled and broken old man who ends up begging for help from a women in order to have Alba returned to him . As Trueba 's power weakens throughout the novel his discourse mirrors this . At the beginning his voice is powerful by the end it is disjointed , confused and almost pathetic , but through the writing of the truth has come Trueba 's forgiveness . Once again , the narrative of Esteban Trueba shows that writing is an act of love ' it brings coherence and a release from pain and loathing ` as my grandfather slowly lost the rage that had tormented him throughout his life , she appeared as she had been at her best ' . ` Isabel Allende 's life and fiction form an inextricable bond , woven together with textured strands of love , violence , politics , adventure and death ' . As writing is an act of love for Allende then it seems obvious that her work should be so intertwined with personal experiences . The character of Clara in The House of the Spirits is based on Allende 's own grandmother , Isabel Barros Moreira , a strong woman who had a taste for the magical and the spiritual . Esteban Trueba 's character was also fashioned from reality with Allende 's grandfather , Augustín Llona Cuevas being the inspiration . Allende 's grandfather represented the traditional , conservative and patriarchal society of Chile but at the same time Allende held him on a pedestal of respect and affection . Often readers of Allende can become confused over were reality ends and fiction begins , but this blurring of the boundaries merely reinforces the idea that Allende writes from love and with a passion for understanding . Allende once said that writing the House of the Spirits was ' a way of drawing out all the sadness that had built up inside , of trying to share the painful experience ' . Allende writes from love to help bring an understanding to people and she carries this idea through her characters in The House of the Spirits . Allende uses Clara 's notebooks to help bring coherence and understanding to Alba . Clara wrote to help clarify events in a way that could not deceive her like the mind often can . Clara also wrote as a way of taking a fresh look at things , ' I write , she wrote , that memory is fragile and the space of a single life is brief , passing so quickly that we never get a chance to see the relationship between events : we can not gauge the consequences of our acts ' . Like Clara , her notebooks seem able to defy time and the odds . Even during the ` infamous pyre ' that destroys all Jaime 's , Nicolas , Trueba 's and Uncle Marcos 's books somehow Clara 's notebooks miraculously escape and as Linda Gould Levine says ` this reflects Allende 's view of the potency of women 's words , words that can not be silenced by oppressive regimes ' . The fire of books is another example of Allende 's desire to make the women 's word as strong as the man 's . Allende 's use of a typical literary device to add believability to her story ; the fact that events that may otherwise seem to bizarre to be true have been written and preserved through time adds an air of authenticity to a story and proves ` the presence and influence of Clara 's notebooks that bear witness to life ; they are not only the most important source for shaping Alba 's narrative , but are also symbolic in a broader sense of the power of the written word and it 's ability to mould and transform life ' . The ability of writing to bring release from pain and loathing , and its capacity to change life is finally proved once and for all at the end of the novel . The everlasting Clara who though unable to save her daughter from rape is able to convey to her the strength to continue and to ` write a testimony that might one day call attention to the terrible secret she was living through , so that the world would know about this horror that was taking place parallel to the peaceful existence of those who did not want to know ' . Once again the reader can see the importance of the written word of the female , as without Clara 's mystical encouragement and her physical notebooks Alba may never have had a chance to write her story . For Alba , the planning of the story gave her a release from the terrible ordeal she was living through and when she was released her writing managed to change her life . By looking back at the past Alba finds out about the rape of Pancha Garcia and is able to see how things link together through time , ' I am beginning to suspect that nothing that happens is fortuitous , that it all corresponds to a fate laid down before my birth ' . Eventually through her writing Alba is released from her hate and need for revenge , ' I seek my hatred and can not seem to find it . I feel it 's flame going out as I come to understand ' . Alba 's realization that revenge is not the answer is something Allende has always espoused , ` we ca n't torture the person who has tortured us because then it 's a never-ending chain of fear and hate and anger and violence ' . Along with Alba 's change of heart at the end of the novel comes her final release from the bonds of pain and loathing , ' I have to break that terrible chain . I want to think that my task is life and that my mission is not to prolong hatred but simply to fill these pages ' . Again this idea of breaking a cycle of hate , anger and violence is another ideal that Allende holds close , and an ideal she expresses through her writing , ' I write for those who want to share the obligation of building a world in which love for our fellow men ... will prevail ' . Isabel Allende writes with love and for an understanding , her ideas are mirrored through her female protagonists . Clara writes for clarity and coherence , Blanca writes for love and Alba writes for the release from the bonds of pain and loathing .
` To bind up the nations wounds ' and ` to do all which may achieve and cherish a just and lasting peace ' . This was how Lincoln viewed the aim of reconstruction at the end of the civil war , but there were many different beliefs as to the aim of reconstruction and an equal amount of ideas of how best to achieve these aims . Reconstruction can said to have begun in 1863 with Lincoln 's Emancipation Proclamation and it is widely agreed that reconstruction formally ended in 1877 with the inauguration of Rutherford B. Hayes ; however these dates are debatable . Reconstruction after the civil war took numerous forms and occurred in a variety of ways all of which are still widely examined , discussed and revised by historians today . In this essay the focus shall be on Presidential Reconstruction and its failure , from Lincoln 's early attempts in 1863 to Johnson 's policies in 1865 . As the Unionists began to gain more and more territory in the war , the issue of what to do when the war ended became of implicit importance . The right of a state to leave the Union had never been mentioned in the Constitution so neither the President or Congress had any guidelines to follow when dealing with the legality of secession . President Lincoln believed that the project of reconstruction lay firmly with the Presidential Executive and not Congress . As early as 1862 Lincoln began devising and implementing policies to deal with reconstruction . The first problem that Lincoln faced was how to form a loyal government in a Confederate state , so he began to implement his Ten Percent Plan . Lincoln 's plan of amnesty and reconstruction required just ten percent of the qualified voters of 1860 to take an oath of allegiance ; they were then allowed to set up a state government which the President would recognize . However Republicans believed this policy to be far too lenient and that the problem of reconstruction belonged to Congress , so they passed the Wade-Davis Bill in opposition to Lincoln 's plan . This Bill made it much harder to be considered a loyal citizen and only after a majority had taken an oath of allegiance could delegates elected to state , where they must take the iron clad oath . This second oath was important as it restricted many more Southerners from voting than Lincoln 's plan did . The Wade-Davis Bill also had another important requirement that was notably absent from Lincoln 's proclamation ; the state had to abolish slavery as a condition of re-entry into the Union . As Congress passed this Bill on the last day in session Lincoln was able to get rid of the Bill with a pocket veto , but the Bill did mention a second problem that faced Lincoln : ` the future of slavery and the negro ' . Lincoln has become martyred as the ` Great Emancipator ' but in reality this is not what Lincoln intended to be . For Lincoln the issue of slavery always came second to a reunion with the South and this showed in his early policies . Historians are only able to guess if Lincoln 's attitude towards blacks would have changed , though most believe that Lincoln would eventually have seen that the acceptance of blacks in the United States was inevitable and that he should flow with the tide and not against it . This view was not shared by Lincoln 's successor , Andrew Johnson . On April 15 th 1865 , Vice President Andrew Johnson was sworn into office as the seventeenth President of the United States . To most politicians this meant a significant change in reconstruction policy for the better . Even radicals like Senator Wade felt pleased with the new President , `` Johnson we have faith in you '' as they believed they had gone from a ` lenient ' Lincoln to a ` firm ' Johnson . An important aspect of Johnson 's time as President is the contrast between the support he received at the beginning of his Presidency and the lack of support he had by the end . How did a southern Democrat have so much support from Radicals and Republicans ? Much of Johnson 's early support came from his beliefs on how to treat the south , `` Treason must be made odious and traitors must be punished and impoverished '' ; this belief was strong within Radical and Republican groups . Johnson 's ideas on how to treat the south stemmed from his own personal dislike of the elite planter class , whom he believed were the real traitors who had dragged the south into war . Andrew Johnson was living proof of the American Dream . He was born in to poverty in Tennessee and gradually worked his way up as a tailor . He was illiterate until he married and then began to work his way into politics . Up until his time as President , Johnson 's political career was another American success story as he had advanced from the very bottom to the very top . During the war Johnson 's fierce denunciations of the secessionists leaders put him firmly within the Republican Party and gained him much early support . Johnson issued his Proclamation of Amnesty in May , and it was greeted with enthusiasm from both republicans , though the radicals believed they had been deceived on the issue of slavery . The amnesty promised a pardon to all those who would take an oath of allegiance , though fourteen classes of Confederates were excluded from this general pardon and had to apply to the President for a personal pardon . The most notable exception were Confederates with taxable property of $ 20,000 and the major Confederate officials , this clause impressed the radicals as they had hoped Johnson would make an example of the ` traitors ' . Unfortunately it soon became clear to radicals and republicans that what Johnson said and what he actually meant were two different things . After the Proclamation of Amnesty Southerners came forward in huge numbers to receive pardons and Johnson received thousands of requests for a personal pardon . Though Johnson gave out these pardon cautiously at first he was soon doing them in mass amounts . Johnson took the south 's willingness to co-operate as a sign that they supported him and his policies , and indeed they did but largely due to the huge relief they felt that the requirements for re-entry were n't harsher . Many historians believe that in May 1865 Reconstruction could have taken a very different path . Johnson could have imposed practically any rule on the Southern states and it would have been accepted but he always believed firmly in state rights and upholding the Constitution , so he stuck to his policy of reconstruction with only two amendments later on . As the radical Charles Sumner later said `` he has missed a golden opportunity '' . Even though in the eyes of some Johnson 's policy was misguided it achieved the outcome that Johnson had hoped for . The South began to reorganize itself as now it had an opportunity to rebuild in a way that reflected every individual state . Nevertheless Johnson 's treatment of the South led to many new problems and the first of these were the South 's choice of new officials . Johnson 's Proclamation stated that practically everyone was loyal , indeed it is a wonder how so many states seceded when everyone claimed to be ' a Union man ' who simply ` followed the state ' . Under Johnson 's requirement s the South began choosing their new governments , though the majority of these men could hardly be called loyal . In the deep South service to the Confederacy proved to be a virtual necessity for electing an official , and the state of Georgia sent former Confederate Vice-President Alexander H. Stephens to the senate . Further up in the South the elections had some of the outcomes the President had been hoping for , and in most states former Whig-Unionist men took up office . For Johnson and the South the 1865 elections had been a success and the states could now concentrate on providing a new constitution and entering the Union as quickly as possible . On the other hand Republicans and Radicals felt that the South was once again back in the hands of the traitors and dissent began to grow as feeling towards Johnson changed . It seemed Johnson liked the idea of being the ` champion of the South ' , indeed Johnson was a Southern Democrat and he did gain support from the South , but at the expense of support from the North . The events that occurred after the elections were of crucial importance to why Presidential reconstruction failed . Johnson made the ratification of the Thirteenth Amendment and repudiation of Confederate debt extra requirements for entry back into the Union , but both these provisions were created with a lack of enthusiasm in the South . These two new conditions laid on by Johnson were hardly radical but the states reverted back to their pre-war prejudices and many refused to ratify the amendment or merely acknowledge that slavery did not exist . The repudiation of state debt also caused similar debates within the newly formed state conventions and eventually Johnson had to publicly declare Confederate debt null and void . The problems that occurred from the acceptance of the two new conditions proved to Radicals and Republicans that the secessionist South had once again come back into power and that Johnson had deceived them with false promises and his desire to gain popularity in the South . Johnson 's fatal mistake was suggesting these changes at a time when Southern hope of ` home rule ' had been revived and they felt able to revise or reject them . Further problems that arose within the year were used as ammunition against Johnson over the next few years as Presidential Reconstruction began to fail . Most of the tribulations that were to affect Reconstruction stemmed from the issue of slavery and the black population . After the abolition of slavery had been accepted most of the South faced a severe labor problem , as the planters now had no personal authority over the blacks the state had to somehow re-establish a form of authority . New laws were needed to make sure that white supremacy was kept intact , and the President 's own beliefs went hand in hand with most of the Southern whites . Many new laws were passed in different states as a legal means of keeping a large black population subordinate . These laws quickly became known as the Black Codes and they played a significant role in the failure of Presidential Reconstruction . On the surface the codes were used as a way of defining freedmen 's new rights and his place in society . In reality they were used as a way of controlling the black workforce and limiting their social and economic possibilities . Legally the codes had not re-enacted slavery but they had come as close as it was possible to doing so and in some respects the freedman was in a worse position than before . Now they were n't just controlled by one man they were controlled by the whole state , as an early historian said the black codes ` put the state much in the place of the former master ' . The Black Codes provided crucial ammunition for Radicals and Republicans to prove that Presidential reconstruction had failed . To Johnson 's growing number of opponents the South 's choice of Confederate leaders showed that they were not loyal to the Union . Johnson had failed to create a ` new political leadership in the South ' and in part this is because it appeared that he had aligned himself with the old Southern elite just to increase his own following and possibility of re-election . The states revision or complete rejection of the terms imposed on them by Johnson were used as an example of the states ` reviving arrogance ' ; the enactment of the Black Codes showed a contempt for the morals of the north ; and the south 's continued determination to resurrect the past and old social structures was clear evidence that Johnson 's reconstruction policy was n't working . Worst of all was the President 's ` apparent tolerance of these things ' and his failure to do anything about them . While it highly unlikely that Johnson actually approved of the Black Codes he regarded states rights as undisputable and he never advocated black suffrage or citizenship for blacks . Johnson 's policy was acceptable on paper but almost a complete disaster in application . Political stability in the South had been restored and new governments had been formed , yet the majority of these governments could hardly be considered loyal . New constitutions and laws had been made , which was what the President wanted but these new laws went against nearly everything the North and the president 's opposition wanted . When Congress was called into session at the end of November 1865 Johnson believed his policy of reconstruction had been a resounding success and that any opposition came from a small number of troublemakers . Unfortunately for Johnson the majority of Congress disagreed with him and over the next few years a bitter feud grew over the issue of Reconstruction . By the time Congress ended Presidential Reconstruction and failed and Congressional Reconstruction began .
