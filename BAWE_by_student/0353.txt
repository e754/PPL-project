Commonly , debt finance -LRB- loan capital -RRB- and equity finance -LRB- share capital -RRB- are the main funding resources for a business . As the Clayton has made its decision of business expansion , it is crucial to compare and contrast these two financing sources in terms of their advantages and disadvantages . Debt finance is a kind of finance which involves a principal amount being lent by an institute for a fixed -LRB- or Variable -RRB- interest rate paid annually . This type of funding source is usually repaid over a longer period of time , usually within 5 to 30 years . This type of finance is quite quick for the company to gather and also it is quite stable regardless of the company 's profits . Such a mode of finance creates a lower pressure on the cash flow of the company . Debt finance is considerably simpler to initiate at the start . One of the main advantages over equity finance would be that the borrowing company would not have to loose out on their control over the company to its investors . The company would not have to include its banking institute in the company 's decision meeting policies . Since the interest amount would have to be paid annually the company would have to have a steady cash flow the entire year . Higher the rates higher would be the interest amount to be paid annually . The result of higher interest would be directly in terms of affecting the profit of the company . This would affect the balance and income statements ' of the company in case of future source of funding . Most of the loans usually come as a secured loan - Debentures . This would mean that in case due to faulty interest payment annually , the company may risk loosing some of its property or any other thing which has been secured against the loan . Equity finance is another term for raising funds by issuing shares . In such type of finance there is no form of interest to be paid annually but the company needs to pay some form of dividends to its shareholders to create an interest in investing with the company . Another huge advantage over debt finance is that the principle amount never needs to be paid and the shareholders make or loose money according to the market demand of the company shares . In the case of liquidation also , the shareholders are the last in line to receive the funds and the company is not forced to pay its shareholders . As mentioned before , the company looses a bit of control over the company 's decision making policy of the company to its shareholders . This is bad in case the company is willing to make change and its shareholders do not agree . The initial cost of issuing shares is also high . To maximise the return for the business expansion , any form of raising funds should be as much effective as possible . Otherwise , in the longer term , the returns may fall . URL URL `` Critical Path Analyses is a path in the design which must meet certain critical timing requirements in order for the system to function properly . '' -LRB- Elsevier , 1995 -RRB- In technical terms , the critical path of a project or a system can be defined as the path in which the `` float '' of all the processes is equal to `` Zero '' . A float can be defined as the ` buffer time ' available for each task to be completed in a complex project . It is derived once the critical path has been established . Critical path analyses -LRB- CPA -RRB- is considered to be a very powerful tool to schedule and manage complex projects of varying magnitude and size . CPA has become one of the tools that are most useful in practice and are applied in the planning and control of the realization of complex projects -LRB- Chanas et al , 2001 -RRB- . They were actually developed as means to manage and propagate various defense projects way back in the 1950 's . But the main ideology behind CPA still exists with minor changes in the line up to suit various companies and organizations -LRB- URL , 2006 -RRB- . Critical path analysis -LRB- CPA -RRB- is a method which is used to help layout , in a systematic order , all the tasks that need to be completed before a project could be termed as `` completed '' . They not only serve as a basis on which a schedule could be prepared but also act as a source of resource planning and time management between the processes . It also acts as a `` policeman '' during the progress of the project as it monitors and maintains the progress of the project goals . This way it helps the organisation to change any processes mid way in case if any actions need to be taken which would over all improve the quality and efficiency of the project -LRB- URL , 2006 -RRB- . Figure 1 below shows a very simple example of a critical path used in constructing a house . The longest sequence of path as highlighted below is the critical path of the entire project of constructing a house . The fundamental basis behind which the critical path analysis works is that the next activity in a work flow can not start before some other activities are over . In other words it follows a sequential flow of processes where in the end of each process is important for the start of the next one . For all the activities to progress the previous process needs to be more or less complete . There are other activities that may not be associated with this path . Those activities are known as independent or `` parallel '' activities , whose start and end time may not be as important for the proper functioning of the tasks in the `` Critical path '' -LRB- URL , 2006 -RRB- . PERT could be explained as an ` add-on ' to the critical path analysis . It allows the company to know the exact time required for each of the tasks in a project . PERT is a variation on Critical Path Analysis that scrutinizes the view of time estimates made for each stage of the project . To use it , estimate the shortest possible time each activity will take , the most likely length of time , and the longest time that might be taken if the activity takes longer than expected -LRB- URL , 2006 -RRB- . It indicates that for the next task to begin the completion of the first task is necessary . Start-to-start indicates that both the tasks need to start at the same time , but the end need not be at the same time . On the other hand , finish-to-finish indicates that irrespective of when each of the tasks starts , they need to end at the same time , in order to complete the project on time . Apart from this , there are also partial dependencies . This indicates that another task could begin after a certain portion of the ongoing task is complete .
