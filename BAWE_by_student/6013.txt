Lions maintain a unique social structure amongst cats ; unlike other big cats lions do not lead a solitary life . The social structure of the Serengeti lion is most known about these lions live in a pride which on average consists of six members -LRB- David Macdonald 1992 -RRB- . However in different parts of the world it has been documented for a pride to have between two and thirty members -LRB- A and J Nacqueline 1991 -RRB- . The lionesses will normally remain in their mother 's pride . However should a female lion be born into a large pride they may find there is a significant shortage of food due to not enough prey available , and in this case they may chose to leave the pride . If the female cubs are going to leave the pride they are born into they normally do this when they are around four years of age . These females will set up a sub group on the edge of their previous territory and may have an independent male or share the same male as the pride they left . It has also been documented that female lions have travelled great distances to establish a new pride , nevertheless very little is known about this , except that if they leave they are less likely to reach their normal life expectancy . The male lions will leave the pride when they reach sexual maturity of three years of age . These males form an alliance and are known as bachelors they hold no territory and subsequently hold no pride of females . They spend their time try to replace the dominant/resident male of a pride . The more bachelors within the group of nomads the more likely they are to succeed in taking over a pride . From a seven year case study in the Serengeti national park it was estimated that only approximately one percent of the lion population is made up of solitary lions . The male lion 's dormancy normally only lasts approximately three years , so the social structure within the pride is constantly changing . When a new dominant male takes over a pride of females the cubs of the last dominant male will be killed . These deaths of cubs occur more frequently in the larger prides , the larger the pride of females the more frequently the resident/dominant male is replaced .
