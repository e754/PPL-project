On the 20 th of February 1909 the first futurist manifesto was printed in the Italian daily circulation ` Le Figaro ' . It 's author Filippo Tommaso Marinetti had written it from a luxurious ` Villa Rossa ' in Milan and pointed to the Parisian public as the target of ` incendiary violence . ' Although Paris , enjoying its title as ` cultural capital of the world ' was regularly a victim of attacks on the establishment the manifesto showed radical ideas of performance the world had never seen before . Marinetti had lived in Paris from 1893-1896 and had enjoyed living in a circle of friends involved in the arts . Amongst these friends was Alfred Jarry . Marinetti was heavily influenced by Jarry 's controversial attitudes to the theatre and many of his ideas for futurist performance can be seen in Jarry 's play ` Ubo Roi . ' ` Ubo Roi ' debuted at Lugne-Poe 's Theatre de l'Oeuvre on the 11 th of December 1896 and was modelled on schoolboy farces and puppet shows . The play was a curious ensemble of ideas , which Jarry set up in the preface . These ideas were often contradictory to stimulate the audience . Jarry wrote how Ubo was to wear a horse head of cardboard ` as in the Old English theatre ' . This showed an interest in theatrical history but in contrast the costumes were too show ` as little colour and historical accuracy as possible ' . Jarry played with theatrical convention by not using a curtain and although only using one set also using a man in evening dress to hold up signs indicating the scene . The set itself was a curious mixture of ` indoors and outside at once ' emphasising Jarry 's desire to show simultaneous ideas in the same setting . Jarry desired ` Ubo Roi ' to provoke reactions in the audience and actively made an effort to aggravate the audience . During the first performance Jarry himself came out only to sip from a glass for ten minutes without any other action . The first word of the play was ` shit ' which was then followed by Ubo slaughtering his way to the throne . Fistfights broke out in the orchestra and the audience left either loving the play or hating it . Jarry had achieved his goal . The futurists took ideas from ` Ubo Roi ' such as the ensemble of ideas and abandonment of convention but it was the drive behind these ideas , the violence and riotous affect Jarry had on an audience , which Marinetti was inspired by can be clearly seen in extracts from the first futurist manifesto . ` Poetry must be conceived as a violent attack on unknown forces . We will glorify War . We will destroy the museums , libraries , academies of every kind ' The manifesto set out in clear terms the futurist 's intentions to counter convention and to do so in a highly aggressive manner . This kind of thinking had never been done before ; previous performances before had been created to entertain not to irritate ; this was clearly not the case with the futurists , later renaming their manifesto ` The Pleasure of Being Booed . ' The first futurist night took place on the 12 th of January 1910 at the Teatro Rosetti in Triest . The venue was specially chosen to provoke conflict for Triest was the border city in the unstable Austro-Italian conflict . The futurists stirred up trouble , publicly insulting the Austrian police and from then on police watched futurist events closely , labelling them as troublemakers . The futurists were important in performance innovation , as it was the first time a group of artists , poets , painters and performers came together to work under one aim : They wanted to shock , revolt and simply provoke response , good or bad . They were rebels against anything conventional , be it political or artistic . This complete rejection of anything prior to them allowed the futurists make significant innovations in performance , as they were free from any previous rules . Futurist nights took place under the loose term of variety theatre . The lack of structure allowed any artist , author , actor or technician to take part and by including all forms of art the futurists were able to disrupt the people 's views on a variety of different mediums on one place . The brief staged performances were called ` synthetic theatre ' with the intention to ` compress , into a few minutes , into a few words and gesture , innumerable situations , sensibilities , ideas , sensations , facts and symbols . ' In Bruno Corra and Emilio Settimelli 's ` Dissonance ' . A lady and page took to the stage dominated by ` thirteenth century environment ' and ` thirteenth century costumes ' and spoke with romantic , poetic speech . ` Page : Love , love , finally in this perfumed and mystic night of silver I can tell you of my pains . ` The performance deliberately sets up the audience with an expectation for a thirteenth century context and then in typical futurist manner these expectations are played with . A man who casually strolls on stage wearing modern dress interrupts the lady and page . He needs a light for his cigarette so he asks the page . ` Gentleman : Excuse me please , have you a match ? ` The page replies that he has n't in the same modern speech and the gentleman leaves . The page resumes speaking in the poetic thirteenth century way until the curtain drops . This is a good example of futurist ideals . It shows a complete disruption of convention , which the futurists often achieved by simultaneous realities . In this case the speech and dress conveyed two completely different contexts , modern and historical . Disrupting the expectations of the audience mocks and annoys the audience forcing them to re-evaluate their ideas on art . Futurist performances could be described as unfulfilling in the context of plays the audience would have expected . The futurists revelled in their own ability to suggest what the audience wanted and then keep it from them causing considerable frustration among the spectators . In ` Feet ' the curtain is raised but only one foot of the ground to allow the audience to only see the feet of the actors . The actors convey seven scenes through use of their feet but the scenes are unrelated so any sense of deciphering the scene is futile . Synthetic theatre thrived on teasing the audience 's expectations of theatre for a plot or characters or context and then denying it to stir up a response . Futurists often embraced tricks to annoy the audience further than their performances would allow , such as double booking the auditorium or sticking glue to the seats . The best example of this ` tease of convention ' is in Francesco Cangiullo 's ` Lights ' . The curtain is raised on a neutral stage but then the theatre is plunged into three minutes of darkness . ` Various actors scattered in the auditorium excite the spectators and encourage their shouting ' of ` Lights ! ' The shouting increased until everyone in the audience was shouting and then the lights were turned on as bright as they would go . At exactly the same time the curtain fell . ` Lights ' is a very good example of futurist work as it directly involves the spectators ; the darkness makes them nervous and the shouting , the action in the performance is provided by the audience themselves . The audience wanted light and were denied it . This provoked the riotous and aggressive shouting and although light was given it was only for a brief second . The futurists were the embodiment of violent rebellion and public upheaval and in ` Lights ' they drew their audience into taking part as rebels themselves . Futurist synthetic theatre often went to even greater extremes , denying the audience even human interaction and taking performance to new places . They played with everything , embracing new technology to battle conventional art forms with new modern styles . Rossulo 's ` Noise Music , ' which combined the sounds of trams , exploding motors , trains and the shouting of crowds , battled the public 's perception of music and often left them screaming for ` no more ' . Painting was brought into a world of three dimensions in futurist ballet and ` Fireworks ' , a 1917 performance by Balla , was based on one of his paintings . The painting was performed through ` Light Ballet ' with the stage and audience being lit and unlit . In five minutes forty-nine different settings were created . Perhaps the greatest display of the futurist embrace for technology is shown through aerial ballet . In 1919 Fedele Azari embraces aeroplanes as a new form of performance as ` Ariel Ballet ' and its ability to embrace the most spectators in the shortest time . This idea can still be seen today in the aerial display shows . Futurism was the movement that defined all movements in art after it . Although previous art movements had brought together painters , actors and poets under one title never before had their impact on the entirety of art been so great . Futurism wanted to distance itself from anything that had been done before and therefore pushed art to new things as it in such dramatic extremes . By abandoning anything that had gone before the futurists broke the mould and allowed any artistic movements afterwards the freedom and context to work under . The futurists were rebels and should be credited , as the first movement to redefine the rules of performance , without their ingenuity art would never have progressed as far as it has done .
Ibsen and Strindberg 's early social plays explore the society present in Europe . Both playwrights were particularly interested in the idea of determinism and the role that it plays on humanity . The plays I will explore in this essay , A Doll 's House and Ms. Julie , are each used to articulate the playwrights ' differing theories on the importance of free will and determinism . The plays , including such dramatic elements as the characters and scenery , are used as visual representations of the playwrights ' study into European society . The female protagonists ' interaction with the other characters causes them to realise to what extent life is determined and if there is enough strength in their independence to take control of their own lives . Through Nora and Julie the audience experience the pressures of life present in Europe in the nineteenth century . The finale of each play presents them with the writer 's conclusion to the dominance of either determinism or free will . Ibsen believed that there were ` two kinds of conscious , one in man and a quite different one in women . They do not understand each other ' . He wrote this in a brief memorandum , attached to the first draft of A Doll 's House , called The Tragedy of To-Day . In the note Ibsen describes the affect of a male dominated Europe and explores the suppressing effect this has on women . He wrote that ' a woman can not be herself in the society of to-day ' for she is always under the influence of man ` who judge feminine conduct from the masculine standpoint ' . It is clear that A Doll 's House directly confronts this issue with each character representing a different aspect of Ibsen 's contemporary society . Torvald is the personification of the unthinking control men take over a woman 's life . Torvald is obsessed with superficiality and throughout the course of the play Ibsen shows how Nora only fits into Torvald 's life as another part of his aesthetic fantasy . Torvald refuses any ugliness to enter his life , and , in turn , Nora 's . By denying Nora a full representation of existence , Nora has become ignorant to any other options in life other than the ones shown to her . Society 's suppression of women has given Nora no chance to develop naturally and this comes at the loss of finding her own personality . A Doll 's House is the story of a woman discovering that her life is based on false notions and realising that there are possibilities outside the house . The play is full of images that suggest Torvald 's obsession with superficial attractiveness and Nora is introduced to the audience as a material possession , just another aesthetic creation of Torvald . In the original stage description the beautiful decorations and ` well-bound books ' set up Torvald 's aesthetic ideals before Nora enters carrying ' a number of parcels -LRB- Pg .1 -RRB- ' another symbol of materialism . The festive setting helps Ibsen to emphasis Nora 's predicament . Her entrance is coupled with the entrance of the Christmas tree and this dual entrance suggests a link between Nora and the tree ; they both represent an interference with nature . The tree has been taken out of its natural environment and placed inside , then covered up . This alteration from its natural state to an artificial creation symbolises Nora 's transformation under Torvald at the loss of her natural personality . Nora 's realisation of her determined situation can be mapped throughout the play through her relationship with the festive backdrop present in the play . In Act I Nora 's jolly demeanour blends in with the festal setting but as the play develops Nora 's growing despair contrasts dramatically . By Act II the tree has been ` stripped of its ornaments ' and displays ` burned down candles on its dishevelled branches -LRB- Pg .29 -RRB- ' , representative of Nora 's increasing discovery of herself . Krogstad and Rank are the personifications of the darker aspects of life that Torvald keeps away from his and Nora 's home ; representing guilt and sorrow respectively . Torvald distances himself from Krogstad and sordid law cases but ` by attempting to exclude conflict , guilt and sorrow from their lives , Torvald and Nora are evading human wholeness for a doll existence ' . As Krogstad and Rank become more prominent figures in the house they enlighten Nora to the imperfect world outside of the house . Ibsen uses dramatic visuals in his staging to contrast Nora 's happy home with the sinister figure of Krogstad . His first entrance comes while Nora is playing with the children and his threatening presence contrasts dramatically with the ` shouts of laughter ' -LRB- pg . 18 -RRB- from the mother and child . Although Torvald had dismissed Krogstad from his life , the side of life he represents inevitably filters into the Helmers ' home . The visiting characters are embodiments of the life Nora has never seen before ; aspects of her character she had never fully realised . Torvald and her father kept her like a child and Ibsen best presents this through Torvald 's fantasy of Nora as his ` young bride ' -LRB- Pg .58 -RRB- brought home for the first time . Nora starts the play as this child like figure of her husband 's fantasy indulging in the name-calling but by Act III she rejects Torvald 's smothering . She has realised that she shares an affinity with Krogstad and his guilt , while Rank serves as a reminder that she is as negatively affected by heredity as he . Rank pays for his father 's hedonistic lifestyle and dies because of it ; through Rank 's death the audience see Nora 's spiritual death , brought on by Torvald and her father . This idea of Torvald moulding Nora at the expense of her character is shown in a multitude of ways throughout the play . Within the first few pages Torvald scorns Nora for her decision to go shopping and for eating macaroons , later on he takes over Nora 's creative decisions by advising her on her costume and directs her dance , the tarantella . Nora 's dance is a perfect visualisation of Torvald 's grip over her . Ibsen 's choice of dance further enhances the negative affect Torvald 's smothering attitude . The tarantella is a dance associated with the convulsions of a tarantula 's victim after it has been infected by the spider 's poisonous bite . The parallels are clear , Ibsen is telling the audience that Torvald 's influence over Nora is killing her and suffocating her personality . Nora dances with increasing ferocity suggesting the torment present over her lack of control over her own life which is represented when she ` suddenly stands still ' -LRB- Pg .49 -RRB- when the music stops . Nora has no input into her own life ; she literally dances to her husband 's tune . Torvald 's suppression of Nora is not treated by Ibsen as a specific case ; instead the private environment displayed on stage , The Helmer 's home , becomes a microcosm of the society present in Europe . When Mrs Linde comments ' I have witnessed incredible things in this house -LRB- pg .54 -RRB- ' it is with a sense of irony for Torvald 's suppression of Nora is not shown by Ibsen as an extraordinary incident but as a commonplace situation . When Nora leaves at the end she says she is in need of education , not in terms of knowledge , but in terms of discovering whom she really is . Nora describes how she was ` simply transferred from Papa 's hand to yours -LRB- Torvald 's -RRB- ' -LRB- Pg .66 -RRB- . This suggests a hereditary link from Nora 's father to Torvald : This is representative of all men . Ibsen creates the Helmers as everymen figures for their time ; their interaction is representative of the European culture that unthinkingly determining a woman 's role leaving no freedom for growth . The movement of the table in the final act is a simple but striking image that the audience can relate to and imagine taking place within every home . By writing Nora 's departure from the marriage Ibsen is telling the audience that even though we live in a deterministic society everyone has the freewill to break free from convention , even if it will be very difficult . It is as if Strindberg wrote Ms. Julie in a direct response to this feeling of hope and optimism Ibsen creates at the end of A Doll 's House . Strindberg 's play places a strong emphasis on determinism but presents life as ultimately absurd ; nothing you do is of any consequence . This is the line of thought Strindberg takes in Ms. Julie . Both Jean and Julie are determined characters seeking to escape in the same way as Nora . The difference between the two authors is that Ibsen saw escape from determinism as a possible feat and Strindberg did not . Ms Julie is a play with two central themes that determine personality and divide the characters ; they are gender and class . Jean and Julie 's relationship is an attempt to overcome these divisions ; they act against determined behavioural patterns and exercise their free will , but they do not succeed . Strindberg uses Jean and Julie 's interaction to show life as utterly absurd ; through their mutually destructive relationship the audience sees how any attempt to force a change in life will ultimately fail . Jean and Julie 's relationship is brought about by their twinned desires to leave the constraints of their life behind them . Jean and Julie 's dreams allow Strindberg to explain their frustrations visually , albeit through the audience 's imagination . In both dreams there is a common theme of height ; in Julie 's dream she is ` on top of a high pillar ' while Jean ` lies under a tree , in a dark forest ' -LRB- Pg .12 -RRB- . The pillar and tree are symbols of the social scale but it also a prominent phallic image . Julie is not only separated from the rest of humanity by her social class but also by her bizarre sexuality ; raised as a man by her mother she was dressed ` in boy 's clothes -LRB- Pg .30 -RRB- ' and has not developed as a natural woman . Strindberg describes Ms. Julie as ' a half woman locked in a desperate struggle with her own sexual nature ; she belongs to a class that no longer has a meaningful role ' This alienation is represented by the pillar in the dream and the desire to fall and sink into the ground reflects Julie 's desire to die and abandon the life she was given . Jean also wishes to escape the life that he was given . His desire to climb the phallic tree and steal the egg symbolises his attempt to reach a higher class through sex . Strindberg shows the audience Jean 's desire through his interactions with other characters ; in a similar way that Ms. Julie descends to the kitchen or the party , Jean attempts to rise at every opportunity . Jean plays the aristocrat with Kristin in the first scene when he scolds her for not warming the plate and then demanding a better glass for his wine . This contrasts directly to Julie 's comment ` I 've simple tastes . I 'd rather have beer than wine ' -LRB- Pg .9 -RRB- . The visual contrast between beer and wine is another symbol of their differing desires and lives . Both Jean and Julie envision each other as a way of transcending the class boundaries but after their union they only bicker and argue as they realise their dreams can not be achieved . The protagonist 's vision of a successful dissolution of classis ruled out by Strindberg from the very start of the play . The abortion mixture Kristin is cooking up at the beginning of the play foreshadows Ms. Julie 's death . Ms. Julie 's dog has been ` at it with the mongrel from the lodge ' -LRB- pg .4 -RRB- and has become pregnant . The two dogs represent on a general scale the merging of two different classes and on a specific scale Jean and Julie . Their union can only end in disaster and death which is visually represented by Kristin 's cooking of the mixture . The divisions of class and sex are also emphasised before the first word has been spoken in the play . After the curtain is raised for the first time the audience witnesses conflicting visuals . The action takes place in the kitchen containing the ` metal utensils ' -LRB- Pg .1 -RRB- suiting the servant 's workplace while the arched entrance to the right and the grand garden in the background are representative of the high class environment the servant 's work in . These images emphasis the differences between the two classes present in the play but they are also used to set up the differences between the sexes . The garden not only represents the class status of Ms. Julie but the Eros fountains , lilacs in bloom and tall poplars are phallic images expressing the sexual conflict that will dominate the play . Jean and Kristin 's costumes , the apron and uniform , double as statements of their menial roles within the house but also as aids for the audience to identify them as stereotypically male and female ; two distinct opposites . Emphasising these stereotypes so early in the play allows Strindberg to identify Jean and Julie as unusual characters , who do not represent man and woman as a whole . This allows him to show the effects if one attempts to act outside of convention . Ms. Julie is certainly not the average woman due to her peculiar upbringing . By setting the play during the festivities of Midsummer Day Strindberg is emphasising the unique and unnatural nature of Jean and Julie . Julie describes the holiday as a time for ` enjoying ourselves , not fussing about mistresses and servants ' -LRB- Pg .6 -RRB- but this break from the rules is exposed to be false by the dancing and derogatory singing of the peasants . When Jean exclaims that ` we ca n't fight them ' -LRB- Pg .20 -RRB- he is expressing the futility he feels after his plans for social aspiration have collapsed . The futility of any attempt to transfer between classes is represented by Jean 's changing costume throughout the play . If Julie is his conquest , his tree to scale , in order to become a gentleman then her order for him to change coats is a symbol of his assumed success . When he changes back on the arrival of the count he finds that he can no longer order Julie and curses ` the damned menial ' within himself . The jacket is a Strindberg 's reminder to the audience that we all have our roles in society , which can not be replaced . The end of Ms. Julie has some strong parallels to A Doll 's House . Like Nora Julie attributes her personality down to heredity : She describes how her father gave her everything she thinks and her mother gave her everything she feels ; this sentiment is suitable for both plays as they carry the same notion of determinism in the world . Strindberg and Ibsen have opposite opinions over the strength of free will . The difference is shown in the outcome of the two plays , for although both characters are educated through interaction with other characters , Nora can escape for her problems are contained within the boundaries of the home . Nora is provided with hope for improvement whereas Julie 's new experiences of the wider world destroy her ideals ; any visions of hope are proven false which drives her to despair and -LRB- assumed -RRB- suicide . Both playwrights engage the themes of determinism but although both writers acknowledged the presence of determinism in life Ibsen endeavoured , through A Doll 's House , to teach the world that it was possible to overcome . Strindberg 's Ms. Julie conveys exactly the opposite message . He believed that the realism of Julie 's character ` must be conditioned by a number of hereditary and environmental circumstances which , combined and accumulated , pull her towards the final destruction . ' In a last final moment of each play the striking image of each character leaving the stage spells out the contrasting opinions of the writers . In both plays the lead woman leaves the stage with their future left very much up to the interpretation of the audience , but while Nora 's exit is under the air of possibilities and hope Ms. Julie 's exit , with razor blade in hand , leaves the audience in no doubt that there is no other outcome other than her death .
This essay will seek to understand how Grotowski 's approach to theatre that resulted in his practice gaining the title of ` poor theatre ' . I will attempt to explain the unique aspects of Grotowski 's practices in the theatre and highlight the reasons behind Grotowski 's theatrical beliefs , including his influences and aims for his own work for Grotowski , as its creator , was directly responsible for the definition of poor theatre . At the same time I will explain what is understood by the description of ` poor theatre ' as a theoretical and a practical approach to performance in the theatre by looking at the different performances Grotowski was a part of . Grotowski 's theatre career really began with his establishment of the Theatre of Thirteen rows with Ludwik Flazzen in Opole , a town sixty miles from Auschwitz in Poland . It was here that he lay down the foundations for ` poor theatre ' but his greatest achievements as a practitioner would not take place until he relocated to Wroclaw on the first of January 1965 . It was there that made his intent clear by giving the Laboratory Theatre the status of the ` Institute of Actor 's Research ' . The two titles are indicative of Grotowski 's desire to explore theatre and push its limits . ` We are seeking to define what is distinctively theatre , what separates this activity from other categories performance and spectacle . -LRB- Grotowski , Towards a Poor Theatre , Pg .15 -RRB- ` Grotowski 's aim was to find the essence of theatre and classify it . This resulted in a systematic removal of anything that was not necessary in performance for it to remain theatre . He went on to say that ` By gradually eliminating whatever proved superfluous , we found that theatre can exist without make-up , without autonomic costume and scenography , without a separate performance area -LRB- stage -RRB- , without lighting and sound effects etc. . It can not exist without the actor-spectator relationship of perceptual . ' -LRB- Grotowski , Towards a Poor Theatre , Pg .19 -RRB- Grotowski understood that the only thing necessary for theatre to function was a core of actor in a space performing to an audience . Everything else that could not be generated by the actors and their interaction with the audience was not necessary and therefore could and would be taken away from his work . As Grotowski wrote ` No matter how much theatre expands and exploits its mechanical resources , it will remain technologically inferior to film and television . Consequently , I propose poverty in theatre . ' -LRB- Towards a Poor Theatre , Pg .19 -RRB- Grotowski 's work was given the label of ` poor theatre ' as it was intentionally stripped down to its very essence and this is its most distinguishing feature . With all other aspects designated as ` superfluous ' to theatre Grotowski formed a great interest in the relationship between actor and audience . He believed that the text of a play did not make theatre itself ; only when performed by an actor to an audience did it then gain the title of theatre . An essential part of this relationship was the audience 's ability to lose themselves completely in the spectator aspect of the performance just as the actor must lose himself completely while giving himself to the audience . The actor 's technique was therefore absolutely essential to Grotowski 's vision of the theatre . He saw the body as an instrument which must be capable of more than the spectators and because of this he went to great lengths to train his actors to express themselves adequately . The training that all Grotowski 's actors went through was a distinguishing aspect of ` poor theatre ' as it was responsible for the striking acting styles that could be seen in the Theatre Laboratory productions . The training did not focus on teaching the actor 's new things -LRB- there was no formal voice coaching for example -RRB- because Grotowski did not want to merely teach his actors a bag of tricks . Grotowski was interested in doing just the opposite : Grotowski wished to strip the actors down so training took the process of via-negativa ; an attempt to break down an actor 's mental blocks and give them freedom . He desired an actor to be released from so called natural behavior which resisted impulses in an attempt to reduce the time between inner thoughts and outer actions . He believed that an actor must be able to accomplish ` self-penetration ' in order to give themselves totally to the audience : The most intimate parts of the actor must be accessible during a performance to allow great ease of communication between actor and spectator . This desire for his actors to attain a passive readiness to act out their most natural desires made Grotowski unique amongst practitioners in the Western world . He said himself that ` It is not a comfortable concept for the western mind , which tends to prefer positive intellect-guided actions ' -LRB- Grotowski 's Theatre Laboratory , pg 194 -RRB- When establishing a form of training for his actors Grotowski drew on his various and eclectic influences . He was Influenced by Stanislavski 's work on physical actions , Dullin 's rhythm exercises , Delsarte 's investigations of extroversive and introversive reactions as well as Meyerhold 's bio-mechanical training and Vakhtonghov 's synthesis . He also brought in knowledge of the training techniques in Oriental theatre , especially the Peking Opera , Indian Kathakali and Japanese Noh theatre . What separated Grotowski from other practitioners however was his adaptation of various training methods to suit his own purpose . He never rested with his ideas and as such his training never became a defined set of motions ; in fact Grotowski actively sought not to have a commercial brand of method like Stanislavski or Brecht . Although he was brought up on Stanislavski he moved away from his ideas although he never lost Stanislavski 's attention to detail . ` We found that it is consummately theatrical for the actor to transform from type to type , character to character , silhouette to silhouette - while the audience watched - in a poor manner , using only his body and craft . ' -LRB- Grotowski , Towards a Poor Theatre , Pg 21 -RRB- Grotowski relied on the actor 's body alone to express itself . Without any artificial enhancements such as lighting and external sound effects the actors had to make their own noises and props . The scene they were in had to be conveyed through the actor 's gestures . Costumes could be created through the way an actor behaved and through association between the acted character and the audience 's perception of them . The few props were used merely as extensions of a character as in The Constant ; the prosecutors wore symbols of power such as cloaks , top-boots and in one case a crown while the accused prince wore a white shirt of purity , a loin cloth representing his vulnerability and a red cloak fitting of a martyr . This reliance on the actor alone can further be seen in the pictures taken from Akropolis . In this play the actors played the parts of the Auschwitz dead while the audience were representatives of the living , witnessing a life they could not partake in . The spectators sat on raised platforms while in the centre of the room a large box was placed ; this was covered in piles of metal junk and this is all the actors had to work with to construct the play 's action . The actors moved in a heavily stylized way which emphasized the fact that they came from another world to the audience . They used no props and swapped characters continuously so they only had their bodies available to express themselves . Their closeness to the audience intensified their manipulation of their bodies in contrast to the audience 's still frames . The actors worked together to form highly emotive tableaus reminiscent of Caravaggio 's paintings and concentration camp art . The defining features of acting in the poor theatre can be summed up as follows : The actors only used their body and voice to depict character , costumes and props were used but only sparingly as extensions of the actor . The actor 's deficiencies were used and not hidden . Actors would exchange parts for there was no attempt made to pretend that the actor actually was their character , instead the idea of actors playing to an audience was exploited for maximum impact . If the actor 's attitude toward performance was an important feature of ` poor theatre ' then the staging , including the positioning of the audience was as equally noticeable . The aesthetics Grotowski created at the Laboratory Theatre were striking though their overwhelming simplicity . The performances were often starved of any technical aspects such as costumes , props , artificial sounds or lighting , the latter often being produced by a few bare bulbs positioned in the theatre . This allowed a greater focus on the actor 's expression and the spectators ' part in the performance . ` Our productions are detailed investigations of the actor-audience relationship . That is , we consider the personal and scenic technique of the actor as the core of theatre art . ' -LRB- Grotowski , Towards a Poor Theatre , Pg .15 -RRB- The special element of a theatre , the situation a spectator is placed into during a performance , was very important to Grotowski . He came up with a variety of different ways of seating the audience in order to engage them in the most effective way . There was no fourth wall mentality in ` poor theatre ' ; the audience were always much more prominent than a regular theatre setup whether it be as a patient -LRB- Kordian -RRB- , dinner guest -LRB- Dr. Faustus -RRB- or merely as a detached onlooker -LRB- The Constant Prince -RRB- . For this reason the audience size was always limited . The Theatre Laboratory seated less than one hundred so the audience was always guaranteed to be small . This allowed for a more intimate and personal experience . Grotowski wished to emphasis the audience 's presence not to hide it : his aim was for the audience to confront their own role within the performances as spectators . He gave them different roles and asked them to judge themselves in that role , whether it be as an object , passive spectator or merely a part of the scenery . The roles designated to the spectator may have changed over the course of Grotowski 's career but the function an audience performs remained at the very heart of ` poor theatre ' and is one of its most striking and defining features . To fully understand the importance of the audience and staging in ` poor theatre ' it is necessary to look at the progression of Grotowski 's work throughout the sixties and the different ways in which he used them to further the play 's themes . Just two years after forming the Theatre of Thirteen Rows Grotowski stated his intentions for an active audience in his production of Forefather 's Eve when he created a total spatial integration of actors and audience , giving the latter the title of participant and designating them roles to fulfill in the play . The next production in 1962 was the play Kordian . It takes up the story of a man -LRB- Kordian -RRB- who dedicated himself to travel in search of the ultimate truth . While at the top of Mont Blanc he has a revelation that everything can be achieved through self-sacrifice . He returns to Poland to kill the Tsar but his attempt fails and he is sent to an insane asylum . The play is set within the asylum and although the audience was not given such obvious roles as in Forefather 's Eve they still took on an element of role-play : They were patients as with the protagonist and as Kordian was condemned and killed the audience went through his story with him . The intellectual distancing between the audience and the actor was undermined through the involving set . It took place on several levels and the scenery was only created through metal beds and chairs . The actors used the beds as performance spaces while the audience were seated on them . The audience was provoked to take part and sing a song . If a member of the audience did not take part then the doctors would identify them . This designation of the audience as patients and direct confrontation with Kordian 's action meant that the actors merely became more active members of the scenario than the audience . This in turn meant that the audience had to find their role in the play and address its key theme , what is insanity ? The staging of Dr. Faustus , with the action taking place round one large dinner table , was another variation of the break down between previously separate actor and audience space but Grotowski was soon to abandon his ideas of an active audience . From Dr. Faustus onwards Grotowski placed the audience back in their more typical role of passive onlooker . This did not mean however that Grotowski was not interested in the audience ' role within theatre but rather that poor theatre became an exploration of the actor 's role as an uninvolved participant . Grotowski 's next two plays , Acropolis and A Constant Prince both shared the same attitude towards the audience ; they were ignored by the actors but were not forgotten in Grotowski ' aims for the piece . Acropolis saw the audience confronted at close quarters with the horrifically stylized movements of the actors . The stage was set so the actors and audience shared the same space and were constantly within touching distance of each other . An American reviewer wrote of the discomfort this staging provoked . ` Actors move in and out of one another 's space in demanding physical ways , but they never violate our space , or even look at us , though missing us by a hair 's breadth . We are reminded , in this structural metaphor , at what close quarters we live , how narrowly we are missed or stricken by disaster or love ' -LRB- Jennifer Kumiega , The Theatre of Grotowski , Pg .61 -RRB- In no other style of theatre could such a feeling be created ; the unique staging of poor theatre allowed a feeling in the audience that was totally fitting with its setting of Auschwitz , a place where ` where our century had had to measure its values ' -LRB- Jennifer Kumiega , The Theatre of Grotowski , Pg 59 -RRB- . The Constant Prince took another step towards an entirely passive audience by furthering them from the actors but Grotowski 's main concern remained with the audience 's assessment of their own position within the performance . The play was based around a loose scenario involving a group of persecutors and their prisoners . The first captive , Don Henri , was symbolically castrated and then joined the group . However the second prisoner , Don Fernando , refused all compromise and opposed his persecution with love and humility . As the persecutors were forced to greater cruelty which ultimately ended in Fernando 's death ` The audience were made to watch the drama of persecution from elevated positions surrounding a wooden enclosure with the guilty curiosity of voyeurs ' -LRB- Jennifer Kumiega , Grotowski 's Theatre Laboratory , Pg . 196 -RRB- . Although the audience participated in no greater form than in any conventional theatre , their elevated position emphasized their presence : Their role in the play forced them to confront the scenes in front of them , a trait unique to ` poor theatre ' . Grotowski strongly desired to break down the audience-actor barrier but he never lost sight of their initial aim to define theatre . If The Constant Prince asked the question ` how far can the relationships of actor and audience change while still remaining theatre ? ' then the answer came in the defining moment of poor theatre Apocalypsis Cum Figuris . All the distinguishing elements of ` poor theatre ' are present in this performance . Set , costumes and props were abandoned as the play took place in a large empty room . The only lighting present was two spotlights and a candle . The main emphasis of the piece was on equality between actor and audience ; while one gave the other received . This was the pinnacle of ` poor theatre ' for nothing was needed except an actor performing to a spectator ; they alone created the scene and when both left the room returned to its state before their entrance . The audience was as important a force in the scenario as the actors . Apocalypsis Cum Figuris did not attempt to manipulate the audience into participating but instead used their passive role to heighten the play 's emotion . As Kumiega wrote ; the persecuted simpleton ` was not only excluded from the group consciousness - he was also trapped between their -LRB- the actors ' -RRB- active rejection and the passive refusal of the surrounding witnesses -LRB- the audience -RRB- to intervene . By their own awareness of the roles they had accepted for the duration of an hour , and their inability to cast of these roles , all present forced the cycle events nightly to its preordained conclusion ' . -LRB- pg .199 , Grotowski 's Theatre Laboratory -RRB- . With the performance of Apocalypsis Cum Figuris ` poor theatre ' had reached its conclusion in dramatic style . Grotowski had shown in practice his theory that nothing in theatre is essential other than the actor-audience interaction but had also managed to manipulate this relationship for dramatic effect . Although his style of theatre became to be known as ` poor ' it was certainly not lacking in depth , drama or beauty : As Leonia Jablonkowna wrote ` It is true that Grotowski 's acetic approach reaches its limits here ... and yet in spite of the poverty the spectacle dazzles with a richness of visual effect -LRB- in the best sense -RRB- . The circumstances , gestures and movement have a dynamic intensity and a fascinating attention to detail ... Each gesture , however insignificant , each movement of the hand or sway of the body has expressive power ... The actor is like an instrument capable of registering a variety of tones and octaves . He could take the place of a powerful symphony orchestra , and himself perform the symphony ' -LRB- The Theatre of Grotowski , Pg .96 -RRB-
