Fraud detection is identifying fraud as soon as possible after it has been perpetrated . Fraud detection becomes necessary once fraud prevention has failed . In practice , of course fraud detection must be a continuous process , as one will not often be aware when fraud prevention has failed . Individuals can attempt to prevent credit card fraud by guarding their cards diligently , but if in spite of this the card 's details are stolen , then the ability to detect , as soon as possible , that fraud is imperative . Fraud detection is continuously evolving . Whenever criminals become aware that one detection method is in place , they will adapt their strategies and try other methods . Of course , new criminals are also constantly entering the field . Many of them will not be aware of past fraud detection methods that have been successful and will adopt strategies , which can lead to identifiable frauds . This means that the earlier detection tools need to be applied as well as more recent strategies . The development of new fraud detection methods is hampered by the fact that the exchange of ideas in fraud detection is severely restricted . It makes no sense to detail fraud detection techniques to any extent in the public domain , as this enables criminals to work out what strategies they need to evade detection . Many fraud detection problems involve huge data sets . For example , the credit card holders with Barclaycard carry out approximately 350 million transactions a year in the United Kingdom alone -LRB- Hand , Blunt , Kelly and Adams , 2000 -RRB- , The Royal Bank of Scotland , which has the largest credit card merchant acquiring business in Europe , carries over a billion transactions a year and AT&T carries around 275 million calls each weekday -LRB- Cortes and Pregibon , 1998 -RRB- . Processing these data sets to identify fraudulent transactions or calls requires more than mere novelty of statistical model , and also needs fast and efficient algorithms . These numbers also indicate the potential value of fraud detection : if 0.1 % of a 100 million transactions are fraudulent , each losing the company just # 10 , then overall the company loses # 1 million . Fraud detection methods may be supervised or unsupervised . In supervised systems , samples of both fraudulent and non-fraudulent records are taken as examples on which to construct models . These allow one to reclassify the examples into one of two classes . Of course , this requires confidence in trying to classify the original data used to build the models . In order to plan a supervised model , both fraudulent and non-fraudulent example transactions would be required , so that the model could be trained to classify between the two models . The only drawback of supervised models is that they can only be used to detect fraud of a type that are already known . In contrast , unsupervised methods simply identify those accounts etc. , which differ most from the average transaction . This means that the fundamental point is that we can rarely be certain , by the sole use of statistical analysis , whether a fraud has occurred . Rather , the analysis should be regarded as a tool which alerts us to the fact that observations are anomalous , or more likely to be suspect than others , so that they can then be investigated in more detail . One can consider the objective of this statistical analysis as being to allocate a suspicion score -LRB- where a higher score is regarded as more suspicious than a lower one -RRB- . Suspicion scores can be given for each record held in the database and these scores can be constantly reassessed . These scores can then be put in order and can be further investigate those with the highest scores or on those that indicate a sudden increase . It is too expensive to undertake a detailed investigation of all records , so for cost-effectiveness investigation is concentrated on those transactions considered most likely to be fraudulent . The diversity of fraudulent activity as indicated by the numerous types of fraud makes the detection of fraudulent behaviour an important task . However , most banks tend to use only the most basic of statistical analyses to develop these guidelines , meaning that in most cases the rule sets consist of a set of simple threshold conditions on account variables .
