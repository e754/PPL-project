Second language acquisition is a thorougly investigated field and one attracting the interest of linguists over the years . The SLA theories that have been developed can be grouped into two major sets : a -RRB- those that `` give primary importance to the learners ' innate characteristics '' -LRB- Lighbrown & Spada , 1999 , p. 31 -RRB- such as Constructivism and Innatism and b -RRB- those which `` emphasize the essential role of the environment in shaping language learning '' -LRB- Lightbrown & Spada , 1999 , p. 31 -RRB- such as Cognitivism and Behaviorism . However , in the limited space provided , this essay examines how Cognitivism , Behaviorism and Constructivism view the role of L1 , input and output in SLA and analyzes how these key issues explain certain features of my learning of Persian such as the course-book design , grammar and vocabulary . One of the main theories interested in the role of L1 is Cognitivism . According to Brown -LRB- 2000 -RRB- , `` cognitive psychologists -LRB- ... -RRB- sought to discover underlying motivations and deeper structures of human behavior by using a rational approach -LRB- p. 10 -RRB- . For them , L1 plays a crucial role in L2 acquisition -LRB- Gass & Selinker , 2001 , p. 118 -RRB- . However , its influence is mediated by several other factors such as `` a -RRB- a learner 's psychotypology , how a learner organizes his or her NL , b -RRB- perception of NL-TL distance , and c -RRB- actual knowledge of the TL '' -LRB- Gass & Selinker , 2001 , p. 131 -RRB- . So learners will decide upon transferring certain features to the TL or not being based on the actual closeness of the two languages . Another theory focusing on the environment 's role is Behaviorism and within it Contrastive Analysis which tries to explain the relationship between L1 and L2 . Thus , `` individuals tend to transfer the forms and meanings -LRB- ... -RRB- of their native language -LRB- ... -RRB- to the foreign language '' -LRB- cited in Gass & Selinker , 2001 , p. 65 -RRB- . This transfer of habits from L1 to L2 can take three different forms : a -RRB- negative transfer , b -RRB- positive transfer , and c -RRB- avoidance , where learners avoid the use of certain forms that are not part of their L1 . So , `` those elements that are similar to the -LRB- learner 's -RRB- native language will be simple for him , and those areas that are different will be difficult '' -LRB- quoted in Johnson , 2001 , p. 60 -RRB- . Therefore , L1 is seen as the main source of learner error and as a barrier to L2 acquisition . On the contrary , a theory that is developed as `` an alternative to CA -LRB- ... -RRB- -LSB- is -RSB- creative construction theory '' -LRB- Johnson , 2001 , p. 67 -RRB- . Its supporters believe that L1 influence is very indirect and difficult to establish . They do not see errors as a result of L1 transfer but as developmental and thus as representing learner 's effort to practice internalized rules -LRB- Johnson , 20001 , p. 68 -RRB- . They also claim that `` children learning a second language use a creative construction process , just as they do in their first language '' -LRB- Brown , 2000 , p. 69 -RRB- . Finally , learners are seen as capable of forming hypotheses about L2 on the basis of `` universal innate mechanisms '' -LRB- Gass & Selinker , 2001 , p. 105 -RRB- and not on L1 basis . Before proceeding to the analysis of L1 in relation to my learning experience it is necessary to clarify the context of this learning situation . As part of a module called `` second language teaching and learning '' in my postgraduate studies I tried to learn a limited amount of conversational Persian , with associated grammar and vocabulary . I used the course-book `` Colloquial Persian '' with an accompanying tape and I covered the first six lessons of the course from mid October till December 9 2005 . While working on the lessons , I recorded my learning experience in a diary . When learning Persian I had certain expectations . Firstly , I expected that some of the theoretical aspects about the role of L1 would apply to my learning experience whereas others not . Secondly , I expected to find slightly different syntax and grammatical rules from Greek -LRB- my L1 -RRB- . Thirdly , I expected to see completely different sounds and a new pronunciation system . In fact , some of my initial expectations were confirmed . Specifically , Contrastive Analysis predicted that areas of similarity between L1 and L2 would be easily acquired . Indeed , I had no problem learning the Persian pronoun system since like in Greek it makes a similar distinction between second person singular and second person plural -LRB- esi - eseis in Greek , to somã in Persian -RRB- . Moreover , the adjectives in Persian are pluralized -LRB- xub - xuban = good -RRB- and so are in Greek -LRB- kalos - kaloi = good -RRB- . Last but not least , most of Ancient Greek verbs form their tenses by adding a prefix to the stem -LRB- feugo-efiga = leave-left -RRB- . Similarly , the Present Tense of Persian verb `` to know '' = `` dunestan '' is formed by the addition of a prefix -LRB- mi + dun + am = I know -RRB- . All these similarities confirmed what CA claims about positive transfer . Also its claims about negative transfer were partly verified as certain elements that were different from L1 caused me difficulty and led me to the production of errors . Specifically , one area of difficulty based on my expectations was the acquisition of the Persian pronunciation system . Actually , I made many mistakes by transferring L1 sounds to L2 system . For example , I kept on mispronouncing the long vowel / ã / in `` salãm '' as Greek lacks long vowels . I also made many mistakes in words with / g , h , q / sounds as these are not part of the Greek pronunciation system . In addition , the rule for adjective-noun combinations in Persian caused me problems . In Persian adjectives follow the nouns -LRB- eg . film-e-xub -RRB- whereas in Greek they precede them -LRB- eg oraia tainia = good film -RRB- . As a result , I would often misplace the adjectives transferring the L1 rule into Persian . Furthermore , I made many mistakes when learning the numbers . Persian numbers from 21 onwards are formed by adding ` o ' = ` and ' between numbers , whereas in Greek all numbers form one word e.g. `` 21 = ïkosiena '' . So instead of saying ` bist o yek ' = 21 , I kept on saying ` yekbist ' . However as it was expected , not all my difficulties were predicted by contrastive analysis . For example , the rule for the construction of the plural of nouns , although similar to Greek , confused me as I thought that Persian had different endings for animate and inanimate nouns . Yet , the only distinction that Persian makes is when animate nouns are used in formal contexts where suffix / ãn / is added instead of the suffix / hã / . So the errors I made by applying different endings to animate and inanimate nouns were developmental and had nothing to do with L1 influence . Furthermore , `` transfer errors do not always occur when they are predicted to occur '' -LRB- Ellis , 1997 , p. 52 -RRB- . For example , although prepositions were not similar to any prepositions in my native language , yet I had no problem learning them by employing certain strategies . Also , definite articles in Persian follow the direct objects eg . `` sãndevic rã '' = `` the sandwich '' whereas in Greek they precede them . However , I had no difficulty internalizing the rule because `` when something in the L2 is very different from the L1 , there is a ` novelty effect ' '' -LRB- cited in Gass & Selinker , 2001 , p. 126 -RRB- . In conclusion , I believe that the closeness or distance of L1 and L2 can lead to positive or negative transfer irrespectively . In my opinion , L1 knowledge should not be treated as a barrier as not all errors are connected to it . On the contrary , it is one of the key issues that theories of SLA have to address apart from the role of input and output that will be now analyzed . Another element that gives us insight to SLA besides the role of L1 is the role of input . According to Ellis -LRB- 1997 -RRB- , `` input -LRB- ... -RRB- is the samples of language to which a learner is exposed '' -LRB- p. 5 -RRB- . Behaviorism , developed in 1950-60 's views input as a kind of stimulus learners have to respond to . As Lightbrown & Spada -LRB- 1999 -RRB- suggest , `` learners receive linguistic input from speakers in the environment and they form ` associations ' between words and objects or events '' -LRB- p. 35 -RRB- . Teachers then reinforce learners ' production with corrective feedback on their errors . Every lesson is organized around a dialogue followed by drilling activities on the structures initiated in it -LRB- Ellis , 1997 , pp. 129-30 -RRB- . Thus learners have to memorize sentence-length grammar , vocabulary and pronunciation . Dialogues offer comprehensible input but drilling does not invite free conversation . In conclusion , this method does not really provide adequate input for L2 acquisition . Cognitivism , being a more recent theory of SLA and one developed by the work of psychologists and psycholinguists addresses the role of input differently . As McLaughlin -LRB- 1987 -RRB- states , `` second - language learning is viewed as the acquisition of a complex cognitive skill '' -LRB- p. 133 -RRB- . Cognitivists who have introduced the notion of ` enhanced input ' claim that humans have two types of memory : a working memory and a long-term memory . So input can pass from the working memory to the long-term one through rehearsal and associations . In addition , `` noticing -LRB- ... -RRB- is necessary and sufficient for converting input into intake '' -LRB- cited in Long , 1996 , p. 426 -RRB- . Finally , `` enhanced L2 input -LRB- ... -RRB- makes subtle L2 features more salient for learners , -LSB- and -RSB- -LRB- ... -RRB- -LSB- helps -RSB- learners identify which forms can occur in the L2 and which can not '' -LRB- cited in Pica , 1994 , p. 502 -RRB- . Creative Construction Theory on the other hand , views input as a source . As Pica -LRB- 1994 -RRB- suggests , `` exposure to L2 input is not sufficient for learners to be able to access and internalize the L2 rules , forms , and features '' -LRB- p. 500 -RRB- . According to Krashen -LRB- 1982 -RRB- , `` we acquire by understanding language that contains structure a bit beyond our current level of competence -LRB- i + 1 -RRB- '' -LRB- p. 21 -RRB- . In this case , our knowledge of L1 , of the world and the environment helps us towards it . But what happens if the message is not picked up by the learner ? If this is so , then acquisition will not occur -LRB- Krashen , 1982 , p. 63 -RRB- . So , his Input Hypothesis does not seem completely accurate either . The optimal conditions for input have not yet been met and so it is worthwhile considering what other theorists of Creative Construction theory -LRB- who have slightly different ideas about input -RRB- claim . As Lightbrown & Spada -LRB- 1999 -RRB- support , `` some interactionist theorists -LRB- ... -RRB- have argued that much second language acquisition takes place through conversational interaction '' -LRB- p. 43 -RRB- . So comprehensible input is not the only prerequisite for acquisition to occur -LRB- Ellis , 1999 , p. 112 ; Long , 1996 , p. 423 -RRB- . Learners , as is very often the case , understand the input but do not acquire it . So what these linguists have proposed instead is the internationally modified input . According to Lightbrown & Spada -LRB- 1999 -RRB- , `` modified input -LRB- ... -RRB- is sometimes called foreigner talk or teacher talk '' -LRB- p. 34 -RRB- . Therefore , teachers do not just simplify input but elaborate on it , paraphrase it and add further contextual cues to make it comprehensible . Learners interact with each other and gradually develop fluency by constantly negotiating for meaning . The previous section provided a brief literary review of the role of input in SLA . This part however , refers to the relation between the input I got and my learning of Persian . To start with , the input from my Persian course , based on Grammar Translation , had no relation to any current method . Every chapter started with a dialogue which included the English translation below it . Vocabulary was presented in lists of isolated words -LRB- English - Persian -RRB- and exercises aiming at immediate production were given for practice . For example , a common type of activity was `` Can you say these in Persian ? '' . However , as Krashen -LRB- 1982 -RRB- claims , `` speaking fluency can not be taught directly '' -LRB- p. 22 -RRB- . Also , grammar points were introduced with a minimum explanation and exercises on speaking and writing immediately followed . At the end of each chapter there was always a comprehension activity for practice of writing , after listening to the dialogue in the tape . Moreover , as Johnson -LRB- 2001 -RRB- maintains , `` the most obvious characteristic -LSB- of CA -RSB- -LRB- ... -RRB- is the lack of authenticity '' -LRB- p. 165 -RRB- . Thus , the course-book design was not helpful at all as each chapter provided too much information that made acquisition extremely difficult . Furthermore , the comprehension activities did not help me to pronounce the Persian words correctly or even remember them as the tapes went too fast and I had not time to listen to them . Although the dialogues were good introductory activities yet they included many unknown and formulaic words and I was not able to understand them without looking at the translation . I also found rather hard to memorize vocabulary while keeping pronunciation alive in memory . Moreover , the grammatical activities were very few and I had to create my own in order to practice the new grammar points . In general , the book did not provide practice in all four skills , lacked an adequate number of activities and was not L1 supported . Overall , I felt I had been given a book that could not really offer me what I wanted and therefore I had to struggle and work things out myself . At that point I realized what kind of input I lacked and I would like to be given . Firstly , I needed a good course-book accompanied by tapes , a companion and a grammar book . In addition , I wanted a teacher to help me learn the language by providing feedback and explaining difficult points . Realia and audiovisual material would definitely be helpful in grammar and vocabulary teaching . Drilling , repetition and interaction with other students would increase my fluency . According to Krashen -LRB- 1982 -RRB- , `` natural , communicative -LRB- ... -RRB- input has -LRB- ... -RRB- real advantages '' -LRB- p. 25 -RRB- On the whole , a variety of different types of input and practice of all 4 skills would help me learn Persian provided that the teacher knew my needs very well and was able to modify the materials according to them . In conclusion , I believe that there is no best way of acquiring input as people have different learning styles . However , one can easily tell the weaknesses of certain types of input such as the one provided in Grammar Translation method . In general , teachers must find ways to know their learners ' learning styles and adjust their way of teaching to them . Only then will input truly facilitate learning . Up to this point the essay has analyzed the role of L1 and input in SLA . However , there is one final aspect that needs to be examined and this is the role of output . To begin with , Behaviorism gave great emphasis on learners ' immediate production . According to Gass & Selinker -LRB- 2001 -RRB- , `` output has traditionally been viewed as a way of practicing what has previously been learned '' -LRB- p. 277 -RRB- . So learners form habits when they respond to stimuli in the environment by imitation , drilling and repetition . The focus is not on meaning but on pattern practice and `` the goal is the memorization of the dialogue '' -LRB- Krashen , 1982 , p. 131 -RRB- . However , this kind of output does not promote acquisition but just the learning of desired responses . According to McLaughlin -LRB- 1987 -RRB- , `` second-language learning -LRB- ... -RRB- involves the gradual integration of sub-skills as controlled processes initially predominate and then become automatic '' -LRB- p. 139 -RRB- . So the learner is able to produce language when he converts '' ` knowledge about ' into ` knowledge how to ' ; -LSB- when -RSB- the knowledge , in other words , becomes proceduralized or automatized '' -LRB- Johnson , 2001 , p. 108 -RRB- . In addition , repetition , memorization and creation of associations are some of the learning strategies that allow knowledge to pass into the long-term memory and thus become part of the learner 's interlanguage . Therefore , only in performance and production can one achieve acquisition . Conclusively and before proceeding to the analysis of output in relation to my learning experience it is worthwhile looking at what Constructivist theory believes about its role . Creative Construction Theory looks at output from two different perspectives . The first view considers output as the product of the acquired system . According to Krashen -LRB- 1982 -RRB- , `` we acquire spoken fluency not by practicing talking but by understanding input , by listening and reading '' -LRB- p. 60 -RRB- . So understanding of message is needed for the acquisition of L2 forms and structures -LRB- Pica , 1994 , p. 500 -RRB- . Therefore , what carries primary importance is not the learners ' output but their exposure to comprehensible input . However , other constructivists attribute a more significant role to output . For them , `` comprehensible output refers to the need for a learner to be ` pushed toward the delivery of a message that is not only conveyed , but that is conveyed precisely , coherently , and appropriately '' -LRB- quoted in Gass & Selinker , 2001 , p. 278 -RRB- . In other words , output is not merely seen as the result of the acquired system but as a necessary condition for L2 mastery . In addition , it can raise learners ' awareness of their errors and gaps in their learning -LRB- Ellis , 1997 , p. 49 -RRB- . So when learners speak or write they are not only able to interpret meaning but they also manage to process its syntactic structure . Finally , attention and comprehension of form in L2 input plays a more significant role in SLA than was considered -LRB- Pica , 1994 , p. 501 -RRB- . Personally I consider output as a useful tool for retention , motivation and mastery of L2 . Teachers should always emphasize the role of input in relation to output that learners need to produce . Mere repetition and memorization of forms and meanings should not be the goal but rather the very first stage in achieving fluency in all four skills . Comprehension , practice , modification , correction , reinforcement , motivation , teacher instruction and application of the correct learning strategies can then make someone a competent user of language . Finally , learners should not be pushed into immediate production nor should they be left with too much time before speaking . For me fluency in oral production is indeed a very high and difficult target yet one of the most important . Knowledge of rules and words alone does not help me towards production . What I need is practice , talking to myself , to my fellow students , to the teacher and to native speakers . Besides , according to Krashen -LRB- 1982 -RRB- , `` output aids learning because it provides a domain for error correction '' -LRB- p. 61 -RRB- . So I believe that this practice needs to be an integral part of every lesson in SL , even of the very early ones . Gradually , little by little and with the teacher 's help everybody can become a fluent speaker and thus master an L2 . However , when these conditions do not apply , acquisition does not occur ; and this can be confirmed by my attempt to learn Persian . As the conditions were not ideal -LRB- lack of teacher , lack of a real classroom situation , lack of authentic material -RRB- the initial motivation I had in learning a new language and the strategies I employed in order to achieve this goal did not help me towards it . The language -LRB- spoken and written -RRB- I produced was just mere repetition of certain formulaic expressions such as ` salãm ' , ` sob -LRB- h -RRB- bexeir ' and other types of greetings and expressions of introducing myself and asking about one 's health . Unfortunately I was not able to form a complete sentence without looking at the dialogue ; and as more and more elements were introduced , my performance kept on deteriorating as I could not catch up with the material . The same disappointing results were brought in surface after speaking with a native speaker of Persian . Although I had made a good revision of all the chapters , I had written down certain expressions and practiced them with my flat-mates the previous day I was only able to use the easiest expressions I had learnt in the very first lessons . What is more , she spoke rather fast and this did not help me understand everything . I felt very anxious when she asked me whether I had any brothers . The previous night I had learned the expression -LRB- `` I have two brothers who are older than me '' = `` do barãdari , mardi az man dãri '' -RRB- but she did not understand it and so she asked me if they were younger or older than me . However as I did not know these adjectives I was not able to say anything and silence prevailed for some seconds . In conclusion , now that I flick through the pages again and try to do the activities I face even more problems , as I have forgotten most of it . So I think that the secret of success is constant contact and practice . To conclude , this paper has examined the role that L1 , input and output play in theories of SLA and has shown how these elements can explain certain features of my learning of Persian . Generally , the acquisition of a second language is a very complex issue and its discussion includes a lot of parameters . In particular , some of these parameters such as the role of instruction , motivation , aptitude and learning strategies were not examined in the essay and thus constitute part of its limitations . Besides , reference was made only to some theories of SLA and not to others which is another limitation of the analysis . However , apart from these limitations the essay provides a good understanding of second language learning . This understanding according to Brown -LRB- 2000 -RRB- , `` will determine your teaching style , your approach , methods , and classroom techniques '' -LRB- p. 7 -RRB- .
Discourse Analysis looks at language in terms of social interaction . According to Michael McCarthy -LRB- 1991 -RRB- , `` discourse analysis is a vast subject area within linguistics , encompassing -LSB- ... -RSB- the analysis of spoken and written language over and above concerns such as the structure of the clause or sentence '' -LRB- p. 32 -RRB- . However , this paper focuses only on written discourse by analysing a text based on two main approaches : cohesion and thematization . Particularly , the discussion begins with a brief outline of the context of the sample , followed by an exposition of the two approaches based on background literature . Then , it moves on to a detailed analysis of the text and finally concludes with the limitations of the analysis . To begin with , the features that are important with regards to the context of discourse are the following : channel , formality , purpose , relationship between the addressor and the addressee , topic and setting . Here , the channel is written and has the form of a formal letter including the addresses ' full address , the date , and the formulaic opening and closing expressions : `` Dear Mr Miller '' and `` Yours Sincerely '' . However , it is a genuine charity letter signed by a well-known game show host celebrity , aiming to personalize it and sensitise the addressee to provide financial help to the leukaemic children . What is striking about its format is that it has a title and a subtitle accompanied by a visual image of two children . The title is in bold letters that easily catch the reader 's attention , whereas the words `` children '' and `` leukaemia '' are capitalized , stressing the children 's difficult state . The subtitle `` inaugurated by Princess '' gives value to those people 's work and the phrase `` no 298405 '' reveals that it is really a general letter sent to many people , yet easy to become personalized by the addition of the addressee 's name . Finally , the names given in the right column , as well as the `` P.S. '' and the sketch next to the signature add to the letter 's general purpose of persuasion . There are many texts that , although they are grammatically correct , are not cohesive . Such texts lack connectivity between sequences and so readers face difficulty in deciphering their meaning . As McCarthy -LRB- 1991 -RRB- supports , `` English offers a -LSB- ... -RSB- set of options for creating surface links between the clauses and sentences of a text otherwise known as cohesion '' -LRB- p. 25 -RRB- . In addition , Halliday and Hasan 's -LRB- 1976 -RRB- system -LRB- as cited in Hatch , 1992 , p. 223 -RRB- `` distinguishes five major types of grammatical cohesive ties : reference , substitution , ellipsis , conjunction , and lexical ties . '' These ties as Brown and Yule -LRB- 1983 -RRB- acknowledge : `` bind a text together '' -LRB- p. 191 -RRB- . As Thompson -LRB- 1996 -RRB- points out , `` reference is a set of grammatical recourses that allow the speaker to indicate whether something is being repeated from somewhere earlier in the text '' -LRB- p. 180 -RRB- . It is binary : `` the first -LSB- ... -RSB- is known as exophoric -LRB- ` pointing outwards ' -RRB- whereas the second is anaphoric -LRB- ` pointing inwards ' -RRB- -LRB- Thompson , 1996 , p. 181 -RRB- . The second cohesive tie is ellipsis , where `` a clause -LSB- ... -RSB- may be presupposed at a subsequent place in the text by the device of positive omission '' -LRB- Halliday , 1985 , p. 288 -RRB- . The third , on the other hand , is substitution that takes place when `` an expression may simply be replaced by another in the text '' -LRB- Brown and Yule , 1983 , p. 201 -RRB- . The fourth type of cohesive tie is conjunction . According to Hatch -LRB- 1992 -RRB- , `` here are several different kinds of conjunctive relations : additive -LSB- ... -RSB- , adversative -LSB- ... -RSB- , causal -LSB- ... -RSB- and temporal '' -LRB- p. 225 -RRB- . The last type of cohesion is lexical cohesion . According to Brown and Yule -LRB- 1983 -RRB- , `` cohesion may be derived from lexical relationships like hyponymy -LSB- ... -RSB- , part-whole -LSB- ... -RSB- , collocability -LSB- ... -RSB- , clausal substitution -LSB- ... -RSB- , comparison -LSB- ... -RSB- and by syntactic repetition '' -LRB- p. 194 -RRB- . Before proceeding to the analysis of the text based on cohesion , it is necessary to mention the reasons for selecting it . Firstly , as it is clear by the pertinent review , many linguists have studied this approach and perceive it as one of the main approaches for analysing written discourse . Furthermore , cohesion offers an insight into the meaning relations between the various elements of a text , as knowledge of the grammatical relations between words is not enough . Finally , according to Halliday -LRB- 1985 -RRB- , `` in order to construct discourse we need to be able to establish -LSB- ... -RSB- relations that may involve elements of any extent -LSB- ... -RSB- -LRB- and -RRB- this can not be achieved by grammatical structure '' -LRB- p. 288 -RRB- . The forms , which according to Brown and Yule -LRB- 1983 -RRB- `` direct the hearer or reader to look elsewhere for their interpretation '' -LRB- p. 192 -RRB- , are exophoric . For instance , phrases such as `` seller and buyer '' -LRB- l. 14 -RRB- , `` life '' -LRB- l. 1 -RRB- do not refer to another item inside the text . On the contrary , they point to the outside world or make reference to things or people that are assumed as known by the addressee . Also the personal pronoun `` I '' -LRB- l. 4 -RRB- has an exophoric use as according to Thompson -LRB- 1996 -RRB- , '' ` I ' means the person who is speaking now in the real world ' rather than the person just referred to in the text ' '' -LRB- p. 181 -RRB- . The other type of reference is endophoric . According to Thompson -LRB- 1996 -RRB- , `` most cohesive endophoric reference is anaphoric -LRB- ` pointing backwards ' -RRB- '' -LRB- p. 181 -RRB- . In this case , the cohesive ties point ahead so that a word or phrase is not specified until further on in the text . For instance , `` child-killer disease '' -LRB- l. 4 -RRB- points forwards to leukaemia -LRB- l. 4 -RRB- . Instances of verbal groups such as `` my friends -LSB- are working -RSB- '' -LRB- l. 7 -RRB- , `` perhaps -LSB- will -RSB- a friend -LSB- ... ? -RSB- '' -LRB- l. 10 -RRB- are also ellipses from the text . To begin with , several words are repeated : `` found '' -LRB- ll . 5 , 6 -RRB- and `` answers '' -LRB- ll . 13 , 15 -RRB- . Thematization , focusing on the order of information in a sentence , is a different way of analysing a written text . The theme can be considered as the point of departure of the message -LRB- Halliday , 1985 , p. 38 -RRB- . It can also be seen as that `` which locates and orients the clause within its own context '' -LRB- as cited in Thompson , 1996 , p. 143 -RRB- . Halliday -LRB- 1985 -RRB- goes even further to provide a more extended definition of theme stating that '' the theme of any clause , -LSB- ... -RSB- extends up to -LSB- and includes -RSB- the topical Theme '' -LRB- p. 56 -RRB- . There are several types of themes such as : subject or adjunct themes , thematizing structures and predicated themes . A theme can have three parts : a textual , an interpersonal and an ideational one . According to Matthiessen & Painter -LRB- 1997 -RRB- , `` the textual and interpersonal stages to the Theme may or may not be present '' -LRB- p. 24 -RRB- . When they are present the order is `` textual + interpersonal + ideational '' -LRB- McCarthy , 1983 , p. 58 -RRB- . Also a theme can have three different statuses : unmarked -LRB- following a normal word order -RRB- , marked -LRB- where something is put forward into the slot -RRB- , and enhanced in thematizing structures . The rheme , on the other hand , is the comment : `` what the speaker states about , or in regard to , the starting point of the utterance '' -LRB- as cited in Brown & Yule , 1983 , pp. 126-7 -RRB- . As Thompson -LRB- 1996 -RRB- suggests , '' the rheme provides the content , the main information that the writer or speaker wants the addressee to know '' -LRB- p. 165 -RRB- . The rheme of the first sentence organizes the progression of topics in the structure of the text . In general , the linking between theme and rheme helps the writer to explain the topic organization . First and foremost , the progression of a text is critically dependent upon the writer 's thematic choices . So , any changes in them , signal a change in his perspective and way of looking at the text . Therefore , it is extremely important for a reader to know how to analyse a text in terms of this approach , in order to understand and evaluate the writer 's ideas . Also , as Halliday -LRB- 1985 -RRB- reports , '' the choice of clause Themes -LSB- ... -RSB- constitutes what is often known as ` the method of development ' of the text '' -LRB- p. 62 -RRB- . As a conclusion , `` analysing the thematic structure of a text gives us an insight into its texture '' -LRB- Halliday , 1985 , p. 67 -RRB- . The text is divided into four paragraphs . The theme of the first paragraph introduces the topic of the whole text . The phrase `` life ... show '' -LRB- l. 1 -RRB- links the whole paragraph back to the concept of life with its unanswered questions that make us wonder . The first rheme , in addition , completes the theme by commenting on life 's unexpected nature . In this rheme , there is a sub-theme `` we '' and a sub-rheme `` still ... answers '' -LRB- ll . 2,3 -RRB- . The second sentence , however , has a more specific theme , which initiates the problem of leukaemia , whereas the rheme analyses its seriousness . Therefore , the writer makes a general comment by setting the frame of his topic and now moves on in its analysis . In the second paragraph , in contrast , there is a change of topic from `` life '' to `` I '' . Here , the addressor expresses his awareness that there are people who work hard to find a cure . The transition in the development of the text is very smooth and the clauses are nicely linked together . The first rheme , `` my friends '' -LRB- l. 7 -RRB- is picked up by the next theme , emphasizing people 's work in that institute . Picked up theme as Lock -LRB- 1996 -RRB- claims , '' is a thematized constituent -LSB- ... -RSB- later picked up by a pronoun in an unmarked position in the clause '' -LRB- p. 227 -RRB- . The rheme following it , consisting of many sub-themes , comments on people 's need for financial help . In the third paragraph , and having stated the situation , the author moves on to explain the purpose of his letter . The theme introduces the topic of the paragraph , which is about persuading the addressee to sell the tickets . The rheme is part of an interrogative clause . Since it is a yes/no question , according to Halliday -LRB- 1985 -RRB- , `` the element that functions as Theme is the element that embodies the expression of polarity '' -LRB- p. 47 -RRB- . In addition , the next clause provides information about the prizes . The theme changes , whereas the rheme is picked up by the previous one . Finally , the paragraph ends with a complex clause , where theme and rheme are picked up from the previous clauses , emphasizing the writer 's request . In conclusion , the last paragraph states the benefits that will arise from selling the tickets . It starts with a picked up multiple theme , `` By ... you '' -LRB- l. 18 -RRB- , followed by a rheme : `` will ensure ... treatments '' -LRB- ll . 18-21 -RRB- that expresses a causal-conditional relationship to it , and is later picked up by the next theme -LRB- l. 21 -RRB- . Here , the letter reaches its conclusion . Thus , the writer manages to state the problem , develop his argumentation and express his request in a rather convincing way . -LRB- For further evidence see Appendix 2 -RRB- . However , there is another related approach to discourse analysis that this paper does not refer to , called clause relationships . This approach constitutes a first limitation of this study . The notion of syllable is one of the key issues in Phonetics and Phonology as it determines the structure and comprehensible production of speech . Its significance has led many linguists to develop several theories for its structure such as : onset - rhyme , mora theory , body - coda organization and flat structure among others . The purpose of the essay is firstly to provide a critical evaluation of the system for syllable structure presented in Roach -LRB- 2000 -RRB- by comparing it to flat structure , and secondly to discuss the importance of a good understanding of syllable and its structure in pronunciation teaching in a language classroom situation . There are two main patterns governing the syllable structure : a -RRB- sonority hierarchy and b -RRB- phonotactics . To start with , every syllable contains a vowel which is the most sonorous element -LRB- Katamba , 1989 , p. 169 -RRB- and which can be preceded or followed by consonants according to the sonority hierarchy . As it is stated `` less sonorous sounds preceding the nucleus are assigned to the initial C - element -LRB- ... -RRB- and those following it are assigned to the other C - element '' -LRB- Katamba , 1989 , p. 159 -RRB- . Apart from this pattern , syllables are governed by a set of phonological consonants which determine `` the possible combinations of phonemes '' -LRB- Roach , 2000 , p. 71 -RRB- . However , different linguists find different sets of restrictions underlying the syllable and thus form different theories about its structure . One such theory is flat structure . Its basic implication is that `` each of the segments is an immediate constituent of the syllable node , with no intervening nodes '' -LRB- Ewen & Hulst , 2001 , p. 129 -RRB- . Its proponents have observed that the vowel can be easily attached to the preceding consonants as well as to the following ones . However , instead of favoring one aspect against the other they claim that `` co-occurrence restrictions holding between the nucleus and preceding elements of the syllable -LSB- are -RSB- as common as co-occurrence restrictions holding between the nucleus and the following elements '' -LRB- Clements & Keyser , 1983 , p. 20 -RRB- . -LRB- For more reference see Appendix -RRB- . Overall , this view has been very strongly criticized by other theories such as the Onset Rhyme theory that is analyzed next . According to this theory the consonants occurring before the vowel -LRB- nucleus -RRB- are called onset whereas those coming after it are called coda . Besides , the restrictions between peak and coda are noticeably more common -LRB- Fudge , 1987 , p. 361 -RRB- and the grouping of `` peak & coda together -LSB- is called -RSB- -LRB- ... -RRB- the rhyme of the syllable '' -LRB- Giegerich , 1992 , p. 138 -RRB- . In fact , most linguists seem to coincide to this view and elaborate on it . Besides , as Clements and Keyser -LRB- 1983 -RRB- admit , `` a few of these constraints could , perhaps , be regarded as accidental gaps '' -LRB- p. 21 -RRB- . Roach -LRB- 2000 -RRB- develops a system for analyzing the possible combinations of English phonemes . He starts by describing the segments that can be part of the onset : a -RRB- `` zero onset '' , -LRB- ... -RRB- b -RRB- `` one consonant '' -LRB- ... -RRB- and c -RRB- `` consonant cluster '' -LRB- p. 71 -RRB- and then moves on to describe the coda and its constituents parts : a -RRB- `` zero coda '' , -LRB- ... -RRB- b -RRB- `` final consonant '' , -LRB- ... -RRB- and c -RRB- `` consonant final cluster '' -RRB- -LRB- p. 73 -RRB- . Finally , he presents the syllable structure according to Onset - Rhyme theory . Overall his system is very clearly structured and easily understood . The presentation starts from the onset where every single possibility is taken into consideration . After the analysis of simple onsets he discusses more complex ones by providing the possible combinations of clusters in a table . Then , following the same structure , he cites the coda combinations through examples and tables . So , one can easily follow his interpretation by looking carefully at the analysis . This analysis is more refined compared to Harris ' -LRB- 1994 -RRB- where neither tables nor examples are provided thus making it hard for the reader to go through . Furthermore , Roach -LRB- 2000 -RRB- does not present his system as the most accurate one , but acknowledges its limitations . Thus although he makes no distinction between onset and rhyme components in his system , he refers to onset-rhyme theory , by calling it `` a more refined analysis of the syllable '' -LRB- p. 76 -RRB- . Also , he recognizes and records other linguists ' proposal about syllable structure as for example the idea that `` since post-initial j only occurs before , u : and -LRB- ... -RRB- one can postulate a diphthong j and remove j from post-initial position '' -LRB- Roach , 2000 , p. 79 -RRB- . However apart from these strengths his system has certain weaknesses that will now be examined . Firstly , the terminology -LRB- pre-initial , initial , post-final 1 ... post-final 3 -RRB- he uses is rather confusing for the reader and causes several problems . Specifically , by treating pre-initial / s / as part of the onset and post-final / s / as part of the coda correspondingly , he debars the sonority hierarchy . Particularly , pre-initial / s / is more sonorous than the following / p , t , k / consonants and `` violates the generalization that onset sequences increase in sonority '' -LRB- Giegerich , 1992 , p. 139 -RRB- . On the other hand and according to Harris -LRB- 1994 -RRB- , `` segments decrease in sonority the further they occur from the nucleus '' -LRB- p. 56 -RRB- . Yet , post-final / s / is more sonorous than the preceding / p , t , k / post-final sounds . Therefore , a proposal made by Ewen and Hulst -LRB- 2001 -RRB- is to consider pre-initial / s / as separate from the onset and treat it as prependix whereas post-final / s / as an appendix -LRB- p. 138 -RRB- . Finally , another idea is that `` sp , st , sk could be treated as individual phonemes , removing the pre-initial position from the syllable onset '' -LRB- cited in Roach , 2000 , p. 79 -RRB- . Secondly , although English distinguishes between long vowels , short vowels and diphthongs , Roach 's main interest is not in the possible nucleus positions but in the sounds occurring under onset and coda categories only . However , this distinction is important and needs to be shown in the syllable structure . In addition , according to Ewen and Hulst -LRB- 2001 -RRB- , `` long vowels -LRB- ... -RRB- occupy two positions in the rhyme , and as such have exactly the same structure as diphthongs '' -LRB- pp. 132-3 -RRB- . Finally as Harris -LRB- 1994 -RRB- supports , `` the difference between branching and non branching nuclei is the formal expression of a contrast between long and short vowels '' -LRB- p. 65 -RRB- . After examining the syllable structure at a theoretical level , the paper moves on to discuss the importance of a good understanding of syllable and syllable structure in pronunciation teaching . Initially , the understanding of syllable and syllable structure is necessary when teaching the rhythm of speech as according to Ladefoged -LRB- 2001 -RRB- , `` there are -LRB- ... -RRB- cases where people disagree on how many syllables there are in a word in English '' -LRB- p. 244 -RRB- . So teachers have to indicate to learners that English is a stress-timed language -LRB- Celce-Murcia , Brinton & Goodwin , 1996 p. 154 -RRB- . One of their priorities is to teach the distinction between stressed and unstressed syllables highlighting the fact that `` stressed syllables -LRB- ... -RRB- have higher pitch and longer duration than their non-stressed counterparts '' -LRB- Katamba , 1989 , p. 221 -RRB- . Finally , they must clearly indicate which words in a sentence tend to receive stress because students usually place stress in each single word and this hinders comprehensibility . Furthermore , a thorough knowledge of syllable and its structure helps teachers to identify possible problems with consonant clusters -LRB- Cruttenden , 1997 , p. 222 -RRB- . Based on their familiarity with the learners ' native language , they can easily explain all the possible and impossible combinations of clusters and predict learners ' errors . As a matter of fact , different languages allow different consonant clustering so the best piece of advice for teachers is to start `` with the presentation of initial clusters because they tend to be easier for students to produce '' -LRB- Celce-Murcia et al. , 1996 , p. 85 -RRB- . Finally , their role is to raise learners ' awareness of the differences and similarities in consonant clustering between English and their native language through examples and lots of practice . Apart from that , the importance of syllable structure is evident when teaching vowel sounds . In this case , teachers must clarify the distinction between diphthongs , long and short vowels in the pronunciation of words by considering branching and non-branching nuclei . However , as not all languages make this distinction , learners of English often make errors leading to unsuccessful communication . For example , `` when a learner says -LRB- ... -RRB- soap in a -LRB- ... -RRB- restaurant where they should have said soup , the inaccurate production can lead to misunderstanding '' -LRB- Kelly , 2000 , p. 11 -RRB- . Therefore , teachers need to illustrate for learners how these vowel sounds can differentiate between word meanings . So theoretical knowledge of syllable structure will allow them to teach these units by designing activities on minimal pairs and drilling . Before this paper reaches its conclusion , a connection between the importance of syllable structure in pronunciation teaching and my learning and limited teaching experience needs to be made . To start with , I had been taught English as a foreign language for seven years in Greece but pronunciation was never part of the lesson . Some teachers considered it insignificant , whereas others had inadequate knowledge of it . One of my most vivid memories is that of my best teacher saying `` do not try to divide syllables '' , as she considered the rule for syllable division very difficult to teach . When I entered the department of English language and literature in the university and I took the module `` teaching the pronunciation of English '' , I realized the importance of a good understanding of pronunciation for the learner and the teacher as well . I have only taught to beginners for a limited period of time but yet I have identified problems with teaching the pronunciation of long vowels and consonant clusters . For example , as Greek lacks longs vowels my students mispronounced words such as `` heart , pool '' . With regards to consonant clusters they mispronounced clusters such as `` nd and mb '' . According to Dalton and Seidhofer -LRB- 1994 -RRB- , `` Greek speakers tend to simplify final nd , mb , and ng , by dropping the nasal consonant , so that `` bend '' becomes `` bed '' , and `` send '' may be misheard as `` sed '' '' -LRB- p. 119 -RRB- . Overall , we tried to overcome the errors by introducing activities on minimal pairs , drilling and other similar ones . In conclusion , the first half of this paper provided a critical evaluation of Roach 's system for syllable structure , whereas the second half presented the importance of syllable and its structure in pronunciation teaching . However , one of its basic limitations is that there was no analysis of other theories of syllable structure such as mora theory or body-coda organization that could give us a better insight to syllable structure and a more critical view to Roach 's system . Furthermore , the reference to the pedagogic implications of syllable and its structure was limited to the teaching of stress , vowels and consonant clusters . However , the importance of syllable could also be seen in teaching intonation and syllabic consonants . Nevertheless , the essay has brought to the surface many interesting views about the syllable and syllable structure and thus leads to further investigation of similar issues one being the teaching of pronunciation : what it includes and how it can be succeeded .
Foreign language teaching is a broad field associated with many issues one of which being testing . Tests offer an insight to learners ' progress and give teachers the chance to assess their own success in the transfer of knowledge . So many researchers have worked on this issue by developing different approaches to testing as well as different types of tests . As testing has a crucial effect on teaching , teachers need to be sure that tests learners take are valid . Thus , every test should be related to a specific context as students ' needs and characteristics differ from one country to the other and even more specifically from one class to the other . Therefore , this paper aims to evaluate the reading part of FCE the `` most widely taken Cambridge EFL examination '' -LRB- UCLES , 2001 : 6 -RRB- in relation to a specific teaching situation . The evaluation following Weir 's framework for reading addresses the theory based , context and scoring validity . The analysis starts by setting the test to its context and then provides a brief literature review on reading and its testing before it proceeds to the discussion of the test 's validity in relation to the three aspects mentioned above . This section provides a brief overview of the basic concepts of language testing . At the outset , a variety of different types of tests has been developed over the past decades . The basic kinds are four : achievement , proficiency , diagnostic and placement tests . Achievement tests evaluate learners ' progress throughout a course -LRB- 0 ' Sullivan , 2005 : 2 -RRB- and are based on the material covered in it . On the contrary , proficiency tests are not related to a specific course but assess students ' general language ability . Diagnostic tests `` diagnose students ' strengths and weaknesses '' -LRB- Hughes , 1989 : 7 -RRB- whereas placement tests provide information about placing a learner to the appropriate class within a language teaching program -LRB- Johnson , 2001 : 293 -RRB- . Tests vary also in the format they follow , as they can measure directly or indirectly learners ' ability to perform a skill . Additionally , tasks involved can be `` discrete points -LRB- where each task involved the use of just one language element -RRB- or integrative '' -LRB- O ' Sullivan , 2005 : 9 -RRB- , whereas the scores can be marked objectively or subjectively . Additionally , results can be reported in different ways depending on the test 's purpose . Particularly , assessing reading is a rather difficult task as no one can really define what takes place in learners ' mind when reading a text . However , this paper evaluates the reading part of FCE exams since it is very challenging and interesting to see how people manage to construct a test of a traditionally considered ` receptive ' skill . Yet before referring to it , attention will be paid to the teaching of reading because as Alderson -LRB- 2001 : 31 -RRB- states : `` an understanding of the nature of reading is crucial to the development of our assessment instruments '' . So , during 1960 's where Audiolingualism was prominent , reading was only learned to assist spoken instruction -LRB- Grabe , 1991 : 376 -RRB- . A decade later , reading involved an active understanding of a wide range of texts that demanded the activation of various learning strategies -LRB- Grabe , 1991 : 377 -RRB- . Today , reading is not seen as a receptive skill but as one that engages the reader in an interaction with the text , enabling him to draw inferences by activating his `` relevant schemata '' -LRB- Ur , 1996 : 108 -RRB- . Additionally , this variety of reading texts should aim to `` engage -LSB- readers -RSB- in purposeful reading '' -LRB- Hedge , 2000 : 221 -RRB- . Similarly , work on the testing of reading until the early 1980 's focused on the formats used to test it -LRB- Urquhart & Weir , 1998 : 150 -RRB- and `` provided no information on learners ' ability to use language for communicative purposes '' -LRB- Brindley , 2001 : 139 -RRB- . However , current research focuses on direct tests which assess readers ' ability to `` understand texts in a range of different topics '' -LRB- Alderson , 2000 : 63 -RRB- . As every test should be immediately linked to a teaching context so FCE reading test addresses students at the intermediate level . Particularly , in Greek society where people start learning English from the age of 9 , FCE examinations constitute the first certificate that Greek students hold . English is being learned mainly for general academic knowledge , for job qualification , for traveling and for communicating with speakers of other languages . In this possible context which I will be teaching in the future , English is taught 6 hours a week at a private language school with the help of a textbook , past papers of FCE and blackboard . The class consists of 10 Greek mixed ability learners and the main aim is to teach them how to communicate adequately in all 4 skills outside class . More specific objectives concern the teaching of language items , functions and skills according to their level . All the research done in testing highlights its importance and immediate need in language teaching . Tests are taken to assess learners ' language abilities -LRB- Bachman , 1990 : 2 -RRB- , identify their strong and weak points and evaluate their overall progress -LRB- Bachman , 1990 : 3 -RRB- . So teachers can reflect on their teaching and make any changes if required . However , as teachers need to base their lesson on a plan , so test developers need to construct their tests following certain criteria with the most important being validity , the degree to which a test measures what it claims to measure -LRB- Popham , 1991 : 55 -RRB- or as the American Psychological Association -LRB- 1985 -RRB- supports : `` the extent to which inferences we make on the basis of test scores are meaningful , appropriate and useful '' -LRB- cited in Bachman , 1990 : 25 -RRB- . Many researchers have attempted to evaluate tests by constructing various frameworks since these depict closely what we aim to test -LRB- Weir , 1993 : 20 -RRB- . Therefore , this paper provides evidence for the three aspects of validity covered by the FCE exam : theory-based , context and scoring validity , following the complete and up-to-date Weir 's -LRB- 2005 -RRB- framework . The discussion starts by the presentation of the tables of the three validity aspects and continues with a detailed analysis of them . To begin with , all tests should be designed by keeping learners and their specific traits in mind . So the test taker table is linked to the theory-based validity one because these characteristics affect the way candidates process the test tasks -LRB- Weir , 2005 : 51 -RRB- . Test taker characteristics fall into three categories : physical , psychological and experiential . The physical characteristics concern short-term ailments such as toothache , or cold , longer-term disabilities in speaking , hearing or vision , the age and candidates ' sex . On the other hand , the topic of reading text should be interesting to learners and appropriate to their age and sex . Additionally , tests should allow for the provision of accommodation for students with disabilities as FCE does . If candidates have any longer-term disabilities they can ask for special arrangements to be made -LRB- UCLES , 2001 : 8 -RRB- . Particularly , students with visual difficulties can be given extra time to complete a paper or read the questions in a different way or write their answers using the Braille version , or be given the questions in enlarged papers . Finally , they can have a reader reading the questions out to the candidate -LRB- Cambridge ESOL , 08/04/2006 -RRB- . Special consideration is also offered to candidates `` affected by adverse circumstances before or during an examination '' -LRB- UCLES , 2001 : 8 -RRB- . Furthermore , the reading part of FCE exams caters for learners ' psychological characteristics by including texts from a wide range of sources -LRB- UCLES , 2001 : 10 -RRB- . Texts referring to activity holidays , to shopping or to someone 's experience of learning to fly `` motivate a deeper reading since they are linked to students ' academic knowledge and leisure interests '' -LRB- Alderson , 2000 : 29 -RRB- . So students ' schemata are activated and their varied language abilities are tested . Topics -LRB- ... -RRB- which might introduce a bias against any group of candidates -LRB- ... -RRB- are also avoided '' -LRB- UCLES , 2001 : 6 -RRB- . Finally , the test considering learners ' experiential traits ensures candidates ' familiarity with the exam format and the task types by the availability of coursebooks , practice materials , and past examination papers -LRB- UCLES , 2001 : 8 -RRB- . Moreover , the specification of the testers ' expectation leaves students with adequate time to prepare themselves for taking the test . So the test challenges learners ' reading abilities while catering for any special as well as general needs . This validity type refers to whether the cognitive processes needed to carry out the task are appropriate or not -LRB- O ' Sullivan , 2005 : 16 -RRB- . It is divided into executive processes -LRB- goal setting , visual recognition , pattern synthesizing -RRB- and resources -LRB- language and content knowledge -RRB- . The first element of executive processes provides candidates with a clear idea of the reading purpose so that they can employ the appropriate reading strategies -LRB- UCLES , 2001 : 10 -RRB- . Candidates `` are expected to -LRB- ... -RRB- read semi-authentic texts of various kinds -LRB- ... -RRB- and to show understanding of gist , detail , and text structure and deduce meaning '' -LRB- UCLES , 2001 : 7 -RRB- . The goals of the reading passages are also determined by the instructions provided in the rubric prior to each test . The first activity for instance asks learners to choose the most appropriate heading from a list -LRB- Appendix -RRB- . Visual recognition , the second element of theory - based validity , is ensured by the actual presentation of the test . The input is clearly printed and well-presented . The various parts are easily recognized as they are written in bold letters whereas overload of information is avoided so that the material can be legible . Moving on to the executive resources available to candidates , it should be noted that grammatical , textual , functional and sociolinguistic knowledge is demanded from them . Students ' textual knowledge is examined in part three of the test which `` tests understanding of how tests are structured and the ability to follow text development '' -LRB- UCLES , 2001 : 10 -RRB- . The test also assesses students ' understanding of the texts ' function and interpretation of their meaning . The second reading passage examines learners ' functional knowledge by asking them to answer questions regarding the opinions expressed in the text . So candidates can evaluate whether certain utterances are intended `` to convey ideas , manipulate , -LRB- ... -RRB- as well as understand indirect speech acts and pragmatic implications '' as Buck -LRB- 2001 : 104 quoted by Weir , 2005 : 97 -RRB- claims . Though there is no clear inference to any particular sociolinguistic group , the instructions really help candidates to use any sociolinguistic knowledge that might be applicable . So language testers can estimate more or less some of the knowledge students bring to the test . Students get information about the content of the test from the instructions , the title as well as the actual reading of it . For example , instructions in part 2 -LRB- Appendix -RRB- provide students with knowledge about the text . Additionally , by reading the passage students learn more about the relationship of the two men . The second major category of a test 's validity considers the task 's settings and linguistic demands as well as the issues related to the test 's administration . The test is divided into four different parts and the settings of each task are described in the Handbook -LRB- UCLES , 2001 -RRB- . Specifically , the main purpose of part one is to assess learners ' ability to search for main ideas -LRB- UCLES , 2001 : 10 -RRB- . Similarly , part 2 tests `` detailed understanding of a text '' -LRB- UCLES , 2001 : 10 -RRB- whereas part 3 assesses comprehension of the test 's structure -LRB- UCLES , 2001 : 10 -RRB- where students choose from a list of paragraphs which one fits each gap . Additionally , the assessment criteria are not applicable since none of the tasks requires detailed responses by the candidates . In contrast , all of them are different versions of multiple choice or matching items . The test also ensures that the weighting of each item is known to candidates as `` questions in parts 1 , 2 and 3 carry two marks -LSB- whereas -RSB- -LRB- ... -RRB- questions in part 4 carry one mark '' -LRB- UCLES , 2001 : 9 -RRB- . Specifically , as it is stated by UCLES -LRB- 2001 : 6 -RRB- `` after selection and editing , the items are compiled into pre-test papers '' . Moving on to the discussion of the texts ' length , it is obvious that the first passage is the shortest one whereas the third is the longest . Texts 2 and 4 on the other hand , have more or less the same length . As mentioned in the Handbook -LRB- UCLES , 2001 : 9 -RRB- each text has 350-700 words approximately and the whole test 1900-2300 words . Furthermore , the test format `` should specify test structure '' -LRB- Hughes , 1989 : 50 -RRB- which is in fact done in the handbook -LRB- UCLES , 2001 : 9 -RRB- . Apart from the third passage where students have to place the paragraphs of a jumbled text in order , all the other passages require multiple matching or multiple choice answers . Even though these techniques are objective yet the difficulty with the MCQ formats is that `` the tester does not know why the candidate responded the way she did '' -LRB- Alderson , 2000 : 212 -RRB- so they allow for the possibility of guessing . Finally the order of items should be carefully considered by the test . This test particularly separates into distinct parts the items focusing on different reading strategies . So it is evident from the test that scanning and skimming are tested in different texts so that learners do not get confused with the reading purpose and the task 's expectation . Apart from the consideration of the task 's characteristics , test developers specify the way it is administered . Particularly , they have created a team responsible for the administration of the test because `` if the test is not well administered , unreliable results may occur '' -LRB- Weir , 2005 : 82 -RRB- . So based on studies conducted by Cambridge ESOL -LRB- 08/04/2006 -RRB- examination centres are independent institutions which administer the examinations by following strict and detailed procedures . These centres are regularly inspected and exam invigilators are provided with all the administrative details concerning the existence of non-distressing physical conditions and the uniformity of administration so that testing conditions are the same for every testing centre . Security is also given a high priority . Cambridge ESOL -LRB- 08/04/2006 -RRB- also ensures that the examination dates are carefully selected and safety measures are taken at each stage of the examination process . As far as the linguistic demands of the task are concerned , the test ensures that all the resources in the text and those required by the candidates are a bit challenging but appropriate for their level . The vocabulary used , taken from traveling , flying and shopping is part of the `` language forms candidates -LRB- ... -RRB- -LSB- are -RSB- expected to handle '' -LRB- Weir , 2005 : 78 -RRB- . The syntactic structures used in the text are according to the grammar students were taught in the FCE published coursebooks . The functions students should know at this level are also stated in the Handbook . So , the test by assessing learners ' skimming and scanning abilities , examines what it claims to examine . Therefore it is up to students to practice adequately these strategies so that they do not meet any serious problems when taking the test . The discourse mode should also be appropriate for the skills and strategies being tested . Students should be able to read semi-authentic texts from magazine articles that they will be reading in their future target situations . Passages 3 and 4 are anonymous while the writer 's name in the first two passages is known to learners . However , the latter might allow for the possibility of learners ' familiarity with the writer which though would constitute an important part of their background knowledge , it might also increase bias against those candidates who are not familiar with the writer . So the test , in an attempt to keep a balance between the two , includes both anonymous and signed texts , taking into account the writer-reader relationship . Finally , the content knowledge required by students is appropriate for their level since the topics selected have wide appeal to heterogeneous group of students who are familiar to them and can activate their schemata . The last type of validity to be evaluated concerns the way grading and results are given and is the scoring validity . The first element , item analysis follows certain procedures for the `` investigating properties of test items -LRB- ... -RRB- prior to -LSB- the -RSB- development of their final format and content '' -LRB- Weir , 2005 : 202 -RRB- . So FCE measures items ' difficulty during the pre-testing phase . Test developers send the test to various centers and after these are completed by students they are returned to the `` Pretesting Section of UCLES EFL -LSB- where -RSB- -LRB- ... -RRB- the items are marked on and analyzed and those which are found to be suitable are banked '' -LRB- UCLES , 2001 : 6 -RRB- . Apart from it , the test 's internal consistency is evaluated and measured . Internal consistency is `` the extent to which individual items are functioning in about the same way '' -LRB- Popham , 1991 : 55 -RRB- . Particularly , Cambridge ESOL estimates it using a formula called `` Cronbach 's alpha '' -LRB- Weir , 2005 : 30 -RRB- . An example of such an analysis using this formula is depicted by Geranpayeh -LRB- 2004 : 21-22 -RRB- . Error of measurement on the other hand , is concerned with `` the difference between an observed score and the corresponding true score , or proficiency '' -LRB- Weir , 2005 : 204 -RRB- . Tiredness , lack of motivation and poor health are some of the obvious sources of errors of measurement since they influence candidates ' performance -LRB- Bachman , 1990 : 160 -RRB- . So FCE exam developers estimate the standard error of measurement based on a system that Luoma -LRB- 2004 -RRB- describes -LRB- cited in Weir , 2005 : 33 -RRB- . Lastly , marker reliability ensures that tests are marked by all examiners consistently with the application of the same criteria . Particularly , `` objective tests -LRB- as the one evaluated -RRB- are marked by a system of on-site supervised marking , and there is extensive use of computerised methods of marking , involving optical mark readers '' -LRB- ALTE 2006 : 2 -RRB- . As Weir -LRB- 2005 : 201 -RRB- claims : `` in receptive tests not much concern is on marker reliability per se '' . So this objective way of marking the test ensures that grading will be consistent and fair . Besides , one of the advantages of MCQ tests is that they `` exhibit almost complete marker reliability '' -LRB- Urquhart & Weir , 1998 : 158 -RRB- . In conclusion , this paper evaluated the FCE reading test with regards to Theory-based , Context and Scoring Validity . Overall , evidence for the test 's validity was easily found from the Cambridge website , the Handbook and the test itself . However it was difficult to find information for some aspects of validity , especially for the scoring part and the executive resources included in theory-based validity as there was not much research done regarding reading . Nevertheless , the whole attempt of evaluating a test was really challenging , motivating , and helpful for inexperienced teachers like me . The test was a good one since it engaged students in purposeful reading of a wide range of interesting to them topics and had all the characteristics that Alderson -LRB- 2000 : 83 -RRB- sees in good tests of reading . So I would definitely recommend the test to my students since it is taken by many other Greek candidates over the past years and it is complete , adequately trialed , as well as in accordance with my learners ' needs . Actually , UCLES has been working a lot on developing valid tests so all teachers should learn how to evaluate certain tests according to their specific teaching context . This would offer a `` clearer understanding of what language proficiency is and the order in which our students acquire it '' -LRB- Weir , 1993 : 170 -RRB- .
