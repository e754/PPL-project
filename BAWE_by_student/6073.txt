During the 5 th Century the Greeks developed a sense of identity between Greek and non-Greek . In this essay I will be using primary sources , such as Thucydides ' History of the Peloponnesian War , and secondary sources to investigate how and why the Greeks formed a sense of Greek and Barbarian . The Greeks saw themselves as a superior and civilised people . The Greeks believed that `` no one could compare to the wise , courageous , judicious and freedom - loving Greeks . '' -LRB- Europe : A History by Norman Davies , Pimlico , 1997 p103 -RRB- The Greeks considered all aspects of their lifestyles as very advanced , and this applied , in particular , to the political systems . Before the 5 th Century , most cities in Greece had been ruled over by Tyrants , leaders who had overthrown the previous Government , usually with the popular support of the people . However , Athens was the first city to adopt a Democratic system . Athens had a direct form of Democracy , whereby the male citizen population of the city would meet at an assembly to make important political decisions and to debate issues which were effecting the city . The Athenians were extremely proud of this system , which was considered by many of the citizens as much more advanced and civilised than any other political system that existed at the time . Democracy gave the people , the ` demos , ' more control over the running of their state , the ` polis , ' without having the rule of a single leader . Democracy started to spread throughout the Greek world and one of the only Greek cities to not adopt this system was Sparta . Many Greeks considered Sparta inferior to Athens , and in a speech to the Spartans , a Corinthian commented , according to Thucydides , `` Thus it happens that the vast experience of Athens has carried her further than you on the path of innovation '' -LRB- Thucydides History of the Peloponnesian War 1.71 . -RRB- The belief that Greeks were very advanced also affected the military strategy , with the heavy infantry , the hoplites , working together , without being forced to fight . Unlike in Monarchies where the soldiers were forced to fight for their ruler , the Greeks fought `` only for themselves , they reap for themselves the rewards of bravery or the penalties of cowardice '' -LRB- Hippocrates Airs , Waters , Places 1 . -RRB- It was believed that this made the Greek , and in particular Athenian , war machines far superior to any other army , since they were fighting for themselves , to help protect their own land , and this gave the men a much greater incentive to fight . The idea of citizenship was extremely important to the Greeks . Greece was not a nation state , but instead consisted of a number of city-states , which had their own political structure and system . The Greeks felt such loyalty to their city that `` whilst there was a concept of national identity when faced with an outside enemy , as when during the Persian wars a Hellenic league was formed to combat Xerxes , most Greeks saw themselves as not primarily Greek , but as a member of their city state . '' -LRB- Ancient Greece Social and Historical documents from Archaic Times to the Death of Socrates -LRB- c800-399 B.C -RRB- by Matthew Dillon and Lynda Garland , Routledge London and New York , 1994 -RRB- . The Greek males who lived in these cities were granted citizenship , a very privileged position that gave new legal rights and responsibilities . For example , Athenian citizens were expected to attend the assembly , but were also not taxed as much as metics , who were freed slaves and non-Athenian people who lived in Athens . The requirements for citizenship were strict and before any person could be classed as a citizen they had to be male , non-slaves who had Athenian parents . As a result , no non-Greeks were granted citizenship , giving the citizens a legally superior position in the society . This developed a sense of difference between the Greeks and barbarians . As can be seen , the Greek sense of identity influenced many important areas of life , in particular the political running of the city-states and the legal status of the citizens . The opposite of the Greek was the barbarian . The Greeks saw these non-Greek foreigners as strange , uncivilized and often savage . Even the word ` barbarian ' was an attack on the speech and language of these non-Greeks , who were considered to be illiterate . Barbarians had unusual practises and traditions that contrasted greatly with those of the Greeks . As with the Greeks , this barbarianism affected all areas of non-Greek life . In many cases the Greeks believed that the Barbarians had different anatomies to the Greeks , often affected by the climate with `` the small variations of climate to which the Asiatics are subject , extremes of both heat and cold being avoided , account for their mental flabbiness and cowardice as well . '' -LRB- Hippocrates Airs , Waters , Places 1 -RRB- Herodotus , in Histories , claimed that the further a person went from Greece the more savage and unusual the local population became . As with the Greeks , barbarians also had their own form of government and the majority of barbarian people were ruled over by Tyrants or the Aristocracy . This was considered old-fashioned and did not offer the people any control in the running of their government . The Greeks also saw the barbarian armies as inferior to their own . Unlike the brave , heavily armoured and well-trained hoplite soldiers that were common in the Greek armies , the barbarians , such as the Persians , used inexperienced and lightly armoured men who did not feel a need to fight . This made victory in battle more certain for the Greeks . However , when the barbarians did have success they were often extremely brutal . For example , when the Thracians managed to break into Mycalessus they `` butchered the inhabitants , sparing neither the young or the old , but methodically killing everybody they met '' -LRB- Thucydides History of the Peloponnesian War 7.29 . -RRB- The Greeks could also fear barbarians . The Greeks constructed some of the strongest images of the barbarians through tragedies . One of the most fearsome barbarian characters can be found in Euripides ' Medea . This character was Medea , the wife of Jason , the famous Greek hero who had sailed in the Argos to Colchis to recover the golden fleece . Medea had married Jason and had returned with him to Corinth , in Greece . This was unusual , since Medea was a barbarian and the daughter of the King of Colchis , Aeetes . It was considered strange and wrong for a Greek Hero to marry a barbarian whose father was a Monarch , and would have been frowned upon in 5 th Century Athens , where the tragedy was performed . Medea quickly showed her barbarism , by firstly killing her own brother to aid her escape and by then killing King Pelias of Iolcus , who was Jason 's uncle . In revenge for Jason leaving her and marrying Glauce , Medea murdered Glauce , Glauce 's father Creon and Medea 's and Jason 's own two sons . This was a clever attack aimed towards Jason and by doing this she made it impossible for Jason to have a family and would also result in him living the rest of his life by himself , whilst Medea escaped unharmed . Medea was a warning to the Athenians , since she taught them to not only show distrust towards barbarians , but also to be wary of women . Although Medea was a barbarian woman , she showed her intelligence and strength of character over Jason , and had defeated a great hero . However , the Greeks did not generally fear the Barbarians . For example `` the Scythians ... were the ideal type of anti-Greek : non agricultural , non-urban , uncivilised , nomadic , '' -LRB- The Greeks : A Portrait of Self and Others by Paul Cartledge , Oxford University Press , 1993 , p71 -RRB- and so the Greeks did not have much to fear from these inferior people . This can also be seen in tragedy and `` in his Persae , Aeschylus creates a lasting stereotype where the civilized Persians are reduced to cringing , ostentatious , arrogant , cruel , effeminate , and lawless aliens '' -LRB- Europe A History , p103 -RRB- As can be seen , the Greeks had some very prejudiced and strong views of the barbarians . As with their own sense of identity , the Greeks constructed a barbarian identity which was an opposite to their own . Whereas citizens were free men , barbarians were ruled over by a single leader or group of leaders and whereas the Greek soldiers were brave and experienced , the barbarian soldiers were weak minded , inexperienced and brutal . The Greeks created the idea of civilised and barbarian as a defence mechanism . The Greeks had suffered during the 5 th Century from Persian invasions , which threatened to crush Greece . However , the Greeks agreed to fight side by side and proved their strength at a number of battles . The Athenians defeated the Persians at Marathon in 490B.C . despite being outnumbered . This was followed by the fighting at Thermopylae , in 480BC , where a Spartan force of only three hundred men succeeded in slowing the advance of many thousands of Persians , and at Salamis , where the Athenians successfully defeated Persia 's fleet . These great victories gave many Greeks a sense of national pride and seemed to show the superiority of the Greek armies The victories also helped to form a feeling of superiority that affected the Greeks politically and ideologically . The Persian invasion was key in bringing the Greek city states together and created a national identity which had never existed before , and as Norman Davies writes `` the Persian wars gave a permanent sense of identity to the Greeks who escaped Persian domination . '' -LRB- Europe A History , p100 -RRB- This was solidified by the formation of a Delian league , a pan-Hellenic alliance that was led by Athens and was formed to help to defeat the future threat of Persian invasion . All cities in Greece were forced to join this coalition , and were expected to give money or ships to aid the war effort . The Delian league unified Greece and created a true sense of a Greek nation . However , the Athenians started to use the Delian league to their own advantage and used much of the money intended for defeating the Persian on elaborate building projects within Athens . The xenophobia of the Greeks was in some respects necessary . The idea of a national identity meant that , if Greece was attacked , the city states were more likely to unite to fight a common enemy , since the threat of Persian invasion remained throughout the 5 th Century . However , towards the end of the 5 th Century this identity became less important . The Peloponnesian War , between Sparta and Athens , began in 431B.C . and ended with the Spartan victory in 404B.C . Despite the Greece-wide hatred of non-Greeks , the Spartans accepted the aid of the Persians in the war , which would have been considered by many as impossible . As can be seen , the idea of Greek and non-Greek stemmed from the Persian invasion of Greece . Although the Greeks did not win easily , the victories that they experienced created a sense of superiority over all non-Greeks . In Conclusion , it can be seen that the Greeks constructed a very strong image of the average Greek and non-Greek through their political systems , war machines , customs and behaviours . These were a result of the fighting that took place between Greece and Persia during the 5 th Century . The Greeks constructed their image of identity by comparing political systems , in particular Democracy against Monarchy and through tragedies such as Medea and the Persians . This had an important affect on the opinions of ordinary Greeks , and was emphasised by the system of citizenship which gave the Greeks a superior position in society .
Gladiators were not considered as heroes by the Romans , but became very popular attractions through their strength , violence and sexual appeal . These characteristics gained them public admiration . In this essay I will be investigating the reasons for the popularity of the gladiators . I will be using secondary sources , including Suetonius ' The Twelve Caesars . Gladiatorial combat was an extremely popular and brutal sport in the Ancient Roman World . Gladiators would fight as part of religious festivals or to celebrate important dates in the Roman calendar . The competitions did not only take place in the city of Rome , but were instead held throughout the empire , even in the small towns . The fighting was carried out in amphitheatres , large and significant structures that could hold the majority of a town 's population . The combat itself varied in size and spectacle , from individual hand to hand combat between a small group of men , to a great naval battle held by `` Titus , who flooded the newly built Colosseum in AD80 to stage a sea fight and other water-borne displays '' -LRB- The Roman World 44BC - AD180 by Martin Goodman with the assistance of Jane Sherwood , Routledge , 1997 , p189 -RRB- The fighting also often involved animals . The gladiators were expected to fight , and die , bravely , to make the competitions as exciting as possible . The gladiators were equipped with a wide variety of weapons and armour , usually highly decorated to increase the spectacle of the combat . The competitions could last over several days , sometimes with several thousand gladiators participating in the bloody sport . The festivals were funded by important and wealthy members of the elite , to show their importance in society , and by emperors , who could afford the most elaborate shows . The vast majority of the population , including women , attended the amphitheatres , and as a result the amphitheatres became the focal points of society , in which the poor and the rich sat near to each other . The gladiators were not exclusively men , since many of the fighters were female . The gladiators came from all over the world , and many of the popular gladiatorial fights involved barbarians from the north , including Britain . As can be seen , gladiator fights were a very important part of society , and therefore it is unsurprising that gladiators were popular . Gladiators were not considered as heroes in the Roman empire , but they were seen as extremely brave warriors . It was impossible to see the gladiators as heroes . Most gladiators were forced into the sport after becoming prisoners of war or becoming condemned prisoners , and so they were treated as prisoners . The gladiators were forced `` to submit to fearful perils and a cruel discipline . His oath bound him to endure unflinchingly scourging , burning , or death . His barracks were a closely guarded prison '' -LRB- Roman Society from Nero to Marcus Aurelius by Samuel Dill , Macmillan and Co. , Limited , 1925 , p242 . -RRB- The gladiators were not permitted to leave their barracks and all of the time in the barracks was used for training for combat . In fact , the only prospect of freedom that gladiators had was that if he `` survived for three years , he was offered the wooden sword of release , if for five , the cap of manumission . '' -LRB- The Roman Experience by L.P.Wilkinson , Elek Books , p163 . -RRB- The gladiators were the very lowest elements of society , at the same level as slaves . As a result , it was impossible for the average Roman to consider a person of such low status as a hero . However , the strength and bravery of the gladiators was greatly admired and `` children played at being Gladiators , and men practised their skills . They were typical topics of conversation . '' -LRB- The Roman Experience , p164 -RRB- Gladiators risked their lives regularly , fighting each other and great wild beasts , often equipped with poor weapons or armour . During the fighting they endured great pain and many were killed . All of this was done for the entertainment of the crowds . However , there were some attractions for the gladiators . Despite their cruel treatment and the possibility of a terrible death , `` the sense of being the focus of thousands of spectators , pride in prowess and satisfaction for blood lust , the adulation of fanatics , especially women , who might be , for instance , the partisans of the Samnite or the Thracian style of arms , and the possibility of enjoying a glorious retirement '' -LRB- The Roman Experience , p163 -RRB- slightly compensated for the dangers . This sense was increased by the support of the emperors , and for example `` Augustus honoured all sorts of professional entertainers by his friendly interest in them ; maintained , and even increased , the privileges enjoyed by athletes ; banned gladiatorial contests if the defeated fighter were forbidden to plead for mercy . '' -LRB- The Twelve Caesars by Suetonious Translated by Robert Graves , Penguin , 2003 -RRB- This gave the gladiators an increased sense of glory . Some people , such as professional soldiers and brave individuals , even volunteered and so `` in all ages we hear of volunteers . '' -LRB- The Roman Experience , p163 -RRB- The popular image of the brave gladiator gave the sport a sense of heroism . Another important factor that made gladiators extremely popular was their sexual attractiveness . Women were amazed by the strength and the masculine appearance of the gladiators ; this was one of the main attractions for women who spectated the combat . Of course , the most attractive gladiators were those who were successful since `` Gladiators became sex symbols when successful '' -LRB- The Roman World 44BC - AD180 , p189 . -RRB- The Gladiators would usually have been visibly muscular and strong . Women were admitted to the games , although they were often made to sit at the rear of the seating , where the view of the arena was the poorest . However , as part of the religious festivals , the Vestal Virgins were given seats at the very front of the arena . As a result , women were a significant part of the audience . Evidence of the female admiration of the gladiators can be seen in the graffiti at Pompeii , with many writings describing certain fighters , and despite the cruel conditions endured by the gladiators `` many graffiti bear witness to the admiration and excitement which they none the less provoked in the hearts of the public , especially women . '' -LRB- History of Rome by Michael Grant , Michael Grant Publications Ltd. , 1978 , p246 -RRB- The `` unconcealed , and not always discreet , admiration of women '' -LRB- Roman Society from Nero to Marcus Aurelius , p243 -RRB- can easily be seen in the graffiti . However , there was also a sexual aspect for the male audience . The gladiators were admired by the women and envied by the men . Importantly , many of the gladiatorial fights included women and as part of the combat the women often fought topless , especially the ` mythological ' combat which included fighting by women dressed as bare breasted Amazons . Despite the brutal fighting , this would have been an attraction for men to attend the games . As can be seen , the sexual aspect increased the view that gladiators as almost heroic fighters . Gladiators were seen in the same way which great heroes were viewed , as incredibly brave and strong , and this would have made them into objects of admiration for the many female spectators . The main attraction of the gladiatorial games was the extreme brutality and violence of the sport . The combat was extremely sadistic and many thousands of people were brutally slaughtered in the name of entertainment . Augustus , in his Res Gestae , wrote `` I gave three gladiatorial games in my own name and five in that of my sons and grandsons ; at these games some 10,000 men took part in combat '' -LRB- Augustus , Res Gestae , 22-3 -RRB- showing the immense scale of the games . The games had originally simply involved sacrifices , but then grew to hand to hand combat and then developed into terrifying spectacles with some extremely vicious and bloody ` performances , ' `` to the point where the Circus Maximus and the Collosseum were in almost permanent session '' -LRB- Europe A History by Norman Davies Pimlico , 1997 , p180 . -RRB- As the scale of the games increased , the people who ran the games were required to find new ways of keeping the crowds entertained . This led to the tremendous naval battles , such as the ones held by Titus , Julius Caesar , Augustus and Claudius . The games became increasingly brutal and `` in time , acts of gross obscenity , bestiality and mass cruelty were demanded '' -LRB- Europe A History , p180 . -RRB- Although the violence of the games was terrible , there was little opposition to the games , especially from the people in power , since `` the opinion of the powerful and enlightened class , with rare exceptions , made no effort to purify and humanise the grossness of the masses . '' -LRB- Roman Society from Nero to Marcus Aurelius , p235 -RRB- The games were a method of state control , which helped to keep the population content with the leadership and acted as a release from the removal of freedoms . Some members of the elite did argue against the games , but not for the case of humanity . Instead , these members of the state were angry at the reduced free thought and intelligence of the people . According to Juvenal `` the people who have conquered the world now have only two interests - bread and circuses '' and it was this that worried the elite . More than any other factor , the violence of the games was the main attraction that drew the most spectators . According to Michael Grant `` The sanguinary cruelty of these sports betrays the existence of a powerful streak of sadism in the Ancient Roman and Italian character '' -LRB- History of Rome , p89 -RRB- at the time when the society was extremely militaristic and violence was common . The gladiators were simply used as performers . As can be seen , the gladiatorial violence would have made the fighters seem heroic to a society that was extremely sadistic . In conclusion , it can be seen that the view of gladiators as heroic characters was created by the bravery of the fighters , their sexual attractiveness and the violence of the games themselves . The bravery made the male population envy the gladiators , whilst the strength and sexual attractiveness of the gladiators gained them the support of the women spectators . The brutality of the fighting made the gladiators heroes for a society that was extremely sadistic and violent . All of these factors gave the gladiators a sense of heroism , despite their suffering , brutal deaths and extremely low position in society .
The Peloponnesian war had a large effect on the society and culture of Athens . This can clearly be seen in the comedies and tragedies from the time of the war , which had changed greatly from the pre-war period to reflect the feelings and events of the war . This essay will investigate the impact of the Peloponnesian war on Athenian culture and will examine several comedies by Aristophanes , and tragedies by Euripides to see the effect of the war . The Peloponnesian war began in Plateia in 431BC after a series of disagreements between Sparta and Athens . The outbreak of war was greatly influenced by smaller city-states which were either members of the Athenian Empire , or supporters of the Spartans . The Athenians and Spartans had agreed upon a thirty year peace in 446/445BC , which divided the Greek world in half . However , this peace only lasted fifteen years . When the war began , the Spartans and Athenians adopted different strategies . Whereas the Spartans focussed their efforts on swift strikes on the Attic countryside , the Athenians retreated inside the walls of the city and decided to fight a defensive war , instead relying on the power of its navy . This was a great change from the wars of the past , where victory had often depended on a single major battle . As a result the Peloponnesian war lasted for seventeen years , with few major battles . Between 421 and 414BC the peace of Nicias was negotiated . However , the Corinthians , a major partner of the Spartans , refused to accept the peace , In 415BC the Athenians attempted a Sicilian Expedition , which resulted in a complete failure , `` everything was destroyed , and few out of many returned home . '' This gave the Spartans an opportunity to strike their weakened enemy , and so war was declared in 414BC by the Spartans . The Spartans allied with Greece 's traditional enemy , the Persians , who provided the Spartans with a navy in an attempt to finally defeat Athens . The war continued until 404BC when the Athenians were defeated at the Battle of Aegispotamai . Athens was captured by the Spartans , and the walls of the city were torn down . The Athenian democracy , and the Athenian Empire , ceased to exist , being replaced by Spartan government . The Peloponnesian war had a great social impact on Athens . The city had started the war as a rich and powerful state . However , at the time of its defeat it had changed to a poor and weak city . The war and more importantly the Athenian reliance on a powerful Navy had acted as a great strain on the war time economy . This was worsened by the fleeing of the metics , the non-Athenians who were responsible for most of the trade within the city . The war had severely damaged the population of Athens , which had dropped by one third , caused by a terrible plague , war dead and the fleeing of the non-Athenian ` metics . ' One of the most important sources of income , the silver mines at Laurium , was impossible to reach due to the actions of the Spartans and this resulted in the devaluing of the coinage . Whilst suffering from these , Athens was also responsible for the control and upkeep of its empire . The citizens , who had originally felt great loyalty to the city , also experienced a change and the attacks on farming by the Spartans caused many `` households to put their own perceived security before the defence of the city . '' This weakened Athens ' ability to defend itself , with fewer citizens feeling a need to protect the city when their own families were under attack . All of these factors had a terrible effect on the Athenian society , causing great suffering and the weakening of the once powerful economy . The Peloponnesian wars had a great impact on the Athenian Society and most importantly its culture . The tragedies and comedies written and performed during the wars are a powerful reflection of the general atmosphere present in Athens . There was a sense of great despair as the war continued and `` Thucydides , who as a first hand witness of the war describes it , in fact , as rather like a disease , worsening by stages and ending in the collapse of the ideals which he ascribes to Pericles . '' This `` worsening by stages '' can be seen in the plays of Aristophanes . At first , in his play `` The Wasps '' written in 422BC Aristophanes makes only a few short comments of the war , mentioning Cleonymus who was given the name `` the great-shield dropper , '' referring to an incident during one of the battles with the Spartans . Written during the first phase of the Peloponnesian Wars , the play focuses primarily on the internal affairs of Athens . However , as the war continued the mood of Aristophanes ' plays changed greatly . `` Peace '' was written by Aristophanes in 421BC , shortly before the negotiation of the Peace of Nicias . The entire play is focussed on the war , describing the adventures of an old farmer , Trygaeus , who travels to heaven on a dung beetle to rescue peace from its cave-prison . Although this is a comedy , with satire as well as slapstick humour , the play carries a strong message , describing the hopes of Aristophanes that peace would be reached . At the end of the play , after Trygaeus successfully rescues Peace , and her assistants Harvest and Festival , a great feast is held . During this feast of Trygaeus speaks a prayer : `` Let us now to heaven pray To bless us this and every day , To give us lots of bread to eat , And wine to drink , and figs so sweet , To give all Greece abundant wealth , Our wives fertility and health , Restore us all the joys of yore , And make at last an end of war , For ever and ever , Amen ! '' This is an excellent indicator of the spirit of the time , showing how , despite some resistance from politicians , and in the play weapon makers , there was a wish for peace held by Aristophanes . As can also be seen , this passage describes the nostalgic belief that peace was associated with the past and the possibility of a return to the `` joys of yore '' . Trygaeus , whilst speaking to Peace , says to her `` show thyself in the entirety of thy noble beauty to us thy lovers , who for thirteen years have been worn out with longing for thee , '' indicating the hardships caused by the war and the length of the fighting . However , unfortunately for Aristophanes peace did not continue for long and war was again declared in 414BC . This led Aristophanes to write the Frogs , this time in the later stages of the war , in 405BC , only a year before Athens ' total defeat . This play once again displays a complete change of mood in the comedy , and shows a sense of great despair and fear . In many ways the story of the Frogs matches that of Peace . However , instead of describing the journey of Trygaeus to rescue peace from heaven , Frogs is a story of the journey of Dionysus , the Greek God of Wine , into the underworld , Hades , to retrieve one of the deceased tragedians and return them to Athens to give a solution to the city 's problems . Once again the play contains frequent slapstick and satirical humour , but also carries a message of despair and depression at the prospect of Athens ' future , and makes several references to the events of the war . For example , when Dionysus attempts to cross the River Styx with his slave Xanthias , he is told `` sorry sir , no slaves allowed . Not unless they fought in the sea battle '' referring to the battle of Arginusae , after which all slaves who fought were freed and given citizenship . Upon arriving in the underworld Aristophanes uses the play to voice his opinions on the solution to the war , and as a result , Aeschylus tells Dionysus `` well in my day everyone knew the answer : Treat enemy soil as yours , your own let go : your ships are wealth , all other wealth is woe . '' This is telling Athens that it should focus all of its efforts and resources on the building of a navy , which had in the past been the main source of its power . Euripides also suggests a complete change of leadership and `` if we are putting our trust in certain persons , and not putting our trust in certain other persons , and the city is not being saved , then it seems to me the only reasonable hope of saving the city lies in reversing the procedure . '' Both of these plans suggest a return to the historic leading of Athens , focussing on the strength of the navy and in the case of Euripides reversing the way that the city had become reliant on several individuals . As can be seen , the atmosphere and general mood of the plays by Aristophanes changed greatly during the Peloponnesian Wars . The Wasps , Peace and the Frogs each show different attitudes to the events of the war and show a large change in the confidence of Athens . The Athenian tragedies written during the time of the Peloponnesian war also reflect the mood and atmosphere present in Athens . Euripides wrote several plays , in particular the Trojan Women and the Bacchae , which showed the fears of the Athenians . In particular , the Trojan Women describes the fears of the women left in Troy as the Greeks approached , reflecting the impeding sense of defeat that was present in Athens as the war started to turn to Sparta 's favour . Euripides also cleverly used his plays to show his shock at the changes to the behaviour of the people caused by the war and `` In the Trojan women , produced in 415BC after the massacre of Melos he voiced a shocked cry of despair at the conscienceless brutality which was infecting all Greeks . '' This shows another change of the belief in the Athenian character . Athens had considered itself more civilised than the other Greek cities in terms of its culture and politics . However Euripides believed that this had since changed and was beginning to damage Athens . The frequent indecision of the Assembly and the massacre at Melos were seen by many as a sign that Athens was no more civilised than many of the other Greek city-states , and this caused much surprise and discomfort for the Athenians . Euripides perfectly displayed this sense of change in Athens ' behaviour in his play the Bacchae . This play focuses on Pentheus , a confident and strong man . However , despite his apparent civilised nature , this did not save him from the fury of his own mother , who , alongside other women , tore apart her son in a Bacchic frenzy . This showed to the Athenians that , despite their civilised society , they could not protect themselves from their true nature . In doing this Euripides succeeded in creating `` a despairing recognition of the triumph of the Irrational , which breaks through the orderly façade of the human intellect like an animal shattering a flimsy cage . '' As can be seen , the tragedies written by Euripides were a strong reflection of the beliefs and fears of the Athenians . The Trojan Women showed the fears of the Athenians if they would be defeated . The Bacchae shows Euripides anger at the violent human nature that was present in all Athenians , even if it had been well disguised by ` civilisation ' . In conclusion it can be seen that the Peloponnesian war had a great impact on the Athenian society . Some of these effects were material , such as the loss of the city 's wealth caused by the upkeep of a navy and the fleeing of the metics . However , the most visible effects were on the culture of Athens . The plays by Aristophanes show the gradual decline in the confidence of the Athenians . Peace shows a strong belief that peace could be reached and the possibility of a joyful future . However after the recommencing of war , in the Frogs , a distinct sense of despair and fear can be seen . The tragedies by Euripides also show a sense of shock at the change of the Athenian character caused by the war , which became less civilised and consequently more brutal .
Carthage 's aims during the First and Second Punic Wars differed greatly . During the First Punic War , Carthage attempted to gain power in Sicily , thereby creating opportunities for further expansion in Italy . Unlike the First , the Second Punic war was an effort by the Carthaginians to revenge the actions of the Romans in the First War , and the central aim of the war was to crush Rome . The First War was led by a large number of generals , many of whom , according to Polybius , were incompetent , whereas the Second War was driven by the personal ambition and objectives of Hannibal . It is difficult to find the true aims of Carthage during the war , since no Carthaginian primary sources survive , and the two main sources are Polybius and Livy . This essay will investigate the aims of Carthage during the First and Second Punic Wars , using Polybius and Livy , as well as various secondary sources , and will investigate the personal aims of Hannibal and the reasons for the survival of the Roman Republic during the war . The First Punic War was a territorial conflict , and was an attempt by the Carthaginians to seize Sicily , which would act as a good stepping-stone for the Carthaginians into the peninsula of Italy . Rome and Carthage had been peaceful to one another for many years , with treaties between both powers dating back , according to Polybius , to 509 B.C. However , this began to change during the 3 rd Century BC , particularly after Campanian mercenaries captured Messana in Sicily . The Campanians were threatened by the Syracusans , and therefore requested assistance from the Romans and the Carthaginians . Both the Romans and the Carthaginians responded with aid , although only the Carthaginians were allowed into the city . However once inside the Carthaginians were once again persuaded to leave the city , and the Romans , led by the Consul Appius Claudius were allowed into the city . In response , Carthage sided with Syracuse and began a siege of Messana . These actions resulted in a war which lasted for twenty four years . Unlike the Second Punic War , in which Carthage was led almost completely by the ambitious Hannibal , the leadership of Carthage during the First Punic war was much larger , and consisted of many different generals with various backgrounds . Perhaps one of the most interesting of these is Xanthippus , a mercenary from Greece who succeeded in defeating a Roman army on African soil . However , after this single victory , Xanthippus returned to Greece . However , according to Polybius , many of the Carthaginian generals were incompetent leaders , and often suffered at the hands of their own men . For example , a general , Hannibal , who had succeeded in saving his men by fleeing with them from Messana after a being defeated by the Romans , was subsequently crucified after being blockaded by the Romans in Sardinia , resulting in the loss of many of his ships . The Romans eventually succeeded in carrying out an amphibious attack on Africa . This was terrifying for the Carthaginians since `` they knew that Africa was extremely vulnerable to attack , and that the population would offer little resistance to any invader who succeeded in getting ashore . '' As a result , Carthage was forced to change its aims from an offensive strategy to conquer Sicily , to a defensive strategy to protect the city of Carthage itself . However , a determined general , Hamilcar Barca , eventually emerged , and through his leadership the Carthaginians succeeded in once again challenging Rome in Sicily . For much of the war , the main aim of Carthage was to use its naval superiority to overcome the Romans in Sicily . As a result , unlike the Second Punic War , the First Punic War involved many large and important sea battles . The Carthaginians were experienced at the construction of ships and also had experience of naval battles . In comparison , the Romans were mostly focused on a land army , and as a result lacked a dedicated navy at the start of the war . Despite this , the Romans repeatedly succeeded in defeating the Carthaginians at sea , ending Carthage 's domination of the seas . Eventually , after many years of fighting , in Sicily , on the Sea , and in Africa , the Carthaginians suffered the affects of attrition and were no longer able to field an effective force , and were forced to surrender . As can be seen , the First Punic war was fought for the control of Sicily . The Carthaginians aimed to defeat the Romans at sea , and use their naval superiority to secure Sicily . However , the unlike the Second Punic War , the Carthaginians did not have a determined and ambitious leader with his own aims , and this caused serious problems during the fighting . The Second Punic war was a conflict driven by the personal ambition and the aims of the Carthaginian leader , Hannibal , and by a desire to seek revenge for the events of the First war , during which Carthage had almost been destroyed . Unlike the First Punic War , much of the conflict was fought in Italy , as Hannibal tried to destroy the Roman Republic . The war began in 218BC , after the sack of Saguntum by the Carthaginians . In his article , the Strategy of the Second Punic War , E.T. Salmon argues `` The truth is that they went to Spain after their defeat in the First Punic War presicely in order to build up a base . They hoped to make it a point d'appui for their planned war of revenge against Rome : the Iberian peninsula was to be for them a springboard for attack . '' By doing this , Salmon claims , the Carthaginians would be able to carry out future attacks into Europe or even Italy , and suggests that the Carthaginians had been planning war since the end of the First War . Although this plan was perhaps in place at the time , the true force behind the Second Punic War was not the government of Carthage , but the leader of the Carthaginian army , Hannibal Barca . According to Livy , in Book XXI , the decision to attack was out of the hands of the Carthaginian senate , and was in fact being controlled by Hannibal . Livy describes a scene in the Carthaginian Senate , where a single senator , Hanno , pleads to the other senators not to allow the war to occur , since they had `` sent to join our troops this dangerous and ambitious man , hot with the desire for power and seeing but one way to its acquisition - a life in arms amongst the soldiery , spent in provoking an endless succession of wars . '' Although this speech was written by Livy many years after the event , it is clear that Hannibal was extremely ambitious , and would have been able to see the direct outcome of an attack on Saguntum ; '' Hannibal knew perfectly well that he risked war with Rome . '' Hannibal would have welcomed a war , and believed that he would be able to crush the Roman Republic . When war was declared , it was clear that the main aim of Carthage , and of Hannibal , was to attack Rome . This created one of the first major differences between the First and Second Punic Wars . During the First , Carthage had possessed a powerful navy , and had succeded in sending troops to Sicily without difficulty . As a result , much of the fighting took place on the seas . In comparison , by the time of the Second , Rome 's navy had grown considerably , making an invasion of Rome by sea almost impossible . As a result , a `` land invasion was necessary because in 218 Rome had a much more powerful navy : 220 ships to 100 Punic . '' Hannibal led a dangerous crossing through the alps which caused the loss of many of his men . The march into Italy was a great risk . However , it is unlikely that Hannibal would have intended to march on Rome itself . Although Hannibal 's men had some experience in capturing cities , in particular after Saguntum , Rome was an extremely large and formidable city . As a result , it would have been somewhat unrealistic at first to consider an attack on the city itself , and instead Hannibal decided to cut the city off from the rest of Italy , thereby taking advantage of the divisions within Italy and Hannibal hoped that `` if these Italian states could be induced to abandon their several alliances with Rome , Rome 's days of greatness would be over . '' This would have been particularly attractive , since it would not have involved an attack on Rome , but would have caused enough damage to the structure of the Roman Republic to seriously weaken it . However , there is a possibility that , as the war progressed , Hannibal may have been able to capture Rome . Throughout the early stages of the war , Carthage gained several important victories against Roman armies . Hannibal had defeated a Consular army at the River Trebia very early in the war , in 218BC , and succeeded in completely destroying a consular army and killing Flaminius , the Roman Consul commanding the army at Lake Tresimene in 217BC . This was followed by a great victory at Cannae , in 216BC , where Hannibal crushed the armies of two Consuls , Paullus and Varro . This essentially left Rome open to attack , since it could not immeadiately replace the large number of soldiers it had lost . Livy describes the scene after Cannae , with the commander of Hannibal 's cavalry , Maharabal , telling Hannibal that `` if you want to know the true significance of this battle , let me tell you that within five days you will take your dinner in triumph , on the Capitol ... The first knowledge of our coming will be the sight of us at the gates of Rome . '' Although this is another of the speeches created by Livy , it is likely that an attack on the city of Rome at this stage would perhaps have been possible . Rome had lost much of its manpower during the fighting , and this would have made the city extremely vulnerable to an immeadiate attack . However , it is unlikely that such a strategy would have ultimately been successful . Although Rome had lost many of its men , it had very large reserves of manpower , in particular in the form of support from its many Italian allies . Although Hannibal had succeeded in undermining alliances between some of the northern Italian states and Rome , it had not experienced the same success in central and southern Italy . Even if Hannibal had succeeded in capturing Rome , it is unlikely that the he would have been able to hold the city uncontested for long . Many of the Roman allies benefitted from Rome 's power and trade and so the arrival of a Carthaginian army would not have been welcomed by many . As a result , Hannibal did not attack Rome , but continued attempting to gain support from Rome 's allies . Eventually Hannibal was defeated , and was forced to retreat from Italy . This was quickly followed by a Roman attack on Africa and the destruction of Carthage . As can be seen , the Second Punic War was led by the ambition of Hannibal . By attacking Rome , Hannibal hoped to revenge Rome 's actions during and after the First Punic War , whilst also capturing the Italian peninsula for Carthage . This was extremely different from the aims of the First Punic war , and the methods used , which focussed on land rather than naval supremacy , were also a great change from the First War . In conclusion it can be seen that the aims of the Carthaginians differed greatly between the First and Second Punic Wars . During the First Punic war , Carthage hoped to capture Sicily , yet lacked effective leadership . Carthage also hoped to use its domination of the sea to defeat the Romans , who lacked experience . In comparison , the Second Punic War was an attempt by Carthage to attack Rome . Unlike the First War , during the Second Punic War Carthage intended to use its land forces to attack Italy itself . Also , a major difference was the leadership of Hannibal , who led his armies with determination and ambition which were not present in the First Punic War .
The Roman emperor was considered divine , although the level to which he was worshipped as a god varied greatly . Roman religion was based around a public , state funded sphere and a private sphere . Although people were permitted to worship the Emperor as a living god in the private cults , state worship of the living emperor was never a feature of Roman religion . Instead of worshipping the living emperor as a god , the state cult performed rituals for the emperor 's Genius , or divine spirit . The State cult also worshipped the dead emperors , the Divi imperators , who , it was believed , were deified upon their death . This essay will investigate whether the Romans believed their emperor to be a god , and will examine the beliefs present in the state and private cults . The worship of the emperor varied greatly in the public and private spheres of Roman religion . The religions of Rome did not have a central governing body , such as a Papacy . As a result , religion was essentially divided into three main spheres , consisting of household , private and state . The top level of Roman religion was the State cult , which was funded by the empire and followed the rituals of the state cult in Rome . Each of these levels had their own considerations of the emperor 's divinity , but ` the state cult recognized only the Divi imperators and the genius of the ruling emperor , ' whereas many private cults openly worshipped the emperor as a living god . It was considered problematic for the state to acknowledge the emperor as divine . Traditionally the emperor was first among equals within the senate , and as a result accepting him as a god would have implied that the emperor was clearly superior to every other senator . This would thereby have undermined the perceived status of the senate . As a result of this , ` the sinister connotations of the emperor 's state divinity left only one option for clearer formulation of the imperial office in the Roman state : the Genius of the living emperor , a cult which did not imply divinity , but certainly did imply social humiliation for the senators involved . '' Although worship of the living emperor was frowned upon , it was considered correct to worship the living emperor 's Genius , which was ` closely attached to his person , though it was not entirely clear whether the Genius was perceived as dwelling within his body or outside it , as more of a guardian spirit . '' It was believed that every person , including the emperor , possessed a Genius . Worship of the Genius constituted a large part of religious ritual , and was not specific to the emperor . For example , many people privately worshipped the paterfamilias , the Genius of the owner of the household . The paterfamilias was usually worshipped by slaves and freedmen , as they owed their living to the master of the household , and were considered clientes , subordinate within the household , by law and tradition , to the power of the paterfamilias . Similarly , the emperor 's Genius could be worshipped as the paterfamilias of the Empire . Worship of the emperor 's Genius in fact had several political benefits for the senators and important members of the Roman political system , as `` the higher the emperor was placed on the status ladder of the world , the higher was the relative placement of the senators themselves ; if the emperor possessed outright divine status , the role and self-esteem of prominent would be as good as in the old days . '' Worshipping the emperor 's Genius thereby served a dual political purpose , avoiding giving ultimate power over Rome to this living deity whilst at the same time allowing the senators to gain improved approval from the emperor and increasing their status in the world . As the Genius was merely attached to the emperor , rather than making the emperor divine , this avoided some of the problems of offering the emperor full divinity . Conferring full divinity upon the emperor would have placed him far higher than his senators , whereas by worshipping the Genius , it was the emperor 's divine spirit , rather than the emperor himself , which was elevated . However , the worship of the Genius created its own problems within the social hierarchy . By worshipping the emperor 's Genius as the paterfamilias of the Roman empire , the position of entire population of the empire , including the senators , would be reduced to that of Clientes , no higher than the slaves and freedmen who worshipped the paterfamilias within the household . The position of the paterfamilias was almost monarchical , with great symbolic power attached , and so ` the time at which the living emperor 's Genius began to receive state worship may thus be taken as the point where the Roman state formally acknowledged itself to be a monarchy . '' Although the worship of the emperor 's Genius by the state cult did not necessarily have any practical implications , as it did not confer any powers on the emperor , it was a symbolic affirmation of the emperor 's position within Rome . As can be seen , the state did not worship the living emperor as a god , due to the political implications . Instead , the only worship the emperor received was for his Genius . However , this had its own problems , as it acknowledged the emperor 's power , as paterfamilias of the Roman empire , over his clientes , the citizens and subjects of the empire . In addition to worshipping the Genius , the state cult also worshipped the Divus imperatores , or the deified dead emperors . Starting with Augustus , it was believed that the emperor was deified upon his death , and ` the princeps could only become the object of worship in the state cult after he was separated from the ranks of the living and then deified . '' However , the Emperor could only become a god if the senate voted in favour of this ` and then only in recognition of the fact -LRB- so the official version went -RRB- that they had , by their merits , actually become gods . '' This could be considered to have given the senate some power over the emperor , for if he went against the senate , or was a poor ruler , the senate could punish him by refusing his deification . This , however , did not have a great impact , as the emperor generally expected to be deified , and the senate would usually follow his will . For example , with Augustus , ` the expectation was expressed in his lifetime that he would ascend to his rightful place in heaven , and immediately after his death Augustus was duly made a divus . '' To indicate his deification , the dead emperor would have Divi added to their name , creating for example , Divi Augustus . The transformation of the emperor from a mortal into a god after his death had benefits for the state cult , allowing it to acknowledge the emperor 's divinity , but only after he had died . This avoided the problems associated with the Genius , as it did not elevate the living emperor 's political position . Although the deceased emperors were acknowledged as gods , their worship as such did not necessarily continue for long after their deaths . Whilst the worship of the Divi imperatores did not result in political advancement for the living emperor , it also did not provide many political benefits for the senators . Worship of the living emperor 's genius could heighten the position of the senators , as it could gain the senator the favour of the emperor . However , worship of a deceased emperor could not provide this , as the living emperor would care little for the worship of his predecessors . As a result , the Divi were only worshipped for short times after their deaths , with the state cult focussing more on the worship of the emperor 's Genius . As can be seen , as well as worshipping the living emperor 's genius , the state cult also paid attention to the deceased emperors . When an emperor died , the senate would decide if he had earned deification . If the emperor was made into a Divi , he could be worshipped , allowing the senate to accept his divinity without providing him with any increased power . Although the state cult did not worship the living emperor , it is clear that it considered the emperor divine . Unlike the state cult , the private sphere of Roman religion was not restricted by politics . Every household would have its own private shrine , which was often concerned with the worship of the paterfamilias , as well as various household gods . When outside the home , the private sphere consisted of cults and religious clubs , each of which worshipped various gods and performed rituals . As the cults were funded by private individuals , the state was unable to restrict their worship , allowing any divinity to be followed . Amongst the gods worshipped by these private cults was the emperor , both as a living god , and as the Divi . Some good examples of the worship of the deceased emperors can be seen in various inscriptions . A good example of the offerings made to the emperor can be seen in an inscription from Pergamum , which states that a new president of the singers should provide `` pastry and incense <for offerings> and lamps for the image of Augustus '' on each day of the mysteries . In addition , new singers were required to pay , as an entrance fee `` to the gods <Roma and Augustus> double , 30 denarii , wine , 3 loaves . '' A glimpse of the worship of the emperor by the public cults can also be seen in an inscription from a local Greek magistracy , which states '' -LSB- the agoranamos -RSB- -LRB- a local magistrate -RRB- shall celebrate the first day of the god Caesar Augustus , son of the god <Caesar> our Saviour and Deliverer . '' However , worship of the Divi Imperatores was not popular , as the emperor was of little importance once he had died . As a result , many more private cults were interested in worshipping the living emperor than any dead emperors , ` their interest was mainly focused on the living emperor . '' Although this would have created political problems within the state cult , this was not an issue for the private sphere , as the personal and private cults could not provide the emperor with any political powers . This allowed unrestricted worship of the living emperor as divine . It was believed that worship of the emperor had social benefits for the members of a cult . Whereas the power of many of the gods found in modern religion is absolute , the position and power of the Roman gods was not strictly defined . As Ittai Gradel argues in Emperor Worship and Roman Religion , the position of the gods was relative , and followed a hierarchy , with no one god exercising absolute control over the other gods . The power and position of the gods was not set in a single document , such as the Bible for Christians or the Qu ` ran for Muslims , allowing individuals to decide if an emperor was human or divine , and how powerful the divinities were . This opened up the prospect of hierarchical advancement within the divinities , with the emperor essentially able to claim he was more divine than his followers , as well as other gods , even if he could not claim powers equal to that of , for example , Jupiter . In turn , by placing the emperor higher in the divine hierarchy , the position of those who worshipped was also elevated , ` they therefore uniformly chose to place him on the superhuman level , deliberately and emphatically so . '' By worshipping the emperor as a living god , the members of the ` imperial cults ' rose above the position of normal citizens or subjects . As a result , private cults would not merely worship the living Emperor 's Genius or the Divi , but would often worship the emperor as a living god , to raise their position as high as possible . As can be seen , the worship of the emperors was not restricted within the private sphere of Roman religion . Although the Divi imperatores were occasionally worshipped , cults to the living emperor were much more widespread throughout the empire . Worship of the living emperor increased his position in the divine hierarchy , and at the same time raised the position of the people who worshipped him . The population 's widespread acceptance of the Emperor as a god clearly shows that , although largely influenced by the social and political benefits offered by this , there was a firm belief that the emperor was divine . In conclusion , it can be seen that the Romans considered their emperor to be divine . Although the acknowledgement of the emperor 's divinity varied greatly , from the state cult that only worshipped the Genius of the living emperor and the Divi , the dead emperors , to the private cults that openly worshipped the living emperor as divine , it is clear that the divinity of the emperor was accepted . Worship of a divine emperor offered several advantages , including a higher placing in the social hierarchy , but could also cause problems for the state cult , as acceptance of the emperor 's divinity increased the emperor 's grip on the political system of Rome . However , although the worship of the emperor was restricted within the state cult , it can be seen that even here the emperor 's divinity was accepted .
Slavery and race are prominent features of Hollywood films on Rome , and are key themes throughout both Spartacus and Gladiator . Slavery and race are closely connected in both films . However , both films have different approaches to the topics , with Spartacus offering a political view of slavery and race , whereas Gladiator primarily uses slavery and race to create a setting for the film and does not create as significant a political statement . This essay will discuss and examine the role of slavery and race as themes in both Spartacus and Gladiator , focussing mainly on particular scenes , but also investigating methods used by the directors and the importance of various characters . Spartacus was released in 1960 , directed by Stanley Kubrick , and followed Spartacus , a Thracian slave , as he led a slave revolt against the Roman Republic . Unsurprisingly , considering the plotline of the film , Spartacus has slavery and freedom as its main themes . The film also addresses race , through the use of the nationality of the slaves , and also through the use of Draba , a black slave forced to fight Spartacus in the arena . The general underlying themes of the film are displayed from the outset in the first scene . The opening narrative states `` the Republic lay fatally stricken with a disease called human slavery . '' This immediately creates a dark and oppressive atmosphere to the film , matched by the scene 's music , a slow and ponderous rendition of the film 's main theme music . The scenery is extremely bleak , set on a mountainside in a desert , and the scene shows slaves being forced to work in terrible conditions . Through this ` Rome 's presence in the world is thus reduced to its basic impact : containment through force . Here slaves are worked to death in a hostile and barren environment , a physical manifestation of the institution of slavery . '' This first scene is in stark contrast to many previous films based in ancient Rome , which often open with a scene displaying the grandeur and wealth of Rome . By doing this , the film shows Rome not as a great empire , but as an oppressive , cruel and overbearing power , which has little regard for human life . The slaves are seen mining rocks , and carrying heavy baskets of stones on their backs . Throughout Spartacus , the slaves are shown to be treated as worthless animals by the Romans . The slaves are thin and heavily tanned , suggesting to the audience that they received little food and were forced to work in the extreme heat for most of their lives . The work they are forced to do is stressful , with the role of carrying the heavy rocks on their backs creating the image that the slaves are treated as mere pack animals . Spartacus is introduced when a fellow slave falls . Spartacus attempts to help the fallen slave , but is mercilessly beaten by the guards , and after biting one of the guards he is punished with death by starvation . The guards have the power of life and death over the slaves , as can be seen in the line spoken by one of them `` Spartacus again ? Well this time he dies . '' The slave who Spartacus attempted to help was dragged away and replaced , reinforcing the way view that the slaves were of no value to the Romans . The image of the slaves as animals is strengthened by the silence of the slaves throughout the scene , creating an impression for the audience that they are almost inhuman . This is greatly contrasted by the later parts of the film , where Spartacus is shown to be a brave and outspoken character , particularly once he gains his freedom , and by the communal spirit of the slaves when freed from their slavery . Through the use of Lentulus Batiatus , the first scene of Spartacus successfully contrasts the hardships suffered by the slaves with the decadence of Rome . First seen at the head of a small group of men , Batiatus is the opposite of the slaves who surround him . Finely dressed and pale skinned , Batiatus appears also to be well fed . This contrast is particularly clear when Batiatus stands next to the slaves , creating the impression that they are almost completely different creatures . The inspection of the slaves by Batiatus again creates a vivid impression that the Romans considered their slaves to be animals . The slaves are chosen according to the strength of their teeth , and Batiatus does not even address the slaves directly , instead asking them questions through the commander of the guard . This creates the impression that Batiatus does not consider the slaves human . The treatment of the slaves is clearly contrasted by Spartacus ' character . Although treated like an animal , it can easily be seen that he is fiercely independent , first helping the fallen slave , and eventually leading the revolt against Rome . Spartacus is unwilling to accept his fate , and this is a major drive for the events of the film . The film addresses several different forms of slavery , of which mining is one . The lives of household slaves is also explored through the use of Varinia , Spartacus ' love interest in the film . Unlike the mine-working slaves , the household slaves appear to be better cared for , although their treatment is generally poor . Varinia is given little freedom from her mundane tasks , and she is seen in various parts of the film sweeping a floor , cooking food and serving wine to guests . The female slaves are also treated as animals , and are even given as ` rewards ' to the gladiators , acting as little more than sex objects . The depiction of slavery in Spartacus is similar to that of many other Hollywood epics , particularly from the mid-20 th Century . There are several reasons for this . The theme of a great power oppressing an entire people is largely influenced by the Second World War , which was still in the memory of much of the audience , and the Cold War , which was a contemporary issue that affected the audience 's lives . The Roman 's treatment of the slaves can be compared to the treatment of the Jews at the hands of the Nazis , and also the fear of communism that pervaded American culture . However , the film may also be a criticism of the McCarthy 's House Committee on Un-American Activities , in which many writers were blacklisted for their communist views , including the writer Donald Trumbo , who co-wrote Spartacus . In contrast to the oppressive and materialistic Romans , the community of the freed slaves can be interpreted as an almost utopian communist society . In short , Spartacus can be seen as criticizing any form of oppression , and the slaves can be seen as representing the oppressed . As can be seen , slavery is an extremely important theme of Spartacus . The film repeatedly depicts the Romans as treating the slaves as animals , and shows the difference between the slaves and their masters through Batiatus , who represents the decadence and corruption of Rome . The theme of race has an important role in Spartacus , and is closely connected to the depiction of slavery . This is shown through the various nationalities of the main characters , and particularly through Draba , a black slave who is forced to fight Spartacus in the arena . All of the slaves in the film are shown to be of a different nationality or race from the Romans . For example , Spartacus is a Thracian slave , and his love interest , Varinia , is a Briton . This creates a clear connection between slavery and race , as it shows the slaves are not Roman , and are an almost separate race from the Romans altogether . This follows the assertion of M.S. Cyrino in Gladiator Film and History `` earlier films traditionally made Rome the imperialistic oppressor of implausibly virtuous and racially harmonious groups . '' The prejudice of the Romans is also seen in Batiatus , who , when asked if he wishes to purchase a Gaul , replies , `` oh , I do n't like Gauls , hairy . '' The portrayal of the slaves as being of different , non-Roman , nationalities helps to create a division between the ` good ' slaves and the ` bad ' Romans . However , the issue of race can most clearly be seen in the character of Draba . Although Draba only appears in a relatively short part of the film , his role is significant , both in the storyline and culturally in regards to the film 's audience . Draba is chosen by wealthy women , who accompanied Crassus to Batiutus ' gladiator training camp , to fight Spartacus to the death in the gladiatorial arena . During the combat , Draba gains the upper hand over Spartacus , and is poised to strike a fatal blow . However , just as this appears to be inevitable , Draba turns away from Spartacus and hurls his trident into the spectators . The imagery and camera work , as well as the fact that Draba was a black slave , would have had a great impact on the film audience . As Draba hurls the trident , the camera view changes to one within the group of spectators . Therefore , the trident appears to fly through the screen , attacking not only the spectators in the film , but also the cinema audience . Draba then attempts to climb up to Crassus , but is hit with a javelin and then stabbed by Crassus . The death of Draba can be seen as a major contributing factor to the slave revolt , as his brave , yet futile defiance of the Romans gave Spartacus great courage , ` Draba 's refusal to kill Spartacus leads to his martyrdom and to the gladiators rebellion . '' This would have been very powerful for the audience , as Spartacus was filmed at a time when civil rights were a major issue in the U.S. Maria Wyke , in Projecting the Past , states that ` in locating the inspiration for revolt largely in the brave defiance of a black gladiator , Spartacus acknowledges the central role of black activism in the emerging protest movements which , in 1960 , confronted Kennedy 's new administration . '' In this way , Spartacus was making a strong political statement about race and oppression , connecting the fictitious Draba with the contemporary civil rights movement , which itself was an example of a people standing up against oppression . As can be seen , race is a very important theme in Spartacus . Connections between race and slavery are made through the non-Roman nationality of each of the slaves . In addition , the character of Draba , who combines slavery and race , is a definite acknowledgement of the struggle of the civil rights movement . Slavery is an important theme in the film Gladiator . Released forty years after Spartacus , in 2000 , Gladiator addresses many of the same themes , but takes a different approach to most of these , and on many occasions has an almost opposite presentation . Unlike Spartacus , Gladiator does not feature many types of slaves , focusing almost exclusively on the gladiators . In addition , the race of the main characters is not as prominent a feature . The film follows Maximus , played by Russell Crowe , a Roman general who , due to the actions of Emperor Commodus , is forced into slavery and made into a gladiator . Maximus eventually uses his new role to seek and gain revenge against Commodus . Throughout the film the slaves are treated as worthless animals , mirroring Spartacus . This can be seen shortly after Maximus is taken as a slave , when he is advised by Jaba `` Do n't die , they 'll feed you to the lions , they 're worth more than we are . '' This shows that the Romans had little regard for the lives of the slaves . However , Gladiator differs from Spartacus in terms of the motivations of the main character . Whereas Spartacus is driven by a wish to be free from the shackles of his slavery , Maximus is willing to use his new position as a gladiator to achieve his ultimate goal , revenge on Emperor Commodus . This displays a different attitude towards slavery than can be seen in Spartacus . Whereas Spartacus is shown as independent and leading a revolt in the hope of freedom , Maximus is shown to accept his new position in society , and is driven solely by a lust for revenge . Proximo , the slave master , is shown to be sympathetic towards Maximus , as he was a gladiator who earned his freedom , and treats his slaves relatively fairly . In some ways , the gladiators are presented less as slaves , and almost as employees of Proximo , as they are given a small level of freedom and even form bonds with their master . This shows a different approach to slavery , as the gladiators accept their position . The race of the slaves is less important in gladiator . The character of Draba in Spartacus is paralleled in Gladiator , through the use of Jaba . However , the role of Jaba is less striking , and can be seen to reflect the attitudes of the director , Ridley Scott . Jaba is first encountered shortly after Maximus is taken as a slave . Maximus recovers consciousness , and sees Jaba looking over him . Unlike Draba , Jaba does not attempt to turn against his masters , and instead accepts that he is a gladiator . P.W. Rose in Gladiator Film and History writes that Jaba reflects ' a specifically Republican image of nostalgia for a lost golden age of race relations , in which blacks do not question whites and obey their superiors . '' This is a complete contrast to Draba , and to the portrayal of the slaves in general in Spartacus . Jaba is as accepting of his slavery as every other gladiator , whereas his counterpart , Draba , refused to follow the will of his masters and acted as the instigator for the slave revolt . Unlike Spartacus , Gladiator does not create many connections between slavery and race . Whereas it is made clear throughout Spartacus that the main characters are of various nationalities , this is not as obvious in Gladiator . Although Maximus is shown to be a Spaniard , and is know as ` the Spaniard ' by the crowds , he is not treated differently because of his race . The slaves are shown merely as slaves , and their race is of little importance . This may be because of the different attitudes represented in Gladiator . The civil rights movement , although still important in contemporary society , is not one of the main issues addressed by the film . Unlike Spartacus , Gladiator focuses less on freedom and oppression , and instead can be interpreted as a critique of modern American society . The empire is shown as more corrupt , yet less oppressive , and appears to be obsessed with violent spectacle . The film is perhaps more of a criticism of American popular culture and the political system of the United States , than a statement of freedom and oppression . As can be seen , the portrayals of slavery and race in Gladiator contrast with the portrayal of these issues in Spartacus . Although certain themes are repeated , such as the treatment of the gladiators as worthless , in the main points Gladiator varies greatly with Spartacus . Maximus is willing to accept his slavery and instead uses it as a tool to seek revenge , rather than attempting to gain his freedom . Jaba , who can be seen to be very similar to Draba , is shown as willing to subject himself to the subordination of his masters , and even allows Maximus to give him orders . In conclusion , it can be seen that race and slavery are major themes in both Spartacus and Gladiator . Spartacus focuses almost entirely on the themes of slavery and freedom . The slaves are shown to be very badly treated and considered little more than animals . Race is represented through the various nationalities of the main characters , and in particular the black gladiator Draba . His act of defiance against his masters can be seen to mirror the civil rights movement of the 1950s and 1960s , and eventually leads to the slave rebellion . Race is also connected with slavery , as the slaves are all non-Romans , strengthening the image that the slaves were separate from the Romans . In contrast , Gladiator 's Maximus does not dream of freedom , and instead uses the system to gain revenge . The slave Jaba , although similar in role to Draba , plays a much smaller role and is clearly shown as submissive . This reflects the different attitudes of the directors and the change in the main issues of the time .
