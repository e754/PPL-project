Human Resource -LRB- HR -RRB- practices are said to improve performance , therefore recently recruitment and selection methods have become important -LRB- Gonzalez & Tacorante , 2004 -RRB- . Also , mounting competition and increasingly complex consumer demands have made companies focus either on service quality or cost reduction to attain competitive advantage -LRB- Redman & Mathews , 1998 -RRB- . Within service industries , service quality and recruitment & selection become even more crucial , mainly because of service characteristics . Inseparability , intangibility , heterogeneity and perishability -LRB- Lashley , 1998 -RRB- , require service excellence , therefore , managers aim to recruit and select ideal employees , to differentiate themselves . Further , Goldsmith , Nickson , Sloan and Wood -LRB- 1997 -RRB- assert service companies ' success is connected to their employees ' personal abilities . However , Redman & Mathews -LRB- 1998 -RRB- argue that because the service industry includes a wide range of different businesses , different approaches must apply to diverse industries , depending on the amount of customer-employee contact . The hospitality & tourism industry is similar to `` secondary labour markets '' -LRB- Goldsmith et al , 1997 , pp : 16 -RRB- . Comprising mainly small businesses that rely on fluctuating demand , profitability is uncertain and production , wages and skills are low . It is also characteristic of `` weak labour markets '' -LRB- Goldsmith et al , 1997 , pp : 20 -RRB- with informal recruitment methods , such as word-of-mouth , employee recommendations , and past applicants , which lead to high employee turnover . According to The HR and Development Website-CIPD -LRB- 2004 -RRB- in 2003 , the highest rates of employee turnover -LRB- over 50 % per year -RRB- were in low paying private service businesses , including hospitality-related ones . Also , the lowest cost of employee turnover is # 750 -LRB- a considerable amount especially for small firms ! -RRB- . Price -LRB- 1994 -RRB- and Goldsmith et al -LRB- 1997 -RRB- claim poor recruitment & selection originates high employee turnover , leading to a `` vicious circle '' -LRB- Price , 2004 , pp : 57 -RRB- characterised by cost reduction strategies , which in turn minimize quality and production . Jameson -LRB- 2000 -RRB- states that with the expansion of hospitality & tourism industries , the importance of good recruitment & selection practices and the role of HR in them is emphasized . Having considered the characteristics of the industry and the costs and consequences of employee turnover , the importance of appropriate recruitment and selection becomes evident . Therefore , this essay aims to examine current recruitment and selection practices in hospitality & tourism , assessing their suitability to achieve `` employee fit '' . `` Employee fit '' refers to staff integration in the business strategy to achieve business goals -LRB- Torrington , Hall & Taylor , 2002 -RRB- . It automatically implies modifying human resource management -LRB- HRM -RRB- strategies to sustain the match -LRB- Kelliher & Perrett , 2001 -RRB- . There are two attitudes towards strategic HRM -LRB- Torrington et al , 2002 -RRB- . The `` best practice '' -LRB- universalist -RRB- approach suggests there is one `` best '' method to manage staff . The contingency -LRB- `` best fit '' -RRB- approach , according to Kelliher & Perrett -LRB- 2001 -RRB- , suggests the adaptation of HR approaches to the organisation 's structure and its ' stage of life-cycle . The resource-based view -LRB- RBV -RRB- is a variant of the best fit approach , which considers employees as unique , resources to achieve competitive advantage -LRB- Redman & Wilkinson , 2001 -RRB- . Staff planning can be based on `` hard '' models that match demand and supply of staff to maximize the number of employees , keeping costs down and profitability up -LRB- Torrington et al , 2002 -RRB- . `` Hard '' models are job-related and linked to RBV . However , `` soft '' models are system-based and suggest nurturing employees , by developing a sense of commitment in them , to achieve differentiation -LRB- Worsfold , 1999 -RRB- . They relate to total quality management -LRB- TQM -RRB- because employee commitment generates increased customer focus , cooperation and constant development , thus achieving competitive advantage -LRB- Redman & Wilkinson , 2001 -RRB- . Different authors disagree on the suitability of `` best practice '' , and even more on improving recruitment & selection methods to achieve `` employee fit '' . Hoque -LRB- 1999 -RRB- claims , that due to the growing importance of service quality , hotels and possibly the hospitality industry , are practising HR procedures . Price -LRB- 1994 -RRB- however argues that the hospitality industry is known for having a basic approach towards human resource management -LRB- HRM -RRB- that deviates from principles of `` good practice '' . Price -LRB- 1994 -RRB- adds that although some evidence of `` good practice '' is seen in larger organizations , such as interviewing , application forms , job descriptions and person specifications , these are applied inconsistently . Kelliher & Johnson -LRB- 1997 -RRB- confirm the increased attention towards HR practices . Companies now seek employee commitment , employee integration in business strategy , incentives to retain employees and a change from a bureaucratic approach to a human relations one . However it is unclear if these improvements represent a move towards HR practices -LRB- Worsfold , 1999 -RRB- or a response to other changes -LRB- Kelliher & Johnson , 1997 -RRB- . Furthermore , given the importance of employees in determining service quality , hospitality & tourism managers should lead HR practices , rather than follow other industries -LRB- Kelliher & Johnson , 1997 -RRB- . Price -LRB- 1994 -RRB- claims an improvement of personnel practices breaks the `` vicious circle '' mentioned above , encouraging quality and productivity whilst seizing opportunities offered by economic growth . Additionally , `` good '' recruitment & selection practices fight off competition from international organizations which have integrated TQM in their HRM strategy -LRB- e.g. Marriott -RRB- . Ultimately , improved personnel practices lead to competitive advantage and increased market share thus originating a `` virtuous '' -LRB- Price , 2004 , pp : 60 -RRB- cycle , based on quality and profitability . McGunnigle & Jameson -LRB- 2000 -RRB- add that appropriate recruitment & selection encourage employee commitment and develop a strong organisational culture . They identified in their study that although hotel managers were willing to implement effective recruitment & selection methods , their practices were still far from an HRM structure . Therefore , McGunnigle & Jameson -LRB- 2000 -RRB- suggest new recruitment & selection methods , like psychometric tests , personality questionnaires and assessment centres -LRB- Redman & Mathews , 1998 -RRB- to be continuously used alongside traditional `` good practice '' to develop high commitment . Redman & Wilkinson -LRB- 2001 -RRB- believe that although `` best practice '' seems like the perfect solution , companies find it hard to implement . Lockyer & Scholarius -LRB- 2004 -RRB- disagree with the `` best practice '' approach claiming it disregards the characteristics of the hospitality & tourism industry , and becomes challenging because of diverse property `` size , location and ownership '' -LRB- Lockyer & Scholarios , 2004 , pp 126 -RRB- . `` Best practice '' overlooks the differences between customized and standardized service and disregards the different roles recruitment staff have in branded properties and independent ones . Culinary taste may be thought of as a combination of particular characteristics , such as our food preferences , the way we act towards them and our lifestyles that will define what we eat , how we eat it , when we eat it and why we eat it . Featherstone -LRB- 1991 -RRB- , suggest we have reached a stage where the limitations of social class no longer tie us down and the socialisation process we go through , does not have the influence stated by Bourdieu . This essay will try to conclude how taste is constructed and the factors that influence its construction . Socialisation is the process through which we learn what is correct and how we should act according to different situations . He determines that culture establishes food preferences and that there is no room for individual taste . Warde -LRB- 1997 -RRB- proposes that social positioning can be recognized in eating procedures , and that our consumer behaviour can be defined by our class positioning and through social evaluation . The focus of this essay will be the identification of the business orientation adopted by Café Noir -LRB- a café / restaurant located in Headington , Oxford -RRB- . Various concepts will be identified in an attempt to recognize the focus implemented by the business ; different approaches will be evaluated , concluding with the assessment of the suitability of the concept preferred . Many business orientations can be found in literature , being the most important : product concept , production concept , sales concept and marketing concept . There are others such as the societal orientation , relationship marketing and ethical marketing which will also be covered . According to Adcock , D. , Halborg , A. , & Ross , C. -LRB- 2001 -RRB- , one of the oldest business concepts is the production concept , which lasted until the 1950 's , characterized by large quantities of production in order to reduce costs and improve efficiency . Lewis , R.C. & Chambers , R.E. -LRB- 2000 -RRB- , state that this concept is also typified by an easy-flow in production , considering that companies following a production concept see customers as hindering their profitability . Brassington , F. , & Pettitt , S. -LRB- 1997 -RRB- state that companies presume clients rely only on price as the differentiating factor between products and therefore choose the cheapest one . The problem is to maintain customers , because in an ever-changing environment , consumers are not content with the same product over and over again , and although in a long term view , this approach may seem profitable , soon its consequences will be clearly visible . Lewis & Chambers -LRB- 2000 -RRB- go further to recognize that these enterprises have instructions for every procedure and do everything by the book and they are so concentrated with efficiency and production that the customer becomes secondary . Although recognizing the use of manuals , they suggest that these sometimes come in the way of customer satisfaction and refer to the lack of flexibility . An example of this practice is a hotel in Switzerland -LRB- Kotler , P. , Bowen , J. , and Makens.J . 2003 -RRB- where customers are discouraged to eat on the balcony through highly poor service . These organizations think they 're highly efficient however , Lewis & Chambers -LRB- 2000 -RRB- consider it as `` customer blindness efficiency '' -LRB- Lewis & Chambers , 2000 p13 -RRB- , as such companies harm their customers , in order to gain an increase in revenue . Eventually , these companies lose market share which becomes practically impossible to regain as they 're always one step behind their competitors . This situation led to the product concept which lasted until the 1960 's -LRB- Adcockal . 2001 -RRB- and has quality as its primary concept . Mawson , S. -LRB- 2000 -RRB- states that organizations are not concerned about making a product that will meet customer requirements ; rather , they settle with trying to sell already existent products , which he defines as `` marketing myopia '' -LRB- Mawson , S. 2000 p5 -RRB- and holds responsible for the failure of companies too blinded to see their customers bored out and won over by competitors . Brassington & Pettitt -LRB- 1997 -RRB- refer to this situation as the company guessing that the product it sells is what the customer wants , when in reality this is not always true . The uncertainty about whether there will be demand or not -LRB- Adcockal . 2001 -RRB- often leads to fiasco when products are launched , when companies having promising markets and faced with disappointment slide into a production concept , leading them to even more complicated situations in an attempt to make a comeback when sales drop ; they finally tend to invest in large budgets concerning promotions and advertising heading to a sales concept . The sales concept lasted until the 1950 's / 60 's and is characterized by prioritizing sales -LRB- Lewis & Chambers , 2000 -RRB- . Kotler et al -LRB- 2003 -RRB- suggest this concept believes that consumers do not purchase adequately except when experiencing a large sales campaign . According to Adcock et al -LRB- 2001 -RRB- organizations that practice such an orientation already have a product and hence the sales staff task becomes almost to force the customer to buy . A marketing orientation should start by the company defining what it can sell . Consequently , it is essential for an organization to understand , identify and respond to the customers ' needs . An enterprise is market orientated when it is both customer orientated but also competitor orientated , only this way will it be able to satisfy customers and maintain a competitive advantage . Reid -LRB- 1989 -RRB- supports that in a marketing concept , selling becomes secondary as supply meets demand and there is symmetry , with both parties ' objectives being met . He suggests that if customers ' unsatisfied needs are met , the company 's goals will also be accomplished as satisfied customers increase revenue , and therefore continuous profits . He states that observations made by costumers greatly affect consumers ' view of the enterprise , making it essential for customers to leave the business delighted . According to Adcock et al -LRB- 2001 -RRB- , it is only when everyone involved in the company believe in the concept and in satisfying customers that the concept comes to life . Lately , other concepts have emerged such as the societal concept which Adcock et al -LRB- 2001 -RRB- believe results from the development of the marketing concept , and apart from responding to the customers ' demands , it takes into account society 's welfare . It is never possible to keep everyone happy , and this should be taken into consideration by companies adopting such a concept , as they may find themselves being criticized by minority groups forcing them to constantly work towards the improvement of their policies . Alternatively , relationship marketing focuses on building a strong relationship between the customer and the enterprise , leading the customer to go back ; the time spent between the two parts becomes therefore crucial to guarantee the customers return -LRB- Adcock et al , 2001 -RRB- . It leans towards costumer retention , and maintaining constant contact with the client , and comprises the dedication of all to better assist the customer . There are many techniques to relationship marketing , a few being loyalty cards , and vouchers which customers receive upon purchase . Ethical marketing may be considered as a higher level of societal marketing as it not only strives to satisfy the customer but takes into consideration moral and ethical values ; a code of conduct may be a way to ensure an ethical approach of staff towards both the customer and the company itself . It is mainly characterized by the practice of high moral and quality standards -LRB- Adcock et al , 2001 -RRB- . The results of the initial environmental analysis impact people management approaches , and therefore recommendations and justifications have been put forth to assist the evaluation of changes needed for a bakery group , Saint Fusion , when considering entering the country . Using the PESTE framework it was found that Korea is a significantly attractive country for business investment , especially in hospitality , as tourism is one of the country 's fastest growing industries . Its increasing levels of growth , rising consumer disposable income and expenditure , which adding to economic reforms and encouraging foreign direct investment policies have contributed to making Korea the current twelfth biggest world economy . The emphasis on education has created a pool of highly skilled professionals which in combination with vocationally trained staff provide the basis for Saint Fusion 's core staff . Further , employment law reforms introducing flexible work further encourage hospitality investment . However , cultural differences and their impact on people management approaches are significant . Characteristics including high power distance , uncertainty avoidance , feminism , long-term orientation and collectivism should result in a hierarchy-based organizational structure and the use of personnel management for operative staff , to meet collectivist and feminist needs , with a consequent progress towards soft human resource management as hierarchical levels progress . Further implications include : a focus on intrinsic rewards -LRB- motivation and reward -RRB- , opportunities to acquire and develop new skills -LRB- training and development -RRB- and the management of equal opportunities at operative level diversity management at managerial . In conclusion , the incorporation of the above factors in a polycentric/regiocentric approach would guarantee the transformation of differences into opportunities and provide competitive advantages for Saint Fusion . Korea 's recent government policy changes shown in increased westernization and business transparency suggest an inclination towards more individualist ideals , which certainly has major advantages , when competing with some neighbouring Asian countries , to attract FDI . Comparing the two countries ' political and economic landscapes , and contrasting the UK 's prevalent values of autonomy , self-interest and competition , with Korea 's control , formality and group values , it is clear that Saint Fusion Korea 's people management approaches should be contextualised and include group management and strong manager-staff relationships where status is emphasized yet moderated with goodwill -LRB- Hofstede & Hofstede , 2005 -RRB- . There are two underlying ideologies behind people management : universalism and contextualism -LRB- Harris et al. , 1997 ; Brewster , 2006 -RRB- . The first , a broad paradigm , focuses on the organization and regards staff as assets who , with strategic management , help achieve the company 's business goals -LRB- Harris et al , 1997 ; Brewster , 2006 -RRB- . It supports a `` best practice '' approach , applicable in various circumstances and characterised by human resource management and business strategy integration , staff loyalty , adaptability and excellence -LRB- Torrington , Hall & Taylor , 2005 -RRB- , whilst disregarding the organization 's external environment -LRB- Brewster , 2006 , Harris et al. 2004 -RRB- ; at an extreme , it 's opposing principles of adaptability and staff loyalty make its objectives unachievable -LRB- Torrington et al. 2005 , Storey , 1992 -RRB- . Disregard for external forces when moving into significantly different environments , has a negative impact on the organization 's success and reputation . Contextualism considers labour markets , culture , political environments and trade unions . Instead of implementing ` best practice ' , it seeks to understand differences , through natural data collection , and adapt to them -LRB- Brewster , 2006 -RRB- . Not only are people management practices influenced by outside forces , but also by how those forces are interpreted by the organizations ' members -LRB- Storey , 1992 -RRB- . Though enabling greater host-country adaptability -LRB- good for staff -RRB- , contextualism can challenge management ideologies -LRB- Brewster , 2006 -RRB- . The most common approaches to managing human resources are personnel management -LRB- PM -RRB- and human resource management -LRB- HRM -RRB- . There seems to be confusion in related literature as to the real differences between HRM and PM , though what prevails is HRM as a development of PM resulting from reduced trade union power and increased competition . PM , however , has collectivist/pluralist implications , centred on managing the workforce , regarded as inflexible and who need strict rules and centralised orders . From being considered an enemy of the managers , focused merely on producing numbers and gathering data , he has become an important member of the management team , shouldering a very holistic role . Not only is he heavily involved at departmental level but he has a crucial responsibility when it comes to strategic decision-making . Both the computerisation of hotel systems and technological developments have been major contributors to his enhanced role . This seminar paper aims to identify the role of the hospitality financial controller and his relation to and effect on the rest of the management team . Controllers play a very important role both in the daily operations and strategic directions of a hospitality organization . They are part of the executive committee and also managers . Controllers work closely with all departments to provide them with the proper financial image and assist departmental managers in developing their budgets . Most importantly , they are the right-hand confidants of the general managers . The hotel controller is one of the key players of the management team -LRB- Burgess , 1995 -RRB- as he assists managers with the provision of accurate information so that effective decisions may be taken . Further , research has shown that hotel controllers are able to exercise a significant amount of power due to their central role in the hotel 's information network and in the decision-making process -LRB- Moore and Stefanelli , 1989 -RRB- . Burgess -LRB- 1998 -RRB- found in her survey , that 71.4 % of financial managers had responsibility for systems management within the hotel operation . An earlier study by Burgess -LRB- 1997 -RRB- showed that over a third of respondents commented on how much impact technology had had on their working lives in the last five years . Although many of the changes are found in the front office area , the responsibility for control remains with the financial manager , who must ensure optimum levels of revenues and costs for the capital investment required . Developments in technology , mainly the computerization of management systems , and the economic recession of the early 1990 's had a major impact on the role of the controller over the past two decades -LRB- Burgess , 1993 -RRB- . This shift has transformed the controller 's tasks from a mere accountant , focused on gathering data to a full participant in the management team . In the last decade there have been many changes to the role and responsibilities of the controller for which new skills and training are required . He is now accountable not only for the hotel 's finances but also for departmental duties -LRB- Burgess , 1993 -RRB- . There is a trend for controllers to become more active in areas such as hotel development ; however , their responsibilities vary depending on the type of hotel ownership -LRB- Burgess , 1993 -RRB- . Their ability of radical cost-cutting was required , and their knowledge that ranged from business to industry was utilized to great effect -LRB- Burgess , 2000 -RRB- . The changed relation between financial controllers and management team demanded them to communicate within organizations in a much more effective way and deliver the best financial views of the effect of management decisions . There are four different perspectives in market orientation relating to how a company views its home-country markets versus its host-country .
