The pursuit of justice is a crucial theme in Greek tragedy . It was a very topical subject at the time these plays were written , in fifth century Athens , because democracy was being established and with it a new form of justice was coming in to place . But the justice that is sought is not always the right course of action . Often the hero of the play starts off seeking justice , but blindly goes to his doom because he pursued what he thought was justice too far . The three principle plays that I shall study in this essay to examine the pursuit of justice are Sophocles ' Antigone , Euripides ' Hecabe , and Aeschylus ' The Eumenides . I have chosen these plays because one deals with the pursuit of justice by many people , and their conflicting views , one with the pursuit of justice by just one person , and the third deals with the very first trial . In the Hecabe , the main character , Hecabe , is seeking justice through reciprocity . Reciprocity to the Greeks was a form of justice , because they believed that you had to avenge your family members if they were killed by killing whoever killed them . Hecabe wants to avenge the murder of her son Polydorus , who at the start of the Trojan War had been sent away with a large amount of money , to live with the King of Thebes , Polymestor . Polymestor , out of sheer greed , killed Polydorus and threw his body into the sea , unburied . Hecabe discovers this and is determined to have her revenge on the King . She lures him in to her tent and blinds him , while at the same time the chorus of Trojan women kill Polymestor 's two children . As Meridor says in his article , the events bear similarities to the Athenian justice system of the time , as ` Hecuba , far from craving personal revenge on Polymestor , expects Agamemnon to act on behalf on justice as well as on her own behalf ' -LRB- Meridor 1978 : 30 -RRB- . And so what starts off as simple reciprocity ends in a terrible act involving the murder of innocent children . Hecabe 's struggle for justice goes too far , as is common in Greek tragedies , and the result is her own downfall . Polymestor predicts her death saying that she will jump off the masthead of the Greek ship and : ` turn into a bitch with fiery eyes ' -LRB- Euripides , Hecabe , l. 1265 -RRB- . This sequence of events is similar to what happens to Creon in Antigone . He is determined to kill Antigone for disobeying his orders and burying the boy of her brother . In the process of doing this , his son Haemon kills himself because he was betrothed to Antigone , and on hearing that her son is dead , Creon 's wife also kills herself . These are both classic examples of how tragedy evolves from a case of justice taken too far , and gone wrong . The form of tragedy dictates that the hero has to determinedly stick to their idea of what justice is , until it is too late and they realise that what they thought was justice , was not . An example of this is Oedipus , in Sophocles ' Oedipus the King . It is ironic as he condemns whoever murdered Laius to death so vehemently without realising that it could be him ' I curse myself as well ... if by any chance he proves to be an intimate of our house ... may the curse I just called down on him strike me ! ' -LRB- Sophocles , Oedipus the King , l. 284-7 -RRB- . All these examples illustrate how in Greek tragedy the hero starts off on a pursuit of justice but through a conscious decision of that hero , the justice goes to far and brings about their own doom . Creon is not the only character in the Antigone who pursues justice . Antigone 's very actions that land in such trouble with Creon were done because she wanted justice . It is a different kind though to what Creon is after . Antigone buries her brother , even though it is against the law , because she is defending the rights of the family to bury their relations , and the proper treatment of the dead : ` No , he -LRB- Creon -RRB- has no right to keep me from my own ' -LRB- Sophocles , Antigone , l. 58-9 -RRB- . She fights for the justice of civilisation , rather than the personal glory and respect that Creon is so desperate for . He does what he does and condemns her to death because he is determined to stick to his idea of justice , disregarding all of Tiresias ' advice , and he does not want to appear a weak king , or man : ' I am not the man now : she is the man if this victory goes to her and she goes free . ' -LRB- Sophocles , Antigone , l. 541-20 -RRB- . Antigone does not care about how her actions make her look to other people , only that her brother gets the right and proper burial that the Greeks believed was so important : ` He is my brother ... No one will ever convict me for a traitor . ' -LRB- Sophocles , Antigone , l. 55-7 -RRB- . In some ways is can be said that Haemon is also seeking justice in the play . He disagrees with his father 's single mindedness and loves Antigone . He argues with Creon because he wants justice for Antigone : ' I see my father offending justice - wrong . ' -LRB- Sophocles , Antigone , l. 833 -RRB- . He accuses of Creon of disobeying the gods for not allowing the body of Polynices to be buried . When the body of Polynices is first discovered buried , the chorus suggest to Creon that maybe the gods did it : ` could this possibly be the work of the gods ? ' -LRB- Sophocles , Antigone , l. 316 -RRB- . This angers Creon greatly , as does Haemon 's later accusation . In the Hecabe , it is really only Hecabe who is pursuing any sort of justice . She is determined that justice be done to Polymestor in revenge for her son 's death . Also she wants to prevent the killing of Polyxena as she thinks it is unjust . She appeals to Odysseus to spare Polyxena 's life . She is honourable , and reminds him of when he came into Troy and , even though she knew who he was , she still spared his life . Se supplicates him and begs him : ` Then at least kill me together with my daughter ! ' -LRB- Euripides , Hecabe , l. 391 -RRB- . But despite her pleading Odysseus remains hardened , and in a moment of irony it is Polyxena who reminds her mother that she still has a son living , Polydorus . Hecabe replies to this : ` Yes , if he is alive ; but I have doubts , so completely has good fortune abandoned me . ' -LRB- Euripides , Hecabe , l. 428-9 -RRB- . Hecabe 's argument for justice is perfectly honourable and persuasive and yet is has no effect on Odysseus . I think this says a lot about the justice system that was being brought in in fifth century Athens . It is Euripides ' way of saying that the system of fair trial where the accused made a speech to acquit themselves is actually not going to work , as the jury , as Odysseus has , may have already made up their minds . When Hecabe is pleading with Agamemnon to acquit her of the crime of killing Polymestor 's children and blinding him , she uses another type of persuasion . She argues that someone should not be allowed to win his case just because he can speak persuasively , but that it should be his actions that condemn him : ` if a man 's actions are criminal , his arguments in turn should be unsound , and never should he be allowed to make a good case out of what is wrong . ' -LRB- Euripides , Hecabe , l. 1189-91 -RRB- . I think this again is Euripides challenging the law courts of Athens , and saying that their system is unjust . She also appeals to his morals ; he sacrificed his own daughter , Iphigenia , and so should know how hard it was for her to see the Greeks do the same to Polyxena , but none of this works . Hecabe argues for justice but is not given it . But she manages to sway Agamemnon with one thing : she uses his taking her daughter Cassandra as a concubine , something which she was always so against . She argues that as a result of this relationship , she is now family to Agamemnon . It is the lowest she can stoop but by now she is desperate , and it works . Agamemnon condemns Polymestor saying : ` Since you could being yourself to commit a wicked act , you must bring yourself to endure its unwelcome consequences . ' -LRB- Euripides , Hecabe , l. 1250-51 -RRB- . These events are cases of justice turned on its head entirely . The persuasive technique that should have won the case failed , as did the appeal to basic human morals . The argument that won however was low and immoral , and completely unlike the Athenian law courts . The Hecabe portrays a very twisted pursuit of justice , where people become so desperate that they will offer absolutely anything to save themselves . To the ancient Greeks , justice was all about revenge and reciprocity , but where should the line be drawn in these situations . The plays that best examine this topic are those of the Oresteia by Aeschylus . This trilogy of plays encapsulates the revenge of two families and three generations of those families . At the end of the last play , The Eumenides , the first ever trial is held and the whole issue of reciprocity as justice is examined . The accused is Orestes , who has killed his mother Clytemnestra , a terrible crime for the Greeks , but he did it in revenge because she murdered his father Agamemnon , and law dictated that you had a right , even a necessity , to avenge the death of your father . The judge is Athena , and the ` lawyers ' are Apollo , for the defence , and the Furies , for the prosecution . Apollo 's argument is that the father 's death had to be avenged , because he is the more important parent . The Greeks did not think the woman had any part in childbirth , except for carrying the baby . It was the male who carried the seed for producing children , and therefore it was no great crime if Orestes killed his mother , because it was of far more importance that his father be revenged . The Furies argue that Orestes should be condemned for his violence alone , and that you should always honour your parents no matter what happens . In the end Athena decides in favour of Orestes , and he is acquitted . She says : ` with all my heat I am my Father 's child . I can not set more store by the woman 's death - she killed her husband , guardian of her house . Even if the vote is equal , Orestes wins . ' -LRB- Aeschylus , The Eumenides , l. 753-56 -RRB- . Athena states that she sides with Orestes because she agrees with Apollo that the mother is the lesser parent , she herself was born of man alone and therefore sees Orestes as just in what he did . Ancient Greek tragedians all had a very different perspective on the pursuit of justice . At the time they were writing their plays , a new system of law was being introduced in Athens , and they are clearly affected by this . Euripides mocks the system for its reliance on rhetoric and persuasive speaking , and undermines it by showing how people in positions of authority can be swayed by bribes . Sophocles comments on how people 's fervent belief that they are following what is justice , will lead to their own downfall if they insist on ignoring the advice of others more wise than them . Aeschylus stages the first ever trial , reflecting the new trials that were being held in Athens , and shows for the first time how the court system works . He puts emphasis on rhetoric , as this is what eventually wins Apollo the case , being able to persuade Athena that the father is the crucial parent . This play shows how the Athenian democratic trial system was at least meant to work , but Aeschylus himself does not really comment on its fairness .
This quote from the Iliad said by Glaucus epitomises what it was to be a Homeric hero . They were the elite and their glory came from fighting . But each hero had a varying view on fighting , and what it meant to be a hero , and this is what I will look at in this essay . There were many reasons to go into battle , for the salvation of your people , the respect of your men , and for your own personal glory . These can be divided in to two sections , a culture of shame , and a culture of guilt . In a shame culture you fought for the glory others would heap upon you , and for the contempt they would feel for you if you were too cowardly to fight . A guilt culture is one in which you fought for your own morals and beliefs , in the knowledge that you would not be able to bear yourself if you did not stand up to the challenge of battle . Both these features of being a hero appear in Achilles , Diomedes and Hector and it is to what extent that I will compare in this essay . Each man has his own ` heroic code ' dependant on his moral opinions , and certain events in the Iliad change their view of this code as the Book progresses . Respect , or timê in Greek , was every thing to a hero . Diomedes ' actions are formulated to win him as much respect as possible from the Greeks . In Book Ten he volunteers to go on the night attack to the Trojan camp . This could be seen as highly dishonourable because it is not fair to your opponent to attack them while they are asleep and defenceless , but Diomedes takes up the charge solely for respect : ` Nestor , my own heart and my own proud spirit arouse me to go into the host of hateful men who lie near us , ' -LRB- Homer , Iliad , Bk . 10 : l. 220-1 -RRB- . He knows that he will win the admiration of his men if he goes and brings back some trophy to prove that he killed some of the Trojans . Hector however cares greatly about the respect of his soldiers and his countrymen . When he is about to face Achilles in the final battle between them he says : ' I feel shame before the Trojans and the Trojan women with trailing robes , that someone who is less of a man than I will say of me : `` Hector believed in his own strength and ruined his people . '' ' -LRB- Homer , Iliad , Bk . 22 : l. 105-8 -RRB- . He does not want men of lower status than him to think badly of him , he feels that as he is a superior fighter he should uphold the honour and safety of Troy . He goes out to fight Achilles knowing that he will be killed , but he does it because he would look like a coward in the eyes of those who respect him . Timê is the main reason why Achilles does not fight for most of the Iliad . The poem begins with Achilles anger at Agamemnon 's lack of respect for him and insulting behaviour towards him . The main theme of the poem is Achilles ' refusal to fight over the few days in which the Iliad is set , and the effects that that has on the Trojan War . He is tired of not receiving due respect for his actions from King Agamemnon , especially when Agamemnon does him the injustice of taking back Briseis : ` And now my prize you threaten in person to strip from me , for whom I laboured much , ' -LRB- Homer , Iliad , Bk . 1 : l. 161-2 -RRB- . But timê is also the reason why Achilles stays in Troy and rejoins the fighting . He is told by his mother Thetis that two fates await him in life . He can either return home to Greece and live a peaceful life and die of old age surrounded by his family . Or he can stay in Troy where he will certainly die but his fame will live on for many years . Immortalising your name so it lived on even when you were dead was one of the main aims for a Homeric hero , and Achilles can not resist the honour and glory it would win him . An ` aristeia ' is the Greek for when the narrative centres on one warrior and his deeds and killings for a while , and it is one of the things all three heroes have in common . Hector , Achilles , and Diomedes all have aristeia in the Iliad . An aristeia can always be recognised because it is preceded by an arming scene where we see the hero deliberately putting on his armour and weapons and preparing himself to go in to battle . Diomedes ' aristeia is in Book Five , when he is inspired by Athene with extra strength and courage . In this instance he is partly a substitute for Achilles as the dominant Greek warrior . He storms through the Trojan ranks killing every one in his path : ` raging as had been before to fight with the Trojans , now the strong rage tripled took hold of him , ' -LRB- Homer , Iliad , Bk . 5 : l. 135-6 -RRB- . There follows a long list of Trojans who Diomedes kills in his recklessness . One of the men he kills who is most closely documented is Pandaros , a skilled Trojan archer , who Diomedes kills with a spear guided by Athene 's hand . He is even told by Athene to fire an arrow at Aphrodite , which he does and wounds her . He is so involved in his rage that he wounds two gods , Aphrodite and Ares , and feels no remorse for his terrible actions . Hector 's aristeia comes in Book Sixteen when he rampages through the Greek troops and kills Patroclus who he assumes is Achilles , since he is wearing his armour : ` But Hector , when he saw high hearted Patroclus trying to get away ... and with the spear stabbed him in the depth of the belly and drove the bronze clean through . ' -LRB- Homer , Iliad , Bk . 16 : l. 818-21 -RRB- . Patroclus and the Myrmidons have driven the Trojan army back to the walls of Troy and Patroclus has just killed Sarpedon when Hector ambushes him and slaughters him . This is uncharacteristic of Hector who does not usually fight on an impulse but likes to plan his attack . Achilles ' aristeia is perhaps the largest in the Iliad . In Book Eighteen he learns of Patroclus death and blames himself for it because he allowed Patroclus to go in to battle wearing his -LRB- Achilles ' -RRB- armour . There follows in Book Nineteen Achilles ' arming scene and in Book Twenty his subsequent rampage through the Trojan battle lines , killing every one in his path until he reaches Hector and takes his revenge . His arming scene is the longest , as his own armour has been taken by Hector from Patroclus ' body , so he needs new armour . His goddess mother Thetis goes to Hephaestos who forges a new shining set of armour and a shield for Achilles . He then charges on to the battle field : ` But the Trojans were taken every man in the knees with trembling and terror , as they looked on the swift-footed son of Peleus shining in all his armour , a man like the murderous war god . ' -LRB- Homer , Iliad , Bk . 20 : l. 44-6 -RRB- . Here Achilles presents quite a formidable appearance , and is compared to Ares himself . This description accurately portrays Achilles anger and determination to kill as many Trojans as he can . Each hero had a different view of what ` the heroic code ' consisted on . Personally I think it was largely dependent on the man 's view on life and what morals he himself adhered to . Achilles is a fiercely principled man , and this is why he reacts to every little thing with such emotion . The classic example of this which I have already looked at is his refusal to fight because Agamemnon slighted him . Another example comes later in the Iliad after Achilles has rejoined the fighting to avenge Patroclus in Book Nineteen . Achilles says to Agamemnon : ` But before this , for me at least , neither drink nor food shall go down my very throat , since my companion has perished and lies inside my shelter ' -LRB- Homer , Iliad , Bk . 19 : l. 209-11 -RRB- . The event he refers to at the beginning is of course the vengeance he wants to take on the Trojans , and in particular Hector , for killing Patroclus . In this passage he is telling Agamemnon that the men have no time for eating but should carry on fighting until they have revenge , and then have a large feast . This demonstrates well how set in his ways Achilles is and how determined he is to stick by his principles . He was a young man when he came to Troy , only about nineteen or twenty , and his age shows in his heroic code , and also in how quickly he becomes disillusioned in it when he learns that few people in battle fight for such strong principles as he . Hector 's heroic code revolves around his country rather than himself . He fights for Troy and his men rather than for his own personal glory . He goes on to the battlefield to save Troy from being sacked by the Greeks . When the Trojans are attacking the wall around the Greek ships and Poulydamas advises Hector to retreat as he has seen an unfavourable omen , Hector replies ` One bird sign is best : to fight in defence of our country . ' -LRB- Homer , Iliad , Bk . 12 : l. 243 -RRB- . His heroism also depends on his family , he wants to defend his father Priam , and his wife and child . He would have known what the Greeks would do to Andromache if they sacked Troy , and he is trying to save her from death , and secure a future for his son Astyanax . Diomedes again has a different personal heroic code to Hector and Achilles . His code depends mainly on his own glory but also on the glory of the Greeks for whom he fights . When an embassy to Achilles is suggested he criticises Agamemnon for being cowardly and says : ` the rest of the flowing-haired Achaians will stay here until we have sacked the city of Troy ... still we two , Sthenelos and I , will fight till we witness the fall of Ilion ; ' -LRB- Homer , Iliad , Bk . 9 : l. 45-8 -RRB- . Diomedes will not run away from a fight like a coward , but will stay there until he has done what he came to do as a matter of honour . Once a hero had killed someone he needed a way of proving it . In ancient times the best way of doing this was to strip your victim of his armour and take it back to camp on your chariot as spoils . This happens throughout the Iliad although as Griffin comments it is noticeable that Homer never allows the Trojans to take Greek armour as spoil , except for the case of Hector and Patroclus where it is necessary to the plot . -LRB- Griffin 1992 : 24 -RRB- In an example of the importance of plunder Hector says to Andromache about their son Astyanax ` and let him kill his enemy and bring home the blooded spoils , and delight the heart of his mother . ' -LRB- Homer , Iliad , Bk . 6 : l. 480-1 -RRB- . Achilles is deeply offended when he sees that Agamemnon never gives him his proper share of the spoils and this leads him to dislike him , ` Never , when the Achaians sack some well-founded citadel of the Trojans , do I have a prize that is equal to your prize . ' -LRB- Homer , Iliad , Bk . 1 : l. 163-40 . Giving gifts was also a way of showing respect to another man , and this is why Achilles is so angry when Agamemnon takes back Briseis , who was a gift to him . Achilles shows himself to be better than the ` material-orientated heroic world ' in Book Nine when the envoy from Agamemnon come to try to persuade him to rejoin the fighting . He refuses the many gifts Agamemnon offers him saying ` Not if he gave me ten times as much , and twenty times over as he possesses now ... would Agamemnon have his way with my spirit . ' -LRB- Homer , Iliad , Bk . 9 : l. 379-386 -RRB- . Even when Achilles returns to the fighting in Book Nineteen and Agamemnon again offers him the gifts he promised , Achilles dismisses the offer saying that Agamemnon can give them to him if he thinks it is important , but that their real concern at that time should be fighting . Diomedes however embraces the idea of gifts and spoil as a sign of his prowess and to gain respect from others . In Book Six when he encounters Glaucus and they discover that their grandfathers were ` xenia ' or ` guest friends ' they agree not to fight each other and to swap armour . Diomedes is very cunning here as he swaps his bronze armour for Glaucus ' gold , therefore making a profit by it ' -LRB- Glaucus -RRB- who exchanged with Diomedes the son of Tydeus armour of gold for bronze , for nine oxen 's worth the worth of a hundred . ' -LRB- Homer , Iliad , Bk . 6 : l. 234-6 -RRB- . When Diomedes goes on the night attack with Odysseus , and Odysseus plans to steal the horses of the Thracian king Rhesus , Diomedes gets carried away : ` But he waited , divided in his mind as to what he would best do , whether to seize the chariot , wherein lay the bright armour ... or to strip the life from still more Thracians . ' -LRB- Homer , Iliad , Bk . 6 : l.503-6 -RRB- . The attitudes to fighting of Achilles , Hector and Diomedes differ greatly in terms of each man 's reason for fighting , but they do have certain features in common . All of them place considerable importance on respect and honour , but from different people in the case of all three heroes . All of them at one point in the Iliad have an aristeia which is their moment of glory and triumph in their career as warrior . They all have different versions of the ` heroic code ' . Diomedes ' is all about personal fame and glory , Hector cares foremost for his family and his country , and Achilles fights for his principles . Achilles loses faith in the heroic code and is determined to stick to his own views , but these are frequently not shared by older , more experienced warriors than him . Hector knows that he will die if he goes in to single combat with Achilles , but does so for the sake of his country , and the respect of the soldiers he commands . Diomedes is quick to fight for his country , and to rebuke any one who calls him a coward , and desires glory for himself and Greece . These men are all great heroes who fight and die in the wars of others , but die bravely , defending what they love and hold dear .
Site formation processes are the ways in which artefacts or other types of evidence of past cultures are preserved within the archaeological record . These have been divided into two types , cultural and natural processes that have been named C-transforms and N-transforms by the archaeologist Michael Schiffer . In this essay I will be looking at the different ways in which these two processes come about and how they affect the survival of the archaeological record . I will also discuss their role in the interpretation of archaeological remains and what they can tell us about a site . I will outline some of the factors that affect formation , pre and post deposition , and I will examine a few ways in which archaeologists can study and analyse formation processes . Cultural formation processes relate to the activities of human beings interacting with the artefacts or buildings or the land . These might include the stages of use of a flint , from it 's finding in the source area and shaping to when it is discarded or broken , and occasionally it 's re-formation for further use . It is important for archaeologists to understand these because from these we can gain knowledge of the kinds of tools that past societies were able to create and how they used them . An example of this is the hunting equipment found with the `` Iceman '' - these tools show us how advanced his culture was in making things from leather and other organic materials . But it must be recognised that artefacts do not always have to have been lost or discarded to enter the archaeological record . Many races in prehistory , such as the Hopi tribe of the American Southwest , produce examples of deliberate deposition . They left prayer sticks , clay figures and vessels of water in underground holes as votive offerings to the supernatural forces and the rain-cloud spirits -LRB- Hodder 2001 : 45 -RRB- . They also buried eagles and other birds in formal graves that would then carry their gifts to the spirits in the hope of bringing rain . It is important for archaeologists to understand this because misinterpretation leads to confusion of the archaeological record . Deliberate deposition also covers burial artefacts which were buried with the dead to be of use to them in the next life . From what it was felt that was necessary in the afterlife we can learn a lot about a culture . As well people buried things which they meant later to retrieve but did n't and so they enter the archaeological record . These artefacts are useful as they provide information about the customs of a society . Natural formation processes are events occurring naturally that affect the preservation of the archaeological record . This is usually gradual covering by sand or soil blown by the wind , but can also include burial on a much larger scale , such as mud slides or volcanic eruptions . It is crucial to understand the ways in which a site has built up naturally over time through changes in the landscape . Natural formation processes can also lead to the destruction of archaeology . Sediment deposition can harm and destroy artefacts or organic remains . Repeated wind erosion of land on exposed sites strips off the topsoil and then removes the natural deposits made by past cultures . Extreme natural events are excellent at making sure that a site survives , the best example of this being the destruction and subsequent survival of Pompeii and Herculaneum when Mount Vesuvius , the volcano in the south of Italy , erupted , both being preserved almost perfectly due to their coating in ash . Along with volcanic activity , mud slides and rock or snow avalanches can preserve a site incredibly well due to the fact that they cover everything in a protective `` blanket '' almost . It is important for an archaeologist to recognise the context in which the artefact in question was deposited . There are three types of depositional context ; primary , secondary and tertiary . Primary is rare because it means that the article was deposited where it was used , such as a spear being left where it was used to kill an animal while hunting . Secondary is more common for this is when the artefact has been left away from where it was used , such as in a rubbish dump . Tertiary contexts refer to re-deposition of secondary contexts , such as the use of rubbish to infill a ditch or building . The understanding of these is crucial to the recording of an artefact for it becomes meaningless without its context and correct interpretation of the circumstances . Site formation is affected by climate . Severe climate conditions such as water logging , for example bogs , or very arid places like caves are very good for preservation of archaeology . In the majority of cases temperate climates , such as in Europe , are not suitable for this but tend to increase the rate of decay . Tropical climates are the worst , the heavy rains and warm temperatures combined with high acidity levels in the soil destroy a site very quickly . The rate of growth in tropical rain forests is particularly detrimental as it is so fast . Deserts , due to their aridity , are beneficial to archaeology , in particular organic materials such as bone and flesh . Some of the best examples of this are the tombs of the Egyptian Kings , especially the tomb of Tutankhamun -LRB- Renfrew and Bahn 2000 : 62 -RRB- . This was discovered in 1922 by Howard Carter and Lord Carnarvon and has been preserved remarkably well owing to the dry air in the tombs and in the surrounding climate . Everything the King was buried with survived , including wreathes placed on his coffin and a large number of papyri . The bodies of the King and his two stillborn children were also preserved due to their mummification . Water logging of sites such as rivers or bogs are also favourable to preserving archaeological remains . Organic material survives particularly well in these conditions , a common find being wood which means that archaeologists often discover boats or wooden huts when exploring underwater sites . Many boats have been found in the recent excavation of the banks of the Severn Estuary . The pH of the soil affects what is preserved . According to Schiffer , and more recently Evans and O'Connor , an acidic soil with a pH of less than 5.5 will preserve pollen and charcoal . An alkaline soil with a pH of greater than 7.0 will preserve bone , charcoal and shell . Tests can be done on soil to determine its pH. These can be divided into two categories ; cultural and natural . Cultural alterations are caused by humans . A common way in which this happens is through building work . Many archaeological sites have been obliterated by towns that are built over them . For example major towns in Roman times that then evolved into modern cities , such as London where large amounts of Roman and Victorian archaeology has been and continues to be built upon . This situation was improved in the nineteen eighties when the Planning and Policy Guidance Notice was introduced to prevent the destruction of archaeological sites without proper records being made . Many sites have suffered from scavenging and looting , especially during medieval times when building materials were often robbed out to build new settlements . But construction is not the only method of cultural alteration ; farming is a destructive force to archaeology . Ploughing disturbs the topsoil and although this sometimes turns up artefacts for field walking , it can also cover organic remains and destroy them with heavy soil deposits . Land drainage systems damage what is under the soil by putting in pipelines and the loss of water breaks up some of the building materials or soil and pollen samples that may be present . Natural alterations are caused by the weather , animals or vegetation . Plant and tree roots damage archaeological evidence by tearing up the soil as they grow outwards . Gravity plays a part through slumping and collapse of soil mounds , which although the covering can preserve , it can also destroy in some cases . Animals that alter the depositional context are underground animals such as rabbits and moles which burrow and therefore disturb important remains . Bacteria and fungus destroy any wooden remains which is why so little is recovered except in special circumstances of preservation . Archaeologists must be able to identify the fact that a site or a piece of evidence has been altered and how . I think this because otherwise they could record a context that actually has no relevance to the site in question and this would severely alter our interpretation of the past . There are a number of ways in which site formation processes can be examined to gain a clearer understanding of archaeological data . One of these involves the sorting and examination of artefacts found on a site . This is a lengthy processes including flotation and sieving to gather evidence of what may have happened to that artefact ; how it was used and later deposited , along with its size and traces of burning or weathering . Another method is chemical and physical analysis . There are several ways of using this technique , such as particle size analysis , examining the pH of soil to determine alkalinity/acidity , X-ray diffraction , and study of the organic residues present like fats or lipids . Sedimentology is also useful for learning more about a site . The sediments can tell us what a site was used for , for example an area used for animal management will produce plant materials and dung . This also tells us what types of animals they kept and what crops and plants they grew . Areas used for sacred purposes are easily identified as there will be a residue of ash and the floor will have been kept clean . From this method we can form a reconstruction of the activities that took place in a building or area of land . An easier method is field observation , in which the deposit components , the stratigraphic sequence and the post-depositional alterations are studied . The frequency of deposits will vary across a site and drawing this out can help to build up a plan of which areas were living spaces and which areas were used for rubbish disposal . Stratigraphy shows how a site has built up and how the layers relate to each other . `` It is often used as a relative dating technique and to assess the temporal sequence of artefact deposition '' -LRB- Renfrew and Bahn 2000 : 572 -RRB- . These techniques all help the archaeologist to construct a picture of how a site was formed and how it has developed through time . It is essential for archaeologists to use some of these methods in their work because they provide firm evidence to support theories or assumptions about the formation of a site . As explored in this essay , site formation processes are the methods in which a site enters the archaeologist 's record . They can be caused by humans , or animals , or more natural occurrences . There are many different methods of studying formation processes available to archaeologists , and these contribute to site reconstruction . The formation of a site is also affected and changed by later activities and it is crucial to recognise these . For as we can see , the understanding of site formation processes is an essential part of every investigation into any archaeological site . They can tell us how a site was built up , in the case of N-transforms , and how the site or artefacts discovered fitted into the society present , in the case of C-transforms . Every archaeologist should research the processes which led to the formation of the site they are studying to expand their understanding of the background of that site .
Publius Vergilius Maro wrote The Aeneid from 29-19 BC at the time that Augustus was Emperor , for the purpose of glorifying the Roman state and in particular the Emperor himself . Augustus had just recently defeated Antony and Cleopatra in 29 BC at the Battle of Actium and had promised to restore the republic and the city of Rome to its former glory . The Aeneid tells the story of the origin of Rome through the deeds of a Trojan , Aeneas , who leaves Troy under divine command to sail to a place called Italy , where he is to establish a city and found a new people . Throughout the Aeneid Virgil is trying to make his readers draw a favourable comparison between Aeneas and Augustus . Aeneas is constantly described as a hero and displays all the heroic qualities considered to be essential for a Roman citizen in that age . His quest to found Italy takes him through many trials all of which were designed by Virgil to emphasise these necessary characteristics . He also shows himself as a good leader and here he reflects many of Augustus 's merits . The central theme of the Aeneid is duty . This was a matter of great importance in Ancient Rome . It is very Roman that Aeneas sticks to his duty and his divine path throughout the book in spite of all the obstacles in his path . The main diversion is of course his affair with Dido Queen of Carthage . Aeneas falls in love with her but then is reminded in a dream that he has a duty to fulfil and that he must continue on his journey to Italy . Dido is distraught and reproaches him for leaving her and begs him to stay but he readies his ships and sails away . She then builds a pyre and commits suicide . Virgil writes very little on Aeneas 's actual feelings about this event . Aeneas feels sympathy for Dido and for himself a kind of pain mixed with necessity . Love is portrayed as a morally dangerous compulsion which at the time was a reflection of the recent defeat of Antony who put love before the Roman state and forgot his duty . Virgil is showing that Aeneas is Roman because he puts his destiny before love and does not turn away from what he has to do . This is made more poignant by the fact that Dido is the founder of Carthage , which was the main enemy of Rome for many years . Other diversions come in the form of wanting to stay in lands in which he has become accustomed to and with people he knows . When he stays in Epirus with other Trojans who escaped from Troy and have already set up their new city he is most reluctant to leave because he thinks there it will be easier for him to settle there than to carry on his so far perilous journey to an unknown land . In Ancient Roman society piety , or ` pietas ' , was essential for a good citizen . Piety was felt towards the gods and your family . Aeneas displays the utmost piety to the gods all the way through the Aeneid . Firstly he leaves Troy carrying his household gods and it becomes his duty to take them safely to Italy . He is guided by messages from the gods and he has to rely on them completely . It was at their bidding that he left Troy even though , in true Roman fashion , he would have preferred to stay and die defending his country , as he tells Dido in Book Four . Aeneas is ultimately faithful to divine wishes even though he finds it hard to be and would often rather take another path . Another thing that Romans were supposed to show piety and loyalty to was their homeland , or ` patria ' . Aeneas is going to Italy because that is to be his new homeland and once there he has to defend it . Even when he has just arrived he is faced with a war for the land which he knows has been appointed to him and it is his destiny to settle on . He fights Turnus , a Rutulian , who resents foreigners coming into Italy and has particular grievance against Aeneas because he is destined to marry Lavinia , who Turnus was going to marry . Having won the war Aeneas does agree though to leave the kingship and customs of the king of the Latins , Latinus , alone in respect for their gods and culture . Aeneas also shows piety through his dedication to giving his companions proper burial rites . This was very important in the Roman world because it meant that the person 's soul could progress into the underworld . In the book he holds elaborate funeral ceremonies for his father and Pallas , the latter being the more outstanding because Aeneas actually collects living sacrifice victims from among the prisoners they have taken from the Rutulians . Another quality which was central to the Roman way of life was revenge . Augustus himself cited revenge as the reason for his raising army at the beginning of the triumvirate . He claimed that he had to avenge the death of his father , Julius Caesar , by going after Mark Antony and killing him and the other conspirators involved in the murder . The Romans , in particular Augustus , claimed that all their wars were carried out for revenge and there were numerous temples to Mars Ultor , or Mars the Avenger , in the City . Aeneas displays this all important quality in Book Twelve . When he first arrived in Italy he had sought support from King Evander in Pallantium , and the King had sent his own son , Pallas into the battle for Aeneas . In Book Ten Pallas was cruelly slaughtered by Turnus and it is for this act that Aeneas seeks revenge . Right at the end of the Aeneid , Aeneas sees that Turnus is still wearing the sword belt of Pallas on his shoulder and flies into a rage . So despite the fact that Turnus surrenders and begs Aeneas to spare his life , Aeneas kills him harshly and mercilessly saying : ` Its he , its Pallas who strikes this blow - the victim shedding his murderer 's blood in retribution ! ' -LRB- Virgil , The Aeneid , Bk.XII : l.948-9 -RRB- . In spite of this Aeneas principally hates war . This was one of Augustus ' main claims during his rule : that he had brought peace to Rome . Aeneas does not want war when he lands in Italy . This is demonstrated in Book Ten as he is about to kill Lausus and he says to him : ` Why rush upon death like this ? You 're too rash , fighting out of your class ; ' -LRB- Virgil , The Aeneid . Bk.X : l.11-12 -RRB- . The relationship between father and son was of great importance in the Roman world . The head of the family , or the paterfamilias , had a duty to his son but the son also had to be loyal to his father . Aeneas displays both of these duties . As a son he is dedicated to his father Anchaises in carrying him from the ruins of Troy since he was unable to walk himself . While he is alive Aeneas shares the rule of the Trojans with his father and is guided by him in all matters . It is Anchaises who urges Aeneas to leave Troy and it is his ghost who chides Aeneas in a dream for staying to long with Dido when his destiny awaits him . When Anchaises dies Aeneas buries his father and builds him a burial mound to commemorate his death . When , one year after his death , the Trojan ships reach the place where Anchaises was buried Aeneas holds the Trojan Games in his father 's honour and performs a sacrifice for Anchaises . In the middle of the book Aeneas is told he has to go down into the underworld and it is only really his love for his father and his desire to see him again that gives him the courage to make the terrible journey . Aeneas is frequently referred to as father , or ` pater ' in the Latin , usually in context of the care he takes of his men . He is constantly concerned for their welfare above his own . An example of this is when he confronts Turnus in Book Twelve and demands that they fight each other alone , for the quarrel is between them and should not involve the lives of their men . He is also called father as he is the father of the Roman race . In founding Italy Aeneas is responsible for his son 's future and all the sons that shall come from him and it is this that drives him once he has seen all the unborn heroes of Rome during his visit to the underworld . Aeneas ' shield bearing the deeds of Augustus at Actium reminds us that this is one of his descendants and that this is what Aeneas is going to Italy to start . We also see this in Book One when Jupiter recounts to Venus the future of the Roman race and how great they will become through the dynasty of Aeneas . Aeneas has great respect for the bond between father and son and Turnus plays on this when in Book Twelve he begs for his life to be spared , not for his own sake , but for the sake of his father Daunus : ` But if the thought of a father 's unhappiness can move you - a father such as you had in Anchaises - I ask you show compassion for aged Daunus , ' -LRB- Virgil , The Aeneid , Bk.XII : l.932-4 -RRB- . Aeneas is a father himself as he has a son Ascanius who he loves dearly . An example of this is when in Book One Aeneas is ship wrecked in Carthage and having discovered that his men and his son have also been saved , he sends one of his men down to the shore to fetch Ascanius at once . ` Aeneas whose love for his son would not allow a moment 's delay , now sent Achates running down to the ships , bade him tell Ascanius the news and return with him , ' -LRB- Virgil , The Aeneid , Bk I : l.643-5 -RRB- . When Pallas is killed by Turnus Aeneas feels so much distress because he feels that he has let Evander , Pallas ' father , down in not looking after his son as he promised . The Aeneid was written as indirect propaganda for Rome and Augustus ' rule . Virgil has made Aeneas a typical Roman hero who displays all the qualities that were important at that time , such as piety to the gods , commitment to your family and dedication to your duty . Virgil is discretely comparing Aeneas to Augustus as he is seen in the book when Aeneas visits the underworld and sees the future kings and leaders of Rome . He is also heralding the start of Augustus ' new regime in Rome , in which he promised to restore the republic and the quality of the Roman state as a whole . Aeneas was seen as the prototype of the ideal Roman as he embodies all the qualities that a citizen should have displayed at the time . The Aeneid glorifies everything about Augustus , his deeds and his rule . But The Aeneid is not just an exercise in propaganda . It expresses pathos for the human condition and tries to involve Augustus ' concern for the welfare of the Roman people . It raises the question of the worth of human struggle through the journey of Aeneas . Aeneas is a new wave of hero and he is everything a Roman ought to be in his heroism and life style .
