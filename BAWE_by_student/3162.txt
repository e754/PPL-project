The essay will then progress onto gaining a greater insight into the operations of hospitality both commercially and privately , taking into consideration the points of Telfer -LRB- 2000 -RRB- on host and guests motives . An evaluation will then be made taking different academics viewpoints into account of whether people are working in commercial hospitality just because it is a lucrative market or if the people within this industry are genuinely hospitable . There has been an ongoing debate between academics to what is the most useful way of understanding hospitality . Academics such as Lashley -LRB- 1999 -RRB- use the three domain approach to gain a greater insight into the structure of the hospitality industry . However Slattery -LRB- 2002 -RRB- suggests this approach which is taken by many academics demeans the hospitality industry . The three domain approach focuses on three spheres of hospitality activities ; these include the commercial , social and private domains . Lashley understands hospitality as : `` a set of behaviour which originates in the foundations of society '' -LRB- Lashley 1999 -RRB- . He suggests it originates from generosity and sharing food from hunting . He proposes there should be a meaning for hospitality which takes into account the three sectors and how each sector has an impact on the other one -LRB- Lashley 1999 . -RRB- In the social domain of activities Lashley -LRB- 1999 -RRB- suggests there are many cultural differences , for example in some societies there is need for people to be welcoming and hospitable to strangers as well as the disadvantaged members of society . Heal -LRB- 1990 cited in King 1995 -RRB- proposes that in her study of hospitality in Elizabethan times between 1400 and 1700 generosity was a key element of hospitality and food and drink were given both to friends and strangers of all social standings . However in our western society hospitality is more of a private activity and people entertain out of choice thus there is no social obligation to invite wider parts of the community . If we take Telfer 's -LRB- 2000 -RRB- views into account , host motives have also changed . For example people will not entertain guests in today 's western society to make themselves of a higher social standing or they will not entertain guests thinking as a result of this invitation they will become more socially mobile . When looking at Lashley -LRB- 1999 -RRB- he takes a some what sociological point of view when suggesting that the nuclear family in the private setting shapes activities in the commercial setting . He suggests that the private setting of hospitality makes us think of the roles that each of us perform when either giving or receiving hospitality . He suggests that in such establishments as bed and breakfasts and pubs which are usually run by families , the nuclear setting is the main reception for the commercial guests . Rybczynski -LRB- 1998 cited in Lynch and MacWhannell -RRB- supports this view from a historical point of view and also proposes that in the seventeenth century the term ` hotel ' usually referred to the large townhouses of the wealthy which were extremely luxurious and well appointed . Riley -LRB- 1984 cited in Lynch and MacWhannell -RRB- proposes that as these aristocrats had such high living standards , this became a model for commercial hospitality and a benchmark for the type of service and quality required in this sector . It can be suggested from a historical context that residences of aristocrats were one of the main ways of setting standards for luxury hotels . Lashley -LRB- 1999 -RRB- in his argument goes on to suggest that commercial hospitality is more formal and differs quite some much from the private domain , for example many restaurants or hotels will have a preferred way for guests to dress during certain times of the day . He goes onto say that in commercial hospitality guests can demand things they would not usually dream of when say going to a friend 's house for dinner . A good example in a restaurant could be a customer requesting the chef to hurry up making the food if they are in a hurry , however guests at a friends house would almost never do this ! Lashley -LRB- 1999 -RRB- is supported by Telfer -LRB- 2000 -RRB- who applies her work to hotels , she suggests that guests can feel free in hotels to use the facilities when they like or according to market demand as they have nothing to give in return but money . Telfer -LRB- 2000 -RRB- argues that this type of hospitality is not hospitable . Instead it is argued by her that there are no feelings involved between the host and guest and backs up Lashleys reasoning that this type of hospitality is all to do with market forces instead . Lashley -LRB- 1999 -RRB- goes on argue that there is a vast difference between being genuinely wanted and welcomed say at a friends house for dinner thus being a truly valued guest , and being a valued guest say at a hotel one stays at regularly for business . He goes onto suggest some commercial companies try to bring aspects of intimacy into the private sphere but sometimes do not succeed . He gives the example of Harvester restaurants where waiters are told to treat guests as though they were in their own home . This author suggests it is difficult for commercial companies to take aspects of the private sphere and put them in to practice . This is mainly due to the fact that one sphere is charged for and the other is not , thus some aspects of intimacy are totally lost in commercial hospitality for example deciding on how much to tip your waiter , or feeling that you have been overcharged for a meal . However an alternative viewpoint can be taken that when one is a guest of a commercial host , there is no obligation whatsoever for the guest to reverse the roles with their host . Aspects of intimacy such as ` treating guests as if they were in your own home ' can work quite well especially in such countries at the USA where the majority of private entertaining is done out of the home . Thus even though when one entertains in commercial setting aspects of intimacy can work well to a certain extent . This author will now look at branded hospitality which many suggest is usually a result of globalisation and standardisation . It can be difficult to meet all customers ' desires and provide intimate service as well as meeting specific needs of each customer . Lashley puts forward the argument that as commercial hospitality is based on money exchange this restricts the amount of pleasure a commercial host can give to their guests . For example if a guest has ordered a buffet meal in a restaurant the waiter will not be able to also give him al a carte dishes with the buffet .
